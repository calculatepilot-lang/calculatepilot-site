How Much House Can You Afford on a $75,000 Salary?

Lenders commonly cap housing at 28% of gross income and total debt at 36%. Here is what that means at $75,000 and other salaries.

→ Use the free House Affordability Calculator

Short answer: with a $75,000 salary, no other debts, 20% down, a 6.5% rate and typical taxes and insurance, the 28/36 rule suggests a home price of roughly $272,000. The rule limits your total housing cost to 28% of gross income ($1,750 a month here). Your result changes with your rate, down payment, taxes and debts.

Max housing payment (PITI) = min(Gross × 0.28, Gross × 0.36 − other monthly debts)

PITI is principal, interest, property tax and insurance, plus PMI when the down payment is under 20%. Lenders commonly use two ratios: a front-end ratio capping housing costs at 28% of gross monthly income, and a back-end ratio capping all debt payments at 36%. Use whichever limit is lower.

Assumptions: 6.5% on a 30-year fixed loan, 20% down (no PMI), property tax of 1.1% of the home price a year and $1,500 a year for homeowners insurance.

Carrying $800 a month of other debt can lower the price you qualify for, because the 36% back-end limit then becomes the tighter one. At $75,000 a year it cuts the estimate from about $272,000 to about $222,000.

Worked example: $75,000 salary, no other debts

  1. Gross monthly income = $75,000 ÷ 12 = $6,250.
  2. Front-end limit = $6,250 × 0.28 = $1,750 a month for housing.
  3. Subtract insurance of $125 a month, leaving about $1,625 for principal, interest and tax.
  4. Solving for the price that fits gives roughly $272,000, with a 20% down payment of about $54,000.

What the rule doesn't include

  • HOA fees and closing costs.
  • Maintenance and repairs, often budgeted separately.
  • Your comfort level. A payment that fits a ratio may still strain your budget.

Some lenders allow higher back-end ratios (up to 43% to 50%) for well-qualified borrowers, and cautious buyers may prefer to stay below these limits. Getting pre-approved by a lender is the most accurate way to know your real number.

Common mistakes

  • Using net pay with a gross-income rule.
  • Ignoring taxes and insurance. They are part of the housing payment in both ratios.
  • Forgetting other debts. Car and student loan payments count toward the 36% limit.

Frequently asked questions

How much house can I afford on a $75,000 salary?

Roughly $272,000 using the 28/36 rule with 20% down, a 6.5% rate and typical taxes and insurance, if you have no other debts. It falls to about $222,000 with $800 a month of other debt.

What is the 28/36 rule?

Housing costs should be no more than 28% of gross monthly income, and total debt payments no more than 36%.

Do lenders ever allow more than 36%?

Some lenders allow back-end ratios up to 43% to 50% for well-qualified borrowers, but higher ratios mean a heavier payment burden.

Does the estimate include closing costs?

No. It also leaves out HOA fees and maintenance.
