CD vs. High-Yield Savings Account: Which Earns More?

Both are safe places for savings. The right choice depends on whether the rate is locked, how soon you need the money and where rates are heading.

→ Use the free CD vs. High-Yield Savings Calculator

Short answer: a CD locks a rate, so it earns more when rates fall; a high-yield savings account (HYSA) lets you withdraw at any time, but its rate can change. The savings account only wins if its rate, averaged over the period, matches or beats the CD's rate. With the illustrative rates below, a locked 4.50% CD earns $450.00 on $10,000 in a year, while a savings account that starts at 4.25% and drops to 3.50% after six months earns $387.43.

Balance = Deposit × (1 + APY)years

APY already includes compounding, so you can apply it directly. If a rate changes mid-year, compound each period at its own APY and multiply the results.

Break-even rule

For one year, the savings account has to average the CD's APY to tie. If a 12-month CD pays 4.50%, an HYSA that averages 4.50% over the year earns the same interest. Over a longer CD term, compare the CD's APY with the average rate you expect the savings account to pay, remembering that you cannot know that average in advance.

Early withdrawal penalties are usually expressed as a number of months of interest, and they differ by bank and term, so read the CD's disclosure before you open it.

A middle option: CD ladder

Splitting money across CDs that mature at different dates gives you regular access to cash while still locking rates. The CD Calculator includes a laddering view.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing APY with a plain interest rate. Compare APY with APY.
  • Assuming the savings rate will stay put. HYSA rates move with the market and the bank's decisions.
  • Locking emergency money in a CD. A penalty can erase the extra interest.
  • Ignoring minimum deposits and promotional terms. Some top rates apply only up to a balance or for a limited time.

Frequently asked questions

Is a CD better than a high-yield savings account?

Neither is always better. A CD wins when rates fall or you want certainty, and a savings account wins when you need access or rates rise.

Which pays more, a CD or an HYSA?

It depends on the rates offered. Compare the CD's APY with the rate the savings account is paying now, and think about how that rate could change.

What happens if I withdraw from a CD early?

You usually pay a penalty, often a number of months of interest. The amount and rules differ by bank, so check the CD's disclosure.

Are CDs and high-yield savings accounts safe?

Both are insured by the FDIC (banks) or NCUA (credit unions) up to $250,000 per depositor, per institution, per ownership category.
