Short answer

For a + bi, the modulus is √(a² + b²) and the argument is atan2(b, a). So 3 + 4i has modulus 5 and argument 53.13°, giving polar form 5(cos 53.13° + i sin 53.13°) = 5∠53.13°.

1. The formulas

  • Modulus (magnitude, absolute value): r = √(a² + b²)
  • Argument (angle, phase): θ = atan2(b, a)
  • Polar form: z = r(cos θ + i sin θ) = r∠θ
  • Exponential form: z = r·e^(iθ)

3. Worked example: 3 + 4i

r = √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5. θ = atan2(4, 3) = 53.13°. Polar form: 5∠53.13°.

4. Why atan2

The ordinary arctangent of b ÷ a cannot tell opposite quadrants apart. 3 + 4i and −3 − 4i both give 4/3, but their arguments are 53.13° and −126.87°. atan2 uses the signs of both a and b.

5. Back to rectangular form

a = r cos θ and b = r sin θ. For 5∠53.13°, a = 5 × 0.6 = 3 and b = 5 × 0.8 = 4.

6. Frequently asked questions

What is the modulus of a complex number?expand_more

|a + bi| = √(a² + b²). For 3 + 4i it is 5.

What is the argument of a complex number?expand_more

The angle from the positive real axis, found with atan2(b, a). For 3 + 4i it is 53.13°.

Are magnitude and absolute value the same?expand_more

Yes. Magnitude, modulus and absolute value all mean √(a² + b²).

How do I get back to a + bi?expand_more

a = r cos θ and b = r sin θ.
