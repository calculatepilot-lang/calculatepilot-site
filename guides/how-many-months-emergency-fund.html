How Many Months of Expenses Should Your Emergency Fund Cover?

The common answer is three to six months, but the right number depends on your income and responsibilities.

→ Use the free Emergency Fund Calculator

Short answer: most guidance suggests 3 to 6 months of essential expenses. Dual-income households with stable jobs often aim for the lower end, single-income households closer to 6 months, and freelancers or commission earners 9 to 12 months. If your essentials are $3,500 a month, that means roughly $10,500 to $21,000.

Target reserve = Monthly essential costs × Months of coverage
Runway = Liquid cash ÷ Monthly essential costs

How many months should you aim for?

SituationCommon target
Dual income, salaried jobs3–6 months
Single income, salariedAbout 6 months
Freelance, commission or 1099 income9–12 months
Dependents or a home to maintainLean to the upper end

These are guidelines, not rules. Your own target depends on how stable your income is, how easily you could find work and what could go wrong, such as medical bills or home repairs.

Count essential expenses only

Base the number on what you must pay during an income gap: housing, utilities, groceries, insurance, transportation and minimum debt payments. Do not include dining out, subscriptions or other spending you could pause.

Worked example

  1. Rent $1,500 + utilities $250 + groceries $500 + insurance $300 + car and fuel $350 + minimum debt payments $300 = $3,200 a month.
  2. Target for 6 months = $3,200 × 6 = $19,200.
  3. If you have $8,000 saved, your runway is $8,000 ÷ $3,200 = 2.5 months.

Where to keep it

  • High-yield savings account: FDIC-insured up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, with access when you need it.
  • Money market funds or short-term Treasury bills: low volatility, though money market funds are not FDIC-insured.
  • Avoid stocks, long-term CDs with early withdrawal penalties, and crypto for this money.

Common mistakes

  • Using total spending instead of essentials, which makes the goal larger than it needs to be.
  • Waiting for the full target. Even one month of expenses helps, so build in steps.
  • Investing the emergency fund. The money is needed when markets and job losses can both be bad.

Frequently asked questions

How many months of expenses should an emergency fund cover?

Most guidance suggests 3 to 6 months of essential expenses. People with variable income or dependents often aim for 9 to 12 months.

How much should my emergency fund be if my expenses are $3,500 a month?

About $10,500 for 3 months and $21,000 for 6 months.

Should I use total spending or essential expenses?

Use essential expenses: housing, utilities, food, insurance, transportation and minimum debt payments.

Where should I keep an emergency fund?

Somewhere safe and accessible, such as an FDIC-insured high-yield savings account. Avoid investments that can fall when you need the money.

Sources

  • FDIC — Deposit insurance coverage, up to $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category
