Key takeaways

  • check_circleX% of Y: (X ÷ 100) × Y: 20% of 150 is 30.
  • check_circlePercentage change: ((new − old) ÷ old) × 100: 80 to 100 is a 25% increase.
  • check_circlePrice discounts: 15% off $200 is $30, so you pay $170.

Searches such as “what is 20% of 35.45?”, “26/35 percentage” and “percentage increase” all use the same basic ideas. The Percentage Calculator combines the common forms in one tool.

1. What is X% of Y?

Use (X ÷ 100) × Y. For example, 20% of 150 is (20 ÷ 100) × 150 = 30.

2. What percentage is X of Y?

Use (X ÷ Y) × 100. If a student scores 26 out of 35, the percentage is (26 ÷ 35) × 100 = 74.29%.

3. How to calculate percentage increase

Subtract the old value from the new value, divide by the old value and multiply by 100: ((new − old) ÷ old) × 100. A change from 80 to 100 is a 25% increase.

4. How to calculate percentage decrease

Use the same change formula. A fall from 100 to 80 is a 20% decrease because the change is −20% relative to the original 100.

5. How to calculate a percentage of a price

To find a 15% discount on $200, calculate 0.15 × 200 = $30. The price after the discount is $170. For a tax or markup, add the percentage to the original amount instead.

6. Percentage difference vs percentage change

Percentage change compares a new value with an original value. Percentage difference is often used when neither value is treated as the starting point and uses the absolute difference relative to the average of the two values.

What is 20% of 35.45?

Multiply 35.45 by 0.20 to get 7.09.

How do I turn a fraction into a percentage?

Divide the numerator by the denominator and multiply by 100.
