How Long Does It Take to Break Even on a Refinance?

Break-even is the number of months your savings take to cover the closing costs. Here is the formula, a table and when refinancing may not pay.

Short answer

Divide your closing costs by your monthly payment savings. Refinancing $300,000 from 7.0% to 6.0% with $6,000 of closing costs saves about $197 a month, so you break even in about 30 months (2.5 years). If you keep the loan longer than that, the refinance pays off; if you sell sooner, it costs you.

Formula
Break-even months = Closing costs ÷ (Old payment − New payment)

Both payments are principal and interest. This is the quick method; a fuller version also tracks how the balance changes, because restarting a 30-year term lowers the payment partly by paying down principal more slowly.

1. Break-even months by closing costs and rate drop

Example: a $300,000 balance currently at 7.0% (payment $1,995.91), refinanced into a new 30-year loan.

Closing costsRate drops 0.50 ptRate drops 0.75 ptRate drops 1.00 pt
$3,00030 months20 months15 months
$6,00060 months40 months30 months
$9,00090 months61 months46 months
$12,000120 months81 months61 months

A bigger rate drop and lower costs both shorten the break-even point. The table shows how fast a small drop with high costs can run past the time you plan to stay.

2. Worked example

  1. 1Old payment: $300,000 at 7.0% for 30 years = $1,995.91.
  2. 2New payment: $300,000 at 6.0% for 30 years = $1,798.65.
  3. 3Monthly savings = $197.26.
  4. 4Break-even = $6,000 ÷ $197.26 = 30.4 months.

3. When refinancing may not make sense

  • You will move before break-even. Closing costs will exceed your savings.
  • You are far into your current loan. Restarting a 30-year term puts you back in the interest-heavy early years, which can raise total interest even with a lower rate.
  • You are switching from fixed to adjustable. An adjustable rate can reset higher.

4. Common mistakes

  • Comparing only the monthly payment. A longer term lowers the payment but can increase total interest.
  • Ignoring costs rolled into the loan. They still count as money you pay back with interest.
  • Using the quick break-even as the only test. Check total interest and the new payoff date too.

5. Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate the refinance break-even point?expand_more

Divide the closing costs by the monthly payment savings. For $6,000 of costs and $200 of monthly savings, break-even is 30 months.

How long should I stay in the home after refinancing?expand_more

At least until the break-even point. If you expect to sell or refinance again sooner, the closing costs may outweigh the savings.

Does a longer new term change the break-even?expand_more

Yes. A longer term lowers the payment partly by paying principal more slowly, so the fuller net-benefit method can show a later break-even than the quick estimate.

Are closing costs always paid upfront?expand_more

No. They can be rolled into the loan, which raises the balance and the interest you pay.
