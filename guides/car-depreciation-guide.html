Why the first year matters most

The biggest depreciation hit happens the moment a new car leaves the dealership. Before you've driven a single mile, the car is now "used" and loses 10–15% of its value. By end of year one, total depreciation is typically 20–30%.

This is why buying a 1–2 year old used car with low mileage is often the best value. You avoid that initial 20–30% drop and get a nearly-new car for significantly less.

How much does a car depreciate per year on average?

On average, new cars depreciate 20–30% in year one and 15–20% per year after that. After 5 years, most cars retain 37–50% of their original value depending on make, model, and condition.

How do I calculate car depreciation?

The most common method is declining balance: multiply the car's current value by the annual depreciation rate each year. For a $30,000 car at 20%: Year 1 = $24,000 ($30,000 × 0.80), Year 2 = $19,200 ($24,000 × 0.80), and so on. Use our calculator to automate this.

Does high mileage increase depreciation?

Yes. The standard assumption is 12,000–15,000 miles per year. Each 10,000 miles above average typically reduces resale value by $500–$1,500 depending on the vehicle. Lower mileage than average adds value.
