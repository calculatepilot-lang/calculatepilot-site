4. Check it yourself: a worked example

You do not have to take either site's word for it. Run this on both and compare.

Formula
Fixed-rate mortgage payment: M = P × [ i(1 + i)n ] ÷ [ (1 + i)n − 1 ]
Ploan amount
imonthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12)
nnumber of monthly payments

Inputs: $300,000 loan, 6.5% annual rate, 30 years (360 payments), no taxes or insurance.

ResultValue
Monthly principal and interest$1,896.20
Total paid over 30 years$682,633.47
Total interest$382,633.47

If a calculator returns a different payment, check whether it is adding property tax, insurance, HOA fees or PMI by default. Those extras explain most "mismatches". The same idea applies to BMI: 70 kg at 175 cm gives 22.9 on any correct calculator, because it is weight ÷ height².

5. Where CalculatePilot is stronger

  • Published methodology. Our methodology page explains that each calculator is built from a named formula and checked against at least three known correct results before publishing.
  • UK coverage. Tools such as the UK Stamp Duty calculator reflect UK rules rather than US ones.
  • Embeds and extensions. Website owners can use our embeddable calculators, and there are browser extensions and a WordPress plugin.
  • Worked-example guides. See our guides for real-number walkthroughs, such as how the mortgage calculator works.

6. Where Calculator.net is stronger

  • Bigger catalogue. Around 200 calculators versus our 160+, including niche tools like Concrete and Subnet calculators.
  • Established brand. Its copyright line on the homepage runs from 2008, so it has a long history and a lot of user familiarity.
  • Familiar US-style finance set. Its homepage lists Income Tax, Sales Tax and Auto Loan calculators, which suit US users.

8. Frequently asked questions

Is CalculatePilot better than Calculator.net?expand_more

Neither is better for everyone. Calculator.net has a larger library (around 200 calculators according to its own homepage) and a long track record. CalculatePilot has 160+ calculators, a published methodology page, UK-specific tools such as a Stamp Duty calculator, and embed options for website owners. Choose by the calculator you need and the region you are in.

Is Calculator.net free to use?expand_more

Yes. Calculator.net states on its homepage that its tools are free with no registration required. CalculatePilot's calculators are also free to use; a paid Pro tier exists for white-label embeds.

Do both sites use the same formulas?expand_more

For standard calculations such as a fixed-rate mortgage payment, BMI or percentage change, both sites rely on the same textbook formulas, so results should match when you enter identical inputs. Differences show up in assumptions: rounding, tax rules, fees and which region's rules apply.

Which site has more calculators?expand_more

Calculator.net, with around 200 calculators as stated on its homepage, versus 160+ on CalculatePilot. Raw count is only one factor: what matters is whether the specific calculator you need exists and explains its assumptions.

Can I put a calculator on my own website?expand_more

CalculatePilot offers embeddable calculators and a WordPress plugin. See the CalculatePilot embed page for current options. Check Calculator.net's own terms of use before copying or embedding anything from it.
