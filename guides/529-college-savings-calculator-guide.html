529 College Savings Calculator: Growth, Contributions & Examples

A practical guide to estimating college savings growth, choosing assumptions and turning a target into a monthly contribution.

If you are asking “How much will my 529 grow?” or “How much should I save each month for college?”, a 529 college savings calculator can turn those questions into a clear projection. CalculatePilot's 529 College Savings Calculator lets you model a starting balance, monthly contribution, expected return and years until college.

What a 529 calculator actually estimates

The calculation combines money you already have with recurring contributions and compound growth. A projection is not a guarantee: the result depends heavily on the assumed return, contribution timing and investment period.

How to use the 529 calculator

  1. Enter your current 529 balance.
  2. Enter the amount you can contribute each month.
  3. Choose a reasonable long-term return assumption.
  4. Enter the number of years until the money is needed.
  5. Compare the projected balance with your education-funding target.

529 growth example

Suppose you start with $5,000, contribute $250 each month, assume a 6% annual return and have 15 years. The calculator shows how much comes from your contributions and how much comes from investment growth. Change the years to 10 or 20 and you can see how strongly time affects the projection.

How much should you save in a 529?

There is no universal monthly amount. Start with a target based on the type of education you want to fund, then work backward using your current balance, years remaining and a conservative return assumption. If the required contribution is too high, test a longer savings period, a different target or a gradual contribution increase.

529 calculator by age

Families often search for a 529 calculator by age because the same monthly contribution produces very different results at different starting ages. The useful comparison is not a single “right” balance but whether your current savings rate is moving toward your target.

State and plan differences

Tax treatment, contribution rules and investment options can vary by state and plan. A general growth calculator should therefore be treated as a planning model rather than tax or investment advice. Check the official rules for the plan you use before making a decision.

Common mistakes

  • Using an unrealistically high return.
  • Ignoring inflation and future education costs.
  • Assuming contributions will remain affordable forever.
  • Treating a projection as a guaranteed account value.
  • Looking at only one scenario instead of conservative, middle and optimistic cases.

Frequently asked questions

What does a 529 calculator calculate?

It estimates future value from a starting balance, recurring contributions, assumed return and investment period.

How much should I contribute each month?

Calculate the monthly amount required to reach your target, then test whether that amount remains realistic under different return assumptions.

What return should I use?

Use conservative assumptions and compare scenarios. Investment returns are uncertain.
