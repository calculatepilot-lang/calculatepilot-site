The student loan payment formula explained

The standard formula for any instalment loan is:

M = P × [r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n − 1]

Where: M = monthly payment, P = principal (loan amount), r = monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12), n = total number of monthly payments (years × 12).

Worked example: $30,000 loan at 6.5% for 10 years. Monthly rate r = 6.5% ÷ 12 = 0.5417%. n = 120 months. M = 30,000 × [0.005417 × (1.005417)^120] / [(1.005417)^120 − 1] = $340.04/month.

What is the monthly payment on a $30,000 student loan?

A $30,000 student loan at 6.5% over the standard 10-year repayment term costs $340/month, with $10,800 total interest paid over the loan life. At the current federal undergraduate rate of 5.5%, the payment would be $326/month with $9,100 total interest.

What is the monthly payment on a $50,000 student loan?

A $50,000 student loan at 6.5% over 10 years has a monthly payment of $567/month, with approximately $18,000 in total interest over the life of the loan.

How much does a school loan cost per month?

Monthly cost depends on loan balance, interest rate, and term. The most common scenario — $30,000 federal loan at 6.5% over 10 years — is $340/month. Graduates with $20,000–$40,000 in debt typically pay $227–$454/month on a standard 10-year plan.

Is it better to pay off student loans early?

Yes, in almost all cases. On a $30,000 loan at 6.5%, paying an extra $100/month saves approximately $2,800 in interest and shortens repayment by about 2.5 years. There are no prepayment penalties on federal student loans.
