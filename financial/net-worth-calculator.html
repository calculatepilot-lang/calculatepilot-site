Why net worth matters more than income

Income measures cash flow, but net worth measures actual financial position — someone with a high income but heavy debt and no savings can have a lower net worth than someone with a modest income and consistent saving habits.

Tracking net worth over time (rather than as a single snapshot) is generally more useful than any single number, since it reveals whether your overall financial position is trending in the right direction.

What counts as an asset vs. a liability

Assets are things you own with monetary value — cash, investments, retirement accounts, real estate, and valuable personal property. Liabilities are what you owe — mortgages, loans, and credit card balances.

It's worth noting that real estate and retirement accounts are less liquid than cash — your net worth can be technically high while still having limited accessible cash for near-term needs, which is why many people also track liquid net worth separately.

Frequently asked questions

Should I include my car's value as an asset?

You can include it under 'other assets' if you want a complete picture, though many people exclude depreciating personal property like vehicles from net worth tracking since it doesn't build wealth the way other asset classes do.

How often should I recalculate my net worth?

Many people track it quarterly or annually — frequent enough to see meaningful trends, but not so often that normal market fluctuations feel misleading.
