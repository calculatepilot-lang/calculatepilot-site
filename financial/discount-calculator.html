Why 20% + 20% Is Not 40% Off

Checkout systems apply a second discount to the already-reduced price, so stacked percentages multiply rather than add:

Final factor = (1 − D₁) × (1 − D₂) = 0.80 × 0.80 = 0.64 → a 36% effective discount, not 40%.

The bigger the discounts, the bigger the gap between the "added" and the real figure.

Volume vs Margin: How Many Extra Units to Break Even?

Sellers often underestimate how many extra units a markdown needs. For a product with a 40% gross margin, a 20% discount halves the gross profit per unit.

Extra volume = D ÷ (M − D) = 0.20 ÷ (0.40 − 0.20) = +100% — twice the units just to earn the same gross profit.

How stacked discounts work

Apply discounts one after another, not by adding the percentages. 30% off then 10% off is 37% off, not 40%.

Example: a $150 jacket at 35% off is $97.50. A further 10% coupon on $97.50 saves $9.75, for a final price of $87.75.

More examples: discount calculator guide. To add sales tax on its own, use the Sales Tax Calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I stack two discounts?expand_more

Apply them sequentially. 20% then 10% off is 28% off, not 30%.

How do I get the original price from a sale price?expand_more

Divide the sale price by (1 minus the discount as a decimal). $60 at 25% off was $80.
