The 529 Tax Advantage: Federal Exemption, State Deductions and Roth Rollovers

529 earnings grow free of federal income tax, and withdrawals are tax-free when used for qualified education expenses such as tuition, fees, books and — for students enrolled at least half-time — room and board. More than 30 states offer a state income tax deduction or credit for contributions, usually with an annual limit.

Under SECURE 2.0, leftover funds in a 529 that has been open at least 15 years can be rolled into the beneficiary's Roth IRA — up to a $35,000 lifetime limit, subject to the annual Roth contribution limit.

Source: IRC § 529 and IRS Publication 970 (Tax Benefits for Education).

College Cost Inflation: Why Tuition Outpaces Headline CPI

Over the long run, published college prices have risen faster than general consumer inflation — higher education is labor-intensive, and its costs don't fall with productivity gains the way many goods do. Increases have slowed in recent years, so treat the inflation rate as a planning assumption and revisit it.

Why it matters: at 5% a year, today's cost roughly doubles in about 14 years — plan for the price in the enrollment year, not today's sticker price.

Why starting early matters so much for college savings

Because compound growth needs time to build momentum, starting a college fund when a child is young — even with modest monthly contributions — can outperform larger contributions started later. This calculator makes that time advantage visible: try comparing a 15-year timeline against a 5-year timeline with the same monthly contribution to see the difference.

529 plans specifically offer tax-advantaged growth for education expenses in many jurisdictions, which can make the effective growth rate meaningfully better than a comparable taxable account — though specific tax treatment varies by location, so it's worth confirming the rules that apply to your plan.

Adjusting your plan as college costs become clearer

Education costs vary enormously depending on the type of institution (public in-state, public out-of-state, private) and are likely to change between now and when your child enrolls. It's worth revisiting this projection periodically and adjusting your monthly contribution as you get a clearer sense of the actual target amount you're aiming for.

This calculator assumes a constant contribution and return rate for simplicity — in practice, many families increase contributions over time as income grows, which would reach the goal faster than this straight-line projection suggests.

Qualified expenses and limits worth knowing for 2026

  • K-12 tuition: up to $20,000 a year per beneficiary can be withdrawn tax-free for K-12 tuition in 2026 (it was $10,000).
  • Credentialing: tuition, fees, books and exam costs for recognized credential programs now qualify.
  • Gifts: the 2026 annual gift tax exclusion is $19,000 per giver per child ($38,000 for a married couple that splits gifts). A 529 also allows five years of gifts at once: $95,000 per giver ($190,000 for a couple) with no other gifts to that child in the period.
  • Non-qualified withdrawals: the earnings portion is subject to income tax plus a 10% additional tax. Contributions come back without tax. Exceptions include the beneficiary's death or disability and certain scholarships.
  • Roth IRA rollover: the account must have been open 15 years, the funds rolled over must have been in the account at least 5 years, the lifetime cap is $35,000, and each year's rollover is limited by the Roth IRA contribution limit.

These figures come from tax-education summaries of the 2025 law changes (the One Big Beautiful Bill Act) and SECURE 2.0. Confirm them with IRS Publication 970 and your plan before acting.

Frequently asked questions

Are 529 plan earnings tax-free?expand_more

In the US, 529 plan growth is generally tax-free when used for qualified education expenses, though specific rules and any state tax benefits vary — check your specific plan and jurisdiction's rules for details.

What if I don't know exactly how much college will cost yet?expand_more

That's normal this far in advance — many families use a general savings target and adjust contributions over time as costs and the child's education path become clearer, rather than trying to pinpoint an exact number today.

How much will my 529 grow in 10 years?expand_more

A 529 plan with $10,000 starting balance and $250/month contributions at 6% annual return grows to approximately $59,000 after 10 years. Use this calculator to project your specific 529 growth.

What is the average 529 plan growth rate?expand_more

Most 529 plan age-based portfolios target 5-7% annual growth for long investment horizons, shifting to more conservative allocations (2-4%) as college approaches. Use your fund's historical return as your estimate.

How much should I have in a 529 by age 10?expand_more

A common benchmark is saving roughly one-third of expected college costs by the time the child is 10. For a $60,000 target, that's around $20,000 by age 10. This calculator helps you see if your current savings pace will hit your goal.

How much should I have saved in a 529 plan by age?expand_more

A common benchmark: save roughly 1/3 of expected 4-year college costs by age 10, 2/3 by age 15, and 100% by the time the child starts college. For a $80,000 4-year college goal: $27,000 by age 10, $53,000 by age 15. Use this calculator to find your required monthly savings.

What is the average 529 plan return rate?expand_more

Most age-based 529 portfolios target 5-8% annual returns for children 10+ years from college, gradually shifting to more conservative investments (2-4%) as college approaches. Historical average for a diversified stock fund is approximately 7% annually, though past performance does not guarantee future results.

How do I choose between a 529 plan calculator for different states?expand_more

You are not required to use your home state's 529 plan. Compare your state's plan (which may offer a state tax deduction) with other states' plans on fees and investment options. Use this calculator to compare projected growth across different contribution levels and return rates.

What is a 529 college savings plan?expand_more

A 529 plan is a tax-advantaged savings account specifically for education expenses. Contributions grow tax-free, and withdrawals for qualified education expenses (tuition, books, room and board) are also tax-free. Most US states offer their own 529 plan, but you can use any state's plan regardless of where you live or where your child attends college.

How much should I save per month in a 529 plan?expand_more

It depends on your target and timeline. If you expect college to cost $60,000 and have 10 years to save at a 6% return, saving the full amount takes about $366/month, while covering one-third ($20,000) takes about $122/month. Use this calculator to model your specific situation based on your child's age and your target college fund amount.

What is the average return on a 529 plan?expand_more

529 plans invested in age-based portfolios (which automatically shift from stocks to bonds as college approaches) have historically returned 5-7% annually when the child is young, declining to 2-4% as college nears. A conservative planning estimate is 5-6% average annual return over a long horizon.

Can I use a 529 for K-12 private school?expand_more

Yes. In 2026, up to $20,000 a year per beneficiary can be withdrawn tax-free for K-12 tuition (the limit was $10,000). Check whether your state treats these withdrawals the same way.

What happens to leftover 529 money?expand_more

You can change the beneficiary to another family member, keep it for graduate school, or roll up to $35,000 over a lifetime into the beneficiary's Roth IRA if the account has been open 15 years. Non-qualified withdrawals pay income tax and a 10% additional tax on the earnings.

How much can I put into a 529 at once?expand_more

A single giver can contribute up to $95,000 per child in 2026 by electing five years of the $19,000 gift tax exclusion at once ($190,000 for a married couple). Plan balance limits are set by each state.

How much do I need to save each month if I start when my child is 10?expand_more

To fully fund four years at an in-state public college with costs rising 4% a year and a 6% return, about $1,880 a month from a $0 balance. Starting at birth takes about $596. Most families set a lower coverage target.

How much will college cost when my newborn enrolls?expand_more

At 5% yearly cost growth, four years at an in-state public college that costs $114,000 today would be about $274,355. At 3% it would be about $194,077.
