Mathematical Mechanics: Marginal Slicing

Stamp Duty Land Tax in England and Northern Ireland works on a progressive marginal slice model, like income tax bands. It replaced the pre-2014 "slab" system where one rate applied to the whole price.

Formal Calculation Formula:Total SDLT = Σ (Slicei × [Ratei + Surcharges])

Where Slicei = max(0, min(Price, Upperi) − Loweri)

check_circle

Crossing a threshold only applies the higher percentage to the part of the price above that threshold, so there are no cliff-edge jumps — except first-time buyer relief, which is lost entirely above £500,000.

check_circle

Additional property (+5%) and non-UK resident (+2%) surcharges are added to the rate on every slice.

Devolved Nations: Cross-Border Tax Systems

Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) applies only in England & Northern Ireland. Scotland and Wales run their own land transaction taxes.

🏴󠁧󠁢󠁳󠁣󠁴󠁿Scotland: Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (LBTT)
Revenue Scotland

Administered by Revenue Scotland. The residential nil-rate threshold is £145,000 (£175,000 for first-time buyers, with no price cap). The Additional Dwelling Supplement (ADS) is 8% of the whole price.

🏴󠁧󠁢󠁷󠁬󠁳󠁿Wales: Land Transaction Tax (LTT)
Welsh Revenue Authority

Administered by the Welsh Revenue Authority. Main residences pay nothing up to £225,000, there is no separate first-time buyer relief, and higher residential rates for additional properties start at 5%.

Worked examples

  • Replacing a main home at £295,000 (GOV.UK example): 0% on £125,000 + 2% on £125,000 (£2,500) + 5% on £45,000 (£2,250) = £4,750.
  • First-time buyer at £500,000 (GOV.UK example): 0% on £300,000 + 5% on £200,000 = £10,000.
  • Same £300,000, three buyer types: first-time buyer £0 · replacing a home £5,000 · additional property £20,000 (£5,000 + £15,000 surcharge).
  • Non-residential freehold at £275,000 (GOV.UK example): 0% on £150,000 + 2% on £100,000 (£2,000) + 5% on £25,000 (£1,250) = £3,250.

Sources: GOV.UK residential rates, higher rates for additional properties, non-residential rates and corporate bodies. HMRC, not this calculator, is the authority.

What this figure does not include

  • Corporate reliefs (for example property rental or development businesses), which replace the 17% rate with the standard higher rates.
  • Shared ownership, linked transactions, multiple dwellings bought together, and lease rent (NPV) on new leases.
  • Refunds of the higher rates when you sell your previous main home within three years.

Frequently asked questions

When did these SDLT rates start?expand_more

1 April 2025, when the nil-rate band returned to £125,000 and first-time buyer relief to £300,000 (on homes up to £500,000). The 5% additional-property surcharge has applied since 31 October 2024.

What if I replace my main home?expand_more

If you are replacing your only main residence, choose Moving Home. Higher rates can apply temporarily if you still own the old home on completion day; you can then claim a refund if you sell it within three years.

Does this cover Scotland or Wales?expand_more

No. Scotland uses LBTT (nil rate £145,000, 8% ADS) and Wales uses LTT (nil rate £225,000, no first-time buyer relief). Use the dedicated calculators linked above.

How much stamp duty does a company pay?expand_more

Companies and other non-natural persons buying a dwelling for more than £500,000 usually pay a flat 17% of the whole price (19% if non-UK resident). Below that, standard rates plus the 5% surcharge apply. Qualifying businesses can claim relief from the 17% rate.

Does this cover a shop or office?expand_more

Yes — choose Non-Residential / Mixed-Use. Slices are 0% to £150,000, 2% to £250,000 and 5% above. GOV.UK's published example is £275,000 = £3,250. Residential surcharges are not added and lease rent is not calculated.

Are these figures an HMRC assessment?expand_more

No. Confirm with your conveyancer. This page is a planning estimate from published bands.
