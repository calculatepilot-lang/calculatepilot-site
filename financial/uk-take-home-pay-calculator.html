Why this is its own page

The user job here is “what lands in my UK account from this salary?” The answer is a function of PAYE bands × employee NI × optional student-loan thresholds. The paycheck calculator asks you to type a US-style effective tax rate. Changing that page to pounds still uses the wrong rules. The UK student loan calculator answers only the Student Finance line. This page combines the three deductions from the same 2026/27 dataset.

2026/27 rates this page uses

Tax year 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. England, Wales and Northern Ireland income tax. Employee Class 1 National Insurance is UK-wide.

Personal allowance
£12,570 a year / £1,048 a month / £242 a week.
Reduced by £1 for every £2 of income above £100,000. Zero from £125,140.

Income tax (England, Wales & NI) — on income above the allowance
Basic 20% on the next £37,700 (up to £50,270 of total income when the full allowance applies)
Higher 40% from there to £125,140
Additional 45% above £125,140

Employee Class 1 NI (category A)
0% up to the primary threshold £12,570
8% between £12,570 and the upper earnings limit £50,270
2% above £50,270

Optional student loan — same PAYE rule as the dedicated page
Plan 1 £2,241 / Plan 2 £2,448 / Plan 4 £2,816 / Plan 5 £2,083 a month at 9%
Postgraduate £1,750 a month at 6%
Deduction = rate × excess, then rounded down to the nearest pound each month

Sources: GOV.UK rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027 (updated 1 September 2026) and GOV.UK — how much you repay. Re-check those pages before you rely on a figure — HMRC, not this calculator, is the authority.

Worked examples (pre-computed)

These use the same bands and student-loan round-down as the calculator, so the answer is readable if JavaScript is off. No pension.

1. £35,000, no student loan
Personal allowance £12,570. Taxable £22,430 × 20% = £4,486 income tax
NI: 8% × £22,430 = £1,794.40
Take-home £28,719.60 a year / £2,393.30 a month

2. £35,000, Plan 5 student loan
Same tax and NI as example 1.
Monthly pay £2,916.67 − Plan 5 threshold £2,083 = £833.67 over the line
9% = £75.03 → PAYE takes £75 × 12 = £900
Take-home £27,819.60 a year / £2,318.30 a month
The student-loan line is why this is not a renamed paycheck page.

3. £60,000, no student loan
Taxable £47,430: £37,700 × 20% + £9,730 × 40% = £11,432 income tax
NI: 8% × £37,700 + 2% × £9,730 = £3,210.60
Take-home £45,357.40 a year / £3,779.78 a month

4. £12,570
At the personal allowance and NI primary threshold. Tax £0, NI £0. Take-home £12,570.

What this figure does not include

  • Scotland. Different income-tax rates and bands. Not a token-swap of this page.
  • Self-employed Class 4 NI, dividends, savings interest. This is an employed salary on PAYE.
  • Tax codes other than a full personal allowance. Unknown codes stay unknown.
  • Net-pay pension, marriage allowance, Blind Person’s Allowance, High Income Child Benefit Charge. Each changes the answer; none is invented here.
  • A US federal / state withholding estimate. Use the paycheck calculator for that job.
  • Student-loan interest, remaining balance or write-off date. The UK student loan calculator is the payslip-deduction sibling; it still does not invent a payoff date.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the same as the paycheck calculator?

No. That page uses a tax rate you type. This page uses published 2026/27 UK bands.

Does this cover Scotland?

No. Scottish income tax is a different rate table. This page is England, Wales and Northern Ireland.

Which tax code is assumed?

A full £12,570 personal allowance, tapered above £100,000. Other codes are not guessed.

How is the student-loan line calculated?

The same monthly PAYE round-down as the UK student loan calculator, then × 12 for the year.

Is this exactly what PAYE will take this month?

No. Annualised bands, not a cumulative payroll run. A real month can differ by a few pounds.
