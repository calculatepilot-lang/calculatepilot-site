2026/27 rates this calculator uses

Tax year 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. The personal allowance is £12,570 (£1,048 a month), reduced by £1 for every £2 of income above £100,000 and zero from £125,140. England, Wales and Northern Ireland tax income above the allowance at 20% on the next £37,700, 40% up to £125,140 and 45% above. Scotland uses six bands from 19% to 48% (table above). Employee Class 1 National Insurance is UK-wide: 8% between £12,570 and £50,270, and 2% above.

Student loans are deducted at 9% of monthly pay above £2,241 (Plan 1), £2,448 (Plan 2), £2,816 (Plan 4) or £2,083 (Plan 5), plus 6% above £1,750 for a postgraduate loan, rounded down to the nearest pound each month. Salary-sacrifice pension reduces taxable pay, NI and student loan; relief-at-source pension is paid from net pay with 20% basic-rate relief added by the provider (higher-rate relief is claimed separately through Self Assessment).

Sources: GOV.UK rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027, GOV.UK — how much you repay and GOV.UK — Blind Person's Allowance. HMRC, not this calculator, is the authority.

Worked examples (England, Wales & NI, no pension)

  • £35,000, no student loan: taxable £22,430 × 20% = £4,486 income tax; NI 8% × £22,430 = £1,794.40; take-home £28,719.60 a year / £2,393.30 a month.
  • £35,000, Plan 5: monthly pay £2,916.67 − £2,083 = £833.67 × 9% = £75.03, rounded down to £75 → £900 a year; take-home £27,819.60 / £2,318.30 a month.
  • £60,000, no student loan: £37,700 × 20% + £9,730 × 40% = £11,432 income tax; NI £3,016 + £194.60 = £3,210.60; take-home £45,357.40 / £3,779.78 a month.
  • £12,570: at the personal allowance and NI primary threshold — tax £0, NI £0, take-home £12,570.

What this figure does not include

  • Self-employed Class 4 NI, dividends and savings interest — this is an employed salary on PAYE.
  • Net-pay-arrangement pensions, the High Income Child Benefit Charge, benefits in kind and taxable expenses.
  • A cumulative payroll run: real PAYE can differ from these annualised figures by a few pounds in any one month.

Frequently asked questions

Does this cover Scotland?expand_more

Yes. Switch Tax Jurisdiction to Scotland to apply the six Scottish income tax bands (19% to 48%). National Insurance stays the same UK-wide. The Scotland take-home pay calculator also shows the difference against the rest of the UK.

Which tax code is assumed?expand_more

1257L by default — a full £12,570 personal allowance, tapered above £100,000. You can enter another code: number-and-letter codes (e.g. 1100L), K codes, and BR, D0, D1, 0T and NT are supported. Emergency (W1/M1) codes are treated as their normal equivalents.

How is the student-loan line calculated?expand_more

The same monthly PAYE rule as the UK student loan calculator: the plan rate × pay above the monthly threshold, rounded down to the nearest pound, then × 12 for the year.

How does Marriage Allowance work here?expand_more

If your spouse or civil partner transfers £1,260 of their personal allowance to you, your tax bill falls by up to £252 (20% of £1,260). You must be a basic-rate taxpayer (or starter, basic or intermediate rate in Scotland) to receive it.

Is this the same as the paycheck calculator?expand_more

No. The paycheck calculator uses a US-style tax rate you type. This page uses the published 2026/27 UK bands.

Is this exactly what PAYE will take this month?expand_more

No. It uses annualised bands, not a cumulative payroll run, so a real month can differ by a few pounds.
