NAR Settlement Rules: What Changed

From August 17, 2024, the National Association of Realtors settlement changed two things. Realtor MLS listings can no longer advertise an offer of compensation to buyer agents. Buyers must also sign a written agreement with their agent before touring homes.

Commissions were always negotiable. Now buyer-agent pay is negotiated deal by deal. The seller may still offer it, the buyer may pay it directly, or the buyer may ask for it as a concession.

Full-Service vs Discount vs Flat-Fee Listings

Sellers can choose a traditional full-service agent, a discount brokerage, or a flat-fee MLS listing where they handle more of the work. Lower fees raise your net. But pricing, marketing and negotiation can move the sale price by more than the fee difference. Compare the net, not just the rate.

How the seller net sheet is calculated

Net proceeds = sale price − mortgage payoff − commissions − transfer taxes − title & escrow fees − prorations − credits − other costs. Commissions are percentages of the sale price; the other costs are the dollar amounts you enter.

The agent snapshot works from the listing side's gross commission: the franchise royalty (if any) comes off the top, the brokerage split is applied to what's left, then any transaction fee.

How to estimate your home sale proceeds

  1. Enter the sale price. Use your expected contract price, or tap a preset.
  2. Set the commissions. Enter the listing agent fee and any buyer-agent compensation you agree to pay. Use 0% for the buyer side if the buyer pays their own agent.
  3. Add your payoff and closing costs. Enter the mortgage payoff, transfer taxes, title and escrow fees, prorations, repair credits and other costs.
  4. Read your net proceeds. The result and the itemized net sheet update as you type. Print the sheet or export it as a CSV.

Worked example: a $500,000 home sale

These are the calculator's default inputs. Change any of them above to see your own numbers.

  • Sale price: $500,000
  • Commissions: 2.5% listing + 2.5% buyer agent = 5% = $25,000
  • Mortgage payoff: $245,000
  • Transfer taxes $2,500 + title and escrow $2,800 + prorations $1,400 + repair credits $1,500 = $8,200
  • Estimated net proceeds: $500,000 − $245,000 − $25,000 − $8,200 = $221,800

In this example, commissions and closing costs take 6.64% of the price, and the seller keeps $221,800 before any tax on the gain.

What sellers usually pay at closing

Besides commissions and the mortgage payoff, sellers commonly pay transfer or excise taxes, which vary widely by state and county. Many areas also charge for the owner's title insurance policy, plus escrow or attorney fees. Sellers cover their share of property taxes and HOA dues up to the closing date. Any repair credits agreed after inspection come off the proceeds too. Ask your title company or agent for local figures.

Profit on the sale may also be taxable. Many homeowners can exclude up to $250,000 of gain ($500,000 for married couples filing jointly). The home must be their main home, owned and lived in for two of the last five years.

Key takeaways

  • Your home sale proceeds are the sale price minus the mortgage payoff, commissions, taxes, fees and credits.
  • Commissions are negotiable. Since the 2024 NAR settlement, buyer-agent pay is negotiated deal by deal.
  • This estimate is before capital gains tax. Your title or escrow company prepares the final settlement statement.

Frequently asked questions

What is a seller net sheet?expand_more

An estimate of the cash a seller receives at closing after the mortgage payoff, agent commissions, taxes, fees and credits are deducted from the sale price. Agents and title companies prepare one so sellers can compare offers by what they actually net. Compare each offer's net, not just its price, because a higher offer with more concessions can leave you with less cash.

Who pays the buyer's agent after the NAR settlement?expand_more

It's negotiated. The seller can still agree to pay some or all of the buyer agent's fee, the buyer can pay it directly, or it can be requested as a concession in the offer. It just can't be advertised on the MLS. Many sellers still offer some compensation to attract buyers, but it is a deal term, not a default. Ask your listing agent how it changes your net before you accept an offer.

Are real estate commissions negotiable?expand_more

Yes. There is no standard or legally set commission rate; both the listing fee and any buyer-agent compensation are negotiable. Rates vary by market, home price and brokerage, and discount or flat-fee brokerages charge less. Get written quotes from more than one agent and enter each one above to compare your net.

Does this include capital gains tax?expand_more

No. Net proceeds here are before any tax on the gain. Many sellers of a main home can exclude up to $250,000 of gain ($500,000 married filing jointly) if they meet the ownership and use tests. Gain above the exclusion, or on a home that doesn't meet the tests, can be taxable, so ask a tax professional how the sale affects you.
