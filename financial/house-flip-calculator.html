    functionsThe 70% Rule & Maximum Allowable Offer (MAO)

    Many flippers use the 70% rule as a quick screen: pay no more than 70% of the after-repair value minus the repair budget.

    Why 70%? The remaining 30% of ARV is meant to cover buying and selling costs, financing and holding costs, overruns, and still leave a profit. In expensive or slow markets the right percentage may differ.

    scheduleHolding Cost Erosion & Delay Sensitivity

    Time is one of the biggest risks in a flip. Interest and carrying costs run every month the property is unsold, and permit or contractor delays add up quickly.

    —

    What holding costs cover

    Holding costs are often underestimated by new flippers — they include property taxes, insurance, utilities, loan interest (if the purchase is financed), and HOA fees accrued during the time between purchase and sale. The longer a renovation takes, the more these costs add up, which is part of why timeline overruns can quietly erode profit even when the renovation budget itself stays on track.

    Building a realistic timeline buffer into your holding cost estimate — rather than assuming a best-case renovation schedule — tends to produce a more reliable profit projection.

    Why accurate ARV estimation matters most

    The after-repair value (ARV) is typically the single most sensitive number in this calculation — since it's an estimate of a future sale price rather than a known figure, an overly optimistic ARV can make a marginal deal look profitable on paper when it isn't. Basing ARV on recent comparable sales in the immediate area, ideally reviewed by a local real estate agent or appraiser, produces a more reliable projection than a rough guess.

    Selling costs (agent commissions, closing costs, and staging) commonly run 8-10% of the sale price and are easy to underestimate — always build this into your profit calculation rather than treating the full ARV as available profit.

    Frequently asked questions

    What's typically included in selling costs?expand_more

    Real estate agent commissions, closing costs, and often staging or minor prep costs — commonly totaling around 8-10% of the sale price, though this varies by market and negotiated agent rates.

    Why is ARV the most important number to get right?expand_more

    Because it's an estimate of a future sale price rather than a known figure, an overly optimistic ARV can make an unprofitable deal look attractive on paper — basing it on solid recent comparable sales data significantly improves the reliability of the whole projection.
