South Dakota v. Wayfair & Economic Nexus

The Supreme Court's 2018 Wayfair decision let states require out-of-state sellers to collect sales tax based on their sales into the state, not just physical presence. The most common threshold is $100,000 in annual sales into a state — some states also use a count of 200 transactions, though many have dropped it.

For sellers: track sales by destination state and register once you pass each state's threshold.

Origin-Based vs Destination-Based Sourcing

Most states use destination-based sourcing: the rate is set by where the buyer receives the goods, including local and special-district rates at that address. A handful of states use origin-based sourcing for some in-state sales, where the seller's location sets the rate.

Tip: rates can differ between neighboring ZIP codes — use the state revenue department's address lookup for the exact rate.

How sales tax is calculated

Tax = price × rate. Total = price + tax. To reverse a taxed total, divide by (1 + rate).

US rates are state and local, not federal. Groceries and prescriptions are often exempt. Outside the US, most countries use VAT instead — see the VAT Calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate sales tax?expand_more

Multiply the pre-tax price by the combined sales tax rate as a decimal. For example, $1,250 at 9.5% is $1,250 × 0.095 = $118.75 in tax, for a total of $1,368.75.

How do I remove sales tax from a total?expand_more

Divide the tax-inclusive total by (1 + rate). A $1,368.75 total at 9.5% tax is $1,368.75 ÷ 1.095 = $1,250 before tax, so the tax was $118.75.

Which states have no sales tax?expand_more

Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon have no statewide or local general sales tax. Alaska has no state sales tax, but many Alaskan localities charge their own.
