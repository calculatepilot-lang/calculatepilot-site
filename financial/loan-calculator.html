How this calculator works

This uses the same amortization formula banks use for personal, auto, and business loans — a fixed payment that covers both interest and principal over the loan term.

M = P × [ i(1+i)^n ] / [ (1+i)^n − 1 ]
M = monthly payment · P = loan amount · i = monthly interest rate · n = number of months

How loan term affects total cost

A longer term lowers your monthly payment but increases total interest paid, since you're borrowing the money for longer. A shorter term raises the monthly payment but reduces total interest significantly.

Choosing the right loan term

A shorter term means higher monthly payments but significantly less total interest, since the balance is paid down faster and has less time to accrue interest. A longer term eases monthly cash flow at the cost of paying more overall.

For example, a $20,000 loan at 7.5% costs about $401/month over 60 months with roughly $4,050 in total interest, versus about $626/month over 36 months with only about $2,530 in interest — the shorter term costs about $1,500 less overall despite the higher payment.

Another worked example

Say you borrow $12,000 at 9% APR over 48 months. The monthly payment works out to about $299, with total interest of roughly $2,344 over the life of the loan — meaning you'd repay about $14,344 in total for the $12,000 borrowed.

If you instead chose 24 months at the same rate, the payment rises to about $548/month, but total interest drops to around $1,148 — less than half — since the balance is outstanding for half as long.

Choosing the right loan term

A shorter term means a higher monthly payment but substantially less total interest, since less time passes for interest to accrue and more of each payment goes toward principal from the start. A longer term does the opposite — smaller payments, more breathing room in your monthly budget, but a higher lifetime cost.

The right choice usually comes down to your monthly cash flow versus your tolerance for paying more in total interest. If you can comfortably afford the higher payment of a shorter term without straining your budget, it typically saves meaningful money over the life of the loan.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between APR and interest rate?

The interest rate is the cost of borrowing the principal. APR (Annual Percentage Rate) usually includes additional fees, giving a fuller picture of the loan's true annual cost.

Does paying extra each month reduce total interest?

Yes — any extra payment goes directly toward the principal, which reduces the interest charged on future payments and can shorten the loan term.

What's a good interest rate for a personal loan?

Rates vary widely based on credit score, typically ranging from around 6% for excellent credit to 20%+ for lower credit scores — shopping multiple lenders is the best way to find your actual rate.

Can I pay off a loan early without penalty?

Many loans allow this, but some carry prepayment penalties — check your specific loan agreement before assuming you can pay it off ahead of schedule without cost.

Does a longer loan term always cost more?

In total interest, yes — but it also lowers the monthly payment, which can be the right trade-off if cash flow matters more than minimizing total cost.

What's considered a good interest rate for a personal loan?

It varies by credit score and lender, but rates for well-qualified borrowers are typically in the high single digits to low double digits, while lower credit scores can see rates well above 20%.

Can I pay off a loan early without penalty?

Many loans allow this, but some carry prepayment penalties — check your specific loan agreement before making extra payments if minimizing cost is the goal.

How does my credit score affect my loan terms?

Credit score is one of the biggest factors lenders use to set your interest rate — higher scores generally unlock significantly lower rates and better terms.
