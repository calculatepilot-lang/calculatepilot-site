The Scottish Devolved Tax Framework

Under the Scotland Act 2016, the Scottish Parliament sets the rates and bands of income tax on non-savings, non-dividend income for Scottish taxpayers. National Insurance, the personal allowance, and tax on savings and dividends remain UK-wide. Scotland uses six bands — from a 19% starter rate to a 48% top rate — against three in the rest of the UK.

Scottish income tax is collected by HMRC through PAYE, using an S tax code — not by Revenue Scotland.
trending_up

Salary Sacrifice & Pension Relief in Scotland

Scotland's 42% higher rate starts at £43,663, well below the £50,271 higher-rate point in England, Wales and NI — so from there to £50,270 a Scottish employee's marginal deduction is 42% tax + 8% NI = 50%.

Salary sacrifice saves both income tax and National Insurance on the amount sacrificed. Relief at source adds 20% basic-rate relief to the pension automatically; Scottish taxpayers paying 21% or more can claim the extra relief from HMRC.

Why this is its own page

The user job is “what do I take home if I pay Scottish income tax?” Removing Scotland from the question changes the tax. The UK take-home calculator applies the England, Wales and Northern Ireland 20% / 40% / 45% table and says so. National Insurance does not justify a second page — it is the same Class 1 schedule — but the six Scottish bands do. A city name pasted onto the rUK page would be a doorway. This page is not that.

2026/27 rates this page uses

Tax year 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. Bands below are the gross-income ranges published for someone with the full £12,570 personal allowance. The calculator applies those same slice widths to taxable income after the allowance, including when the allowance tapers.

Personal allowance
£12,570. Reduced by £1 for every £2 of income above £100,000. Zero from £125,140.

Worked examples (pre-computed)

No pension and no student loan, so the only difference versus England, Wales and Northern Ireland is income tax. National Insurance is identical. Figures use the same slices as the calculator.

1. £20,000 — Scotland pays less
Taxable £7,430. Starter £3,967 × 19% = £753.73. Basic £3,463 × 20% = £692.60. Scottish tax £1,446.33.
Rest of UK: £7,430 × 20% = £1,486.00. Scotland pays £39.67 less.
NI 8% × £7,430 = £594.40.
Scottish take-home £17,959.27 a year / £1,496.61 a month.

2. £35,000 — Scotland pays slightly more
Taxable £22,430. Starter £753.73 + basic £2,597.80 + intermediate £5,474 × 21% = £1,149.54. Scottish tax £4,501.07.
Rest of UK: £4,486.00. Scotland pays £15.07 more.
NI £1,794.40.
Scottish take-home £28,704.53 a year / £2,392.04 a month.

3. £60,000 — the higher-rate gap is large
Taxable £47,430. Starter, basic and intermediate £6,320.09 + higher £16,338 × 42% = £6,861.96. Scottish tax £13,182.05.
Rest of UK: £11,432.00. Scotland pays £1,750.05 more.
NI: 8% × £37,700 + 2% × £9,730 = £3,210.60.
Scottish take-home £43,607.35 a year / £3,633.95 a month.

What this figure does not include

  • England, Wales or Northern Ireland income tax. Use the UK take-home pay calculator.
  • Dividends and savings interest. Scottish taxpayers pay the same UK rates on those. They are not forced through the starter-to-top table.
  • Self-employed Class 4 National Insurance. This is an employed salary on PAYE.
  • Tax codes other than a full personal allowance. Marriage allowance and Blind Person’s Allowance are not invented.
  • High Income Child Benefit Charge and net-pay pensions. Each changes the answer. (Relief-at-source pensions and benefits in kind are modelled above.)
  • LBTT on a house purchase. That is a different tax. The Scotland LBTT calculator does not belong inside a payslip.

Frequently asked questions

Is Scottish income tax the same as the rest of the UK?expand_more

No. For 2026/27 Scotland uses six bands on non-savings, non-dividend income: starter 19%, basic 20%, intermediate 21%, higher 42%, advanced 45% and top 48%. England, Wales and Northern Ireland use 20%, 40% and 45%. National Insurance is the same across the UK.

Does National Insurance change in Scotland?expand_more

No. Employee Class 1 National Insurance is reserved to the UK. This page uses 0% up to £12,570, 8% to £50,270 and 2% above that — the same rates as the England, Wales and NI take-home calculator.

Which student loan plan should a Scottish employee pick?expand_more

Most Scottish undergraduates are on Plan 4. The deduction is still the UK PAYE rule: 9% of monthly pay above the published Plan 4 threshold, rounded down to the nearest pound. Confirm the plan in your Student Finance account.

Who pays less tax, Scotland or the rest of the UK?expand_more

On employment income with a full personal allowance and no pension, a Scottish taxpayer pays less income tax than England, Wales and NI at £20,000 (by £39.67) and more at £35,000 (by £15.07) and at £60,000 (by £1,750.05). The crossover is inside the intermediate band. Dividends and savings interest stay on UK rates and are not in this comparison.

Is this exactly what PAYE will take this month?expand_more

No. This is an annualised estimate on an S1257L-style full personal allowance. Real PAYE uses cumulative tables and per-period rounding, so a single month can differ by a few pounds.
