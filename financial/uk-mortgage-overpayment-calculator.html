The Mathematics of Mortgage Overpayment

UK lenders charge interest daily or monthly on the outstanding balance. Adding an overpayment (ΔO) cuts the balance that interest is charged on straight away, so every later payment clears more capital and the loan ends sooner.

Pn = Pn−1(1 + r) − (M + ΔO)

Where Pn is the balance after month n, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate / 12), M is the contractual monthly repayment and ΔO is your monthly overpayment. The contractual payment is M = P × [ r(1+r)n ] / [ (1+r)n − 1 ].

How this calculator works

The contractual monthly payment is standard amortisation on the outstanding balance over the remaining term. A lump sum is taken off the opening balance before the first month. Each month interest is balance × monthly rate, and the rest of the payment (contractual + overpayment) reduces the balance. We count the months until the balance reaches zero and compare the interest with and without your overpayments. Overpayments here shorten the term; some lenders can instead reduce your monthly payment.

A worked sterling example

Outstanding balance £200,000 · rate 4.5% · 20 years remaining. With no overpayment the scheduled payment is about £1,265.30 and interest totals about £103,672 over 240 months. Adding £200 a month (≈ £1,465.30 in total) clears the loan in 192 months — 4 years sooner — with about £80,735 of interest, saving roughly £22,936.

What this figure does not include

  • Early repayment charges. Many fixed deals cap penalty-free overpayments at around 10% of the balance a year. The guardrail above checks year-one overpayments against that typical cap — your offer document has the real limit.
  • Offset accounts. Savings held against the loan reduce interest in a different way.
  • Rate changes. The rate is held constant. For a remortgage, use the refinance breakeven calculator.
  • Stamp Duty or a new purchase. Use the UK Stamp Duty calculator or the UK repayment calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Do UK lenders allow overpayments?expand_more

Most repayment mortgages allow them, often up to a yearly percentage of the balance without an early repayment charge. Check your offer document for the cap and whether extra payments cut the term or the monthly payment.

Does this include early repayment charges?expand_more

No. Interest saved is amortisation only. The 10% guardrail is a typical penalty-free allowance, not your lender's ERC table.

Should I overpay or invest in an ISA?expand_more

Overpaying gives a guaranteed, tax-free return equal to your mortgage rate. A stocks and shares ISA may earn more over the long run but can fall in value. Many people keep an emergency fund first, then split spare cash based on their mortgage rate and appetite for risk.

Should I overpay or remortgage?expand_more

Overpaying keeps the current rate and shortens the term. Remortgaging changes the rate and usually adds fees. Use this page for extra payments and the refinance breakeven calculator if the question is a new rate.

Is this the same as the UK repayment vs interest-only calculator?expand_more

No. That page compares two products for a purchase. This page takes an existing repayment mortgage and measures the effect of overpaying.

Are these figures a lender illustration?expand_more

No. Confirm with your lender or a regulated adviser, especially around ERC limits.
