How this calculator works

This page uses the same fixed-rate amortization formula lenders use for principal and interest. It then adds the monthly share of property tax and homeowners insurance so you see a housing cost, not only the loan payment.

M = P × [ i(1+i)^n ] / [ (1+i)^n − 1 ]

M is monthly principal and interest. P is the loan (home price minus down payment). i is the annual rate divided by 12. n is the number of months. Extra principal shortens n. Estimated PMI of 0.50% of the loan per year appears only when the down payment is below 20%.

Enter your own tax and insurance. This page does not pull live mortgage rates or ZIP tax tables, because those figures go stale and hide the math.

What goes into a monthly mortgage payment

Most quotes describe PITI: principal, interest, taxes and insurance. Optional HOA dues and PMI sit on top when they apply.

  • Principal — the slice that reduces the balance.
  • Interest — the cost of the remaining balance. Early payments are mostly interest.
  • Property tax — usually collected monthly into escrow. Enter your annual bill.
  • Homeowners insurance — also often escrowed. Enter your annual premium.
  • HOA — optional monthly fee. Leave at 0 if you have none.
  • PMI — estimated only below 20% down. Override the assumption with a lender quote when you have one.

A 20% down payment typically avoids PMI. Use the Down Payment Calculator if you are still deciding how much cash to bring.

A worked example (readable with JavaScript off)

Home price 400,000 · down payment 80,000 · loan 320,000 · rate 6.5% · annual tax 3,600 · annual insurance 1,400 · no extra principal · no HOA.

30-year term (360 payments). Principal and interest ≈ 2,022 per month. Tax ≈ 300. Insurance ≈ 117. Estimated PITI ≈ 2,439 per month. Lifetime interest on these inputs is typically more than 400,000.

15-year term (180 payments). Principal and interest ≈ 2,787 per month. Tax and insurance stay ≈ 417. Estimated PITI ≈ 3,204. Lifetime interest typically falls under 182,000 at the same 6.5%.

The 15-year path raises monthly principal and interest by about 765 and removes on the order of 220,000 of interest across this example loan. Compare every period on the Amortization Calculator.

15-year vs 30-year on the same numbers

A 15-year loan front-loads principal. You pay more each month and far less interest over the life of the loan. A 30-year loan keeps the payment lower and leaves cash for savings, repairs or other debt.

The cards above the essay rerun both terms on the loan, rate, tax, insurance, HOA and PMI you entered. They ignore extra payments so the comparison stays clean.

What extra payments change

On the 320,000 example at 6.5% over 30 years, a scheduled payment of about 2,022 is mostly interest in the early years. An extra 200 applied to principal each month is the same formula with a smaller balance after every period.

On this example it typically cuts the calendar term by roughly 6–7 years and removes on the order of 90,000–110,000 of interest, before any prepayment penalty. Read the note if a penalty exists. Do not assume extra payments are free in every contract.

Who should not treat this number as a quote

  • Anyone who needs flood insurance, lender fees or closing costs inside the monthly figure — those stay outside this PITI estimate.
  • Adjustable-rate, interest-only or balloon products. This page is fixed-rate amortization only.
  • Buyers who will sell or refinance inside a few years and need a fee breakeven — use the Refinance Calculator.
  • Households still choosing a purchase price. Start with the House Affordability Calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How is a monthly mortgage payment calculated?

Fixed-rate amortization for principal and interest, then monthly tax, insurance, optional HOA and estimated PMI. Estimate only — not a lender quote.

Does this include PMI, HOA fees, or closing costs?

HOA is optional in the calculator. Estimated PMI appears below 20% down. Closing costs are not included.

How much house can I afford?

A common starting point is 28–36% of gross income. See the House Affordability Calculator.

Should I choose a 15-year or 30-year mortgage?

15-year usually costs less interest and more per month. 30-year keeps the payment lower.

Does a larger down payment lower my monthly payment?

Yes. It reduces the loan amount and often removes PMI.

Are these figures a formal loan offer?

No. Confirm rates and fees with a lender.

How much does PMI add?

Typically 0.3–1.5% of the loan per year below 20% down. This page estimates 0.50%.

Does paying biweekly save money?

26 half-payments equal 13 full payments and can shorten a 30-year loan if the servicer applies them immediately.
