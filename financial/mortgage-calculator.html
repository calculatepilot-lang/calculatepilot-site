How this calculator works

This calculator uses the standard fixed-rate amortization formula for principal and interest, then adds property tax and home insurance so you can see a realistic monthly housing cost — not just the loan payment.

M = P × [ i(1+i)^n ] / [ (1+i)^n − 1 ]
M = monthly principal & interest · P = loan amount · i = monthly interest rate · n = number of months

Loan amount is home price minus down payment. The monthly rate i is the annual rate divided by 12. Property tax and home insurance are divided by 12 and added on top. PMI and HOA fees are not included here — add those separately if they apply.

What goes into a monthly mortgage payment

Most lenders quote PITI: principal, interest, taxes, and insurance.

  • Principal — the portion that reduces the loan balance.
  • Interest — the cost of borrowing. Early payments are mostly interest.
  • Property tax — usually collected monthly into escrow.
  • Homeowners insurance — also often escrowed.

A 20% down payment typically avoids private mortgage insurance (PMI). Use the Down Payment Calculator if you are still deciding how much to put down.

PMI, HOA fees, and biweekly payments

These three items are the most common reasons a real payment differs from the PITI estimate above.

  • PMI — required on conventional loans below 20% down. Typically 0.3–1.5% of the loan balance per year, or roughly 80–375 a month on a 320,000 loan. It cancels automatically once the balance hits 78% of the original home value, or you can request cancellation at 80%.
  • HOA fees — set by the property, not the lender, and not part of PITI. They range from 0 to several hundred a month; add your community's figure on top of the readout above.
  • Biweekly payments — splitting the monthly payment into 26 half-payments a year equals 13 full monthly payments instead of 12. On this example loan that alone can cut roughly 4–6 years off a 30-year term and remove tens of thousands in interest, with no rate change. Confirm your servicer applies extra payments immediately — some hold them until a full payment accrues, which cancels the benefit.

Interest over the full term is typically under 182,000 at the same 6.5% — a shorter term cuts total interest even before any rate discount a 15-year loan might receive.

The 15-year path raises monthly principal-and-interest by about 765 and removes on the order of 220,000 of interest across the life of this example loan. That trade-off is why this page is not the same as the generic loan calculator. Compare every period on the amortization schedule.

Who should not treat this number as a quote

  • Anyone who needs PMI, HOA dues, flood insurance or closing costs in the monthly figure — those are not in this PITI estimate.
  • Adjustable-rate, interest-only, or balloon products. This page is fixed-rate amortization only.
  • Buyers who will sell or refinance inside a few years and need a breakeven against today’s loan — use the refinance breakeven calculator.
  • Households still deciding the purchase price. Start with the house affordability calculator.
  • UK buyers comparing a 25-year repayment mortgage with interest-only, in pounds. That is a different product — use the UK mortgage repayment calculator. Do not treat a currency switch on this PITI page as a UK quote.

What an extra 200 / month does on the 30-year example

On the 320,000 loan at 6.5% over 30 years, a scheduled payment of about 2,022 goes mostly to interest in the early years. An extra 200 applied to principal each month is the same formula with a smaller remaining balance after every period.

On this example it typically cuts the calendar term by roughly 6–7 years and removes on the order of 90,000–110,000 of interest, before any prepayment penalty. If a penalty exists, read the note; do not assume extra payments are free in every contract.

Frequently asked questions

How is a monthly mortgage payment calculated?

Principal and interest use the fixed-rate amortization formula above. Monthly tax and insurance are then added. The result is an estimate, not a lender quote.

Does this include PMI, HOA fees, or closing costs?

No. This page covers principal, interest, property tax, and homeowners insurance. PMI, HOA dues, utilities, and closing costs are separate.

How much house can I afford?

A common starting point is keeping total housing costs around 28–36% of gross income, then stress-testing a higher rate. Pair this tool with the House Affordability Calculator and the Loan Calculator for non-mortgage debt.

Should I choose a 15-year or 30-year mortgage?

A 15-year loan usually has a lower rate and far less interest, but a higher monthly payment. A 30-year loan keeps payments lower and leaves more cash for savings or other goals.

Does a larger down payment lower my monthly payment?

Yes. A larger down payment reduces the loan amount, so both principal-and-interest and often PMI go down.

Are these figures a formal loan offer?

No. Rates, taxes, insurance, and fees vary by location and lender. Use this as a planning estimate, then confirm with a quote.

How much does PMI add to my mortgage payment?

PMI on a conventional loan below 20% down typically runs 0.3–1.5% of the loan balance per year — about 80–375 a month on a 320,000 loan — and cancels once you reach 20–22% equity.

Does paying biweekly actually save money on a mortgage?

Yes. Splitting your monthly payment into 26 biweekly half-payments adds one extra full payment per year, which can shorten a 30-year loan by 4–6 years and cut total interest by tens of thousands of dollars — confirm your servicer applies payments immediately rather than holding them.
