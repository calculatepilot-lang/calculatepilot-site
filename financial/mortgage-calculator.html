Mathematical Formula & Methodology

Standard residential amortization uses an ordinary annuity present value model where the periodic payment is calibrated so the remaining debt equals exactly zero at term maturity:

M = P · [ r(1 + r)n ] / [ (1 + r)n − 1 ]
M: Monthly Payment
P: Principal Amount
r: Periodic Rate (Rate/12)
n: Total Months (Term × 12)

Lender disclosures are governed by the Truth in Lending Act (Regulation Z, 12 CFR Part 1026) and will include fees this estimate leaves out. Escrow amounts are held constant; real property tax and insurance bills usually rise over time.

How this calculator works

This calculator uses the standard fixed-rate amortization formula for principal and interest, then adds property tax, home insurance, HOA dues and PMI so you see the full housing cost — not only the loan payment. The loan amount is the home price minus your down payment. PMI is estimated at your chosen rate (0.50% of the loan per year by default) only when you put down less than 20%, and it drops off automatically once the balance reaches 78% of the original home value. Extra principal payments are applied every month and shorten the loan.

What goes into a monthly mortgage payment

Most lenders quote PITI: principal, interest, taxes, and insurance.

  • Principal — the portion of each payment that reduces the outstanding balance.
  • Interest — the cost of borrowing. Early payments on a 30-year loan are mostly interest.
  • Property tax — county assessments, usually collected monthly into escrow.
  • Homeowners insurance — hazard coverage, often escrowed with taxes.
  • PMI — required on conventional loans below 20% down, typically 0.3–1.5% of the loan per year.
  • HOA dues — set by the association, not the lender.

Biweekly payment power: 26 half-payments a year equal 13 full payments and can shorten a 30-year loan by about 4–6 years. You can model the same effect by entering one-twelfth of a payment as extra principal.

Frequently asked questions

How is a monthly mortgage payment calculated?expand_more

Fixed-rate amortization for principal and interest, then monthly tax and insurance. Estimate only — not a lender quote.

Does this include PMI, HOA fees, or closing costs?expand_more

HOA is optional. Estimated PMI appears below 20% down. Closing costs are not included.

How much house can I afford?expand_more

A common starting point is 28–36% of gross income. Use the House Affordability Calculator.

Should I choose a 15-year or 30-year mortgage?expand_more

15-year usually costs less interest and more per month. 30-year keeps the payment lower. The 15-Year Fixed card above shows how much interest you'd save at your inputs.

Does a larger down payment lower my monthly payment?expand_more

Yes. It reduces the loan amount and often removes PMI at 20% down.

Are these figures a formal loan offer?expand_more

No. Confirm rates and fees with a lender quote.

Should I pay extra on my mortgage or invest that money instead?expand_more

On a $400,000 loan at 7%, $300/month extra saves $168,391 in interest and pays off 7.8 years early — a guaranteed 7% return. Investing the same $300/month at 8% would grow to ~$450,389 over the same period. See the full extra-payments-vs-investing breakdown for the math.

When exactly does PMI come off my loan?expand_more

By federal law, PMI automatically ends at 78% of your home's original value; you can request removal yourself at 80%. This calculator drops PMI at the 78% point, and extra payments bring that date forward.
