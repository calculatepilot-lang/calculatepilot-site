How this calculator works

This calculator uses the standard fixed-rate amortization formula for principal and interest, then adds property tax and home insurance so you can see a realistic monthly housing cost — not just the loan payment.

M = P × [ i(1+i)^n ] / [ (1+i)^n − 1 ]
M = monthly principal & interest · P = loan amount · i = monthly interest rate · n = number of months

Loan amount is home price minus down payment. The monthly rate i is the annual rate divided by 12. Property tax and home insurance are divided by 12 and added on top. Extra principal shortens the term. Estimated PMI (0.50% of the loan per year) is shown when the down payment is under 20%.

What goes into a monthly mortgage payment

Most lenders quote PITI: principal, interest, taxes, and insurance.

  • Principal — the portion that reduces the loan balance.
  • Interest — the cost of borrowing. Early payments are mostly interest.
  • Property tax — usually collected monthly into escrow.
  • Homeowners insurance — also often escrowed.

A 20% down payment typically avoids private mortgage insurance (PMI). Use the Down Payment Calculator if you are still deciding how much to put down.

PMI, HOA fees, and biweekly payments

These three items are the most common reasons a real payment differs from the PITI estimate above.

  • PMI — required on conventional loans below 20% down. Typically 0.3–1.5% of the loan balance per year, or roughly 80–375 a month on a 320,000 loan. It cancels automatically once the balance hits 78% of the original home value.
  • HOA fees — set by the property, not the lender. Add your community's figure in the calculator above.
  • Biweekly payments — 26 half-payments a year equals 13 full payments and can cut 4–6 years off a 30-year term. Confirm your servicer applies extra payments immediately.

The 15-year path raises monthly principal-and-interest by about 765 and removes on the order of 220,000 of interest on this example loan. Compare every period on the amortization schedule.

Who should not treat this number as a quote

  • Anyone who needs flood insurance or closing costs in the monthly figure.
  • Adjustable-rate, interest-only, or balloon products. This page is fixed-rate amortization only.
  • Buyers who will sell or refinance soon — use the refinance breakeven calculator.
  • Households still deciding the purchase price — start with the house affordability calculator.
  • UK buyers comparing a 25-year repayment mortgage with interest-only — use the UK mortgage repayment calculator. Do not treat a currency switch on this PITI page as a UK quote.

A UK capital-and-interest mortgage is usually quoted on a 25-year term, has no PMI line, and treats Stamp Duty as a purchase cost rather than a monthly escrow.

Worked UK example on that page: a £300,000 property, £30,000 deposit, 4.5% rate, 25 years — repayment ≈ £1,500.75 / month versus interest-only ≈ £1,012.50 / month with the £270,000 still due at the end.

What an extra 200 / month does on the 30-year example

On the 320,000 loan at 6.5% over 30 years, a scheduled payment of about 2,022 goes mostly to interest early on. An extra 200 applied to principal each month typically cuts the term by roughly 6–7 years and removes on the order of 90,000–110,000 of interest.

Frequently asked questions

How is a monthly mortgage payment calculated?

Principal and interest use the fixed-rate amortization formula above. Monthly tax and insurance are then added, plus optional HOA and estimated PMI when applicable. The result is an estimate, not a lender quote.

Does this include PMI, HOA fees, or closing costs?

HOA is optional in the calculator. Estimated PMI appears automatically below 20% down. Closing costs, points, and utilities are not included.

How much house can I afford?

A common starting point is keeping total housing costs around 28–36% of gross income. Pair this tool with the House Affordability Calculator and the Loan Calculator.

Should I choose a 15-year or 30-year mortgage?

A 15-year loan usually has a lower rate and far less interest, but a higher monthly payment. A 30-year loan keeps payments lower and leaves more cash for other goals.

Does a larger down payment lower my monthly payment?

Yes. A larger down payment reduces the loan amount, so both principal-and-interest and often PMI go down.

Are these figures a formal loan offer?

No. Rates, taxes, insurance, and fees vary by location and lender. Use this as a planning estimate, then confirm with a quote.

How much does PMI add to my mortgage payment?

PMI on a conventional loan below 20% down typically runs 0.3–1.5% of the loan per year — about 80–375 a month on a 320,000 loan. This page estimates 0.50% / year.

Does paying biweekly actually save money on a mortgage?

Yes. Twenty-six half-payments a year equals 13 full payments and can shorten a 30-year loan by 4–6 years — confirm your servicer applies payments immediately.
