Think of It Like a Graduate Tax

UK income-contingent student loans don't work like a mortgage or credit card. What you repay depends only on what you earn above your plan's threshold — never on the balance. If your pay drops below the threshold, repayments stop. Any balance left at the end of the term is written off.

Key point: for many graduates the balance mostly determines whether anything is written off, not what they pay each month.
compare_arrows

Plan 2 vs Plan 5

For students starting in England from August 2023 (Plan 5), the write-off period rose from 30 to 40 years and the threshold fell to £25,000, while interest dropped to RPI only — no extra 3%. The result is that many more Plan 5 borrowers are expected to repay in full than on Plan 2.

Write-off terms: Plan 1 — 25 years (most loans); Plan 2 — 30 years; Plan 4 — 30 years; Plan 5 — 40 years; postgraduate — 30 years.

Why this is its own page

The user job here is “how much will Student Finance take from this UK salary?” The answer is a function of plan × threshold × rate, not of a loan balance and a 10-year term. The US student loan calculator uses standard amortisation. Changing that page to pounds would still be the wrong formula. GOV.UK publishes the bands; this page does not invent them.

2026/27 thresholds (from 6 April 2026)

You repay a percentage of income above the threshold for your plan. Below the threshold the deduction is £0.

Undergraduate plans — 9% of income over the threshold
Plan 1: £26,900 a year / £2,241 a month / £517 a week
Plan 2: £29,385 a year / £2,448 a month / £565 a week
Plan 4: £33,795 a year / £2,816 a month / £649 a week
Plan 5: £25,000 a year / £2,083 a month / £480 a week

Postgraduate loan — 6% of income over the threshold
£21,000 a year / £1,750 a month / £403 a week

PAYE formula this page uses
monthly pay ≈ annual salary ÷ 12
excess = max(0, monthly pay − published monthly threshold)
deduction = 9% or 6% of excess, then rounded down to the nearest pound

Source: GOV.UK — how much you repay. Re-check that page before you rely on a figure — Student Finance, not this calculator, is the authority. Overseas thresholds are different and are not modelled here.

Worked examples (pre-computed)

These use the same monthly thresholds and round-down rule as the calculator, so the answer is readable if JavaScript is off.

1. Plan 5, £35,000 salary
£35,000 ÷ 12 = £2,916.67 a month
minus Plan 5 monthly threshold £2,083 = £833.67 over the line
9% of £833.67 = £75.03 → PAYE takes £75 a month

2. Plan 2, £40,000 salary
£40,000 ÷ 12 = £3,333.33
minus Plan 2 monthly threshold £2,448 = £885.33
9% of £885.33 = £79.68 → PAYE takes £79 a month
The same salary on Plan 5 is higher (lower threshold). Plan type changes the deduction, which is why this is not a renamed copy of the US page.

3. Plan 5 plus a postgraduate loan, £35,000
Postgraduate 6% of (£2,916.67 − £1,750) = £70.00 → £70
Plan 5 9% of (£2,916.67 − £2,083) = £75.03 → £75
Combined PAYE ≈ £145 a month
GOV.UK takes both when you hold both plan types.

4. Plan 5, £24,999
Monthly pay is under £2,083. Excess = £0. Deduction = £0.

What this figure does not include

  • A US-style fixed instalment. Use the student loan calculator only if the loan amortises on a term and rate.
  • An exact payoff date. The projection above is an estimate from the balance, rate and salary growth you enter. Your Student Finance account is the authority on your balance and interest.
  • Overseas repayment thresholds. Different country bands. This page does not mint a country × plan doorway URL.
  • Income tax or National Insurance. This is the student-loan line only. Combine it with PAYE tax and NI on the UK take-home pay calculator. Do not treat a currency switch on the US paycheck page as PAYE.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the same as a US student loan calculator?expand_more

No. UK undergraduate and postgraduate loans repay a percentage of income above a plan threshold. The US student loan calculator on this site models a fixed amortising monthly payment. Switching the currency on that page does not produce a UK payslip deduction.

Which plan am I on?expand_more

Plan 5 covers England and Wales courses that started on or after 1 August 2023. Plan 2 covers England and Wales 1 September 2012 to 31 July 2023. Plan 1 is earlier England/Wales and most Northern Ireland loans. Plan 4 is Scotland. Postgraduate loans use a separate 6% rate. Confirm on GOV.UK or your Student Finance account.

What if I have a postgraduate loan as well?expand_more

GOV.UK says you repay 6% of income over the postgraduate threshold and 9% of income over the lowest undergraduate plan threshold you have. Tick the postgraduate box on this page to add both.

Why might PAYE take a pound less than 9% of the excess?expand_more

Payroll rounds the deduction down to the nearest pound. This page shows the unrounded pence and the PAYE amount.

Does this show when the loan is paid off?expand_more

It projects it. The payslip deduction uses the published 2026/27 thresholds exactly. The payoff projection is an estimate: it depends on your salary growth, the interest rate you enter, and thresholds staying at today's levels.
