First-Time Buyer Relief Differs by Nation

First-time buyers get very different treatment depending on where the home is.

  • England & NI: 0% up to £300,000 and 5% from £300,001 to £500,000 — no relief at all above £500,000 (maximum saving £5,000).
  • Scotland: nil-rate band raised to £175,000, worth up to £600, with no price cap.
  • Wales: no separate first-time buyer relief; the standard £225,000 nil-rate band applies.

How UK Property Tax Was Devolved

Stamp Duty Land Tax once applied across Great Britain. The Scotland Act 2012 devolved it, and Land and Buildings Transaction Tax replaced SDLT in Scotland on 1 April 2015, administered by Revenue Scotland.

The Wales Act 2014 followed, and Land Transaction Tax — collected by the Welsh Revenue Authority — replaced SDLT in Wales in April 2018. Northern Ireland still uses SDLT, administered by HMRC.

Why one comparison page, not three copies

The user job here is “what would this price cost in each nation, and which rule actually applies to me?” The nation calculators answer “how much do I pay under the statute that covers my purchase?” Both jobs are real. Publishing a city page for every town would not change the tax. Publishing England, Scotland and Wales separately does, because the statutes differ. This page only exists to put those three answers next to each other.

What this comparison leaves out

  • Non-resident SDLT surcharge (2%). Tick the toggle above to add it. It applies to residential purchases in England and Northern Ireland only; it is not added to a non-residential freehold, and Scotland and Wales have no equivalent.
  • Lease rent, linked transactions and multiple dwellings relief. The non-residential option is the freehold / premium table only. Rent (NPV) stays unknown.
  • The mortgage instalment. Purchase tax is not a monthly payment. Use the UK repayment calculator for that.

Frequently asked questions

Is stamp duty the same in England, Scotland and Wales?expand_more

No. England and Northern Ireland charge Stamp Duty Land Tax. Scotland charges Land and Buildings Transaction Tax. Wales charges Land Transaction Tax. The bands, first-time buyer rules and additional-home rules are different statutes.

Which nation is cheapest for a main home?expand_more

It depends on the price. At £250,000 a main home is £2,500 in England and Northern Ireland, £2,100 in Scotland and £1,500 in Wales. At £150,000 England and Northern Ireland charge £500, Scotland charges £100 and Wales charges £0. Use the table for the price you are actually paying.

Do first-time buyers get relief in every nation?expand_more

England and Northern Ireland: nil-rate to £300,000 if the price is £500,000 or less, otherwise no relief. Scotland: nil-rate to £175,000 with no £500,000 cap, saving up to £600. Wales: no separate first-time buyer relief. The Welsh £225,000 nil-rate band is the standard main-rate threshold.

How do additional homes differ?expand_more

England and Northern Ireland add 5% of the whole price on top of standard bands. Scotland adds an 8% Additional Dwelling Supplement on the whole price. Wales does not add a flat surcharge. It uses a separate higher-rate table.

Does this compare a shop or office?expand_more

Yes, for a non-residential freehold or lease premium. On GOV.UK’s £275,000 commercial example England and Northern Ireland charge £3,250, Scotland charges £2,250 and Wales charges £1,500. Revenue Scotland’s £465,000 case is £12,750 in England, £11,750 in Scotland and £11,000 in Wales. A £300,000 premises is £4,500, £3,500 and £2,750. ADS, the English 5% surcharge and first-time buyer relief do not apply. Lease rent is not calculated.

Does this replace the nation calculators?expand_more

No. This page answers which statute applies and what each one charges on the same price. The England, Scotland and Wales calculators are the pages that apply one statute in full, including notes those tools already publish.
