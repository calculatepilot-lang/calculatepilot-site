Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between sample and population standard deviation?expand_more

Population standard deviation divides the squared deviations by N, the number of values, and is used when you have every member of the group. Sample standard deviation divides by n − 1 (Bessel's correction), which gives a better estimate of the population's spread when your data is only a sample.

What's the difference between a permutation and a combination?expand_more

In a permutation the order matters, so ABC and CBA count as different arrangements. In a combination the order doesn't matter, so they count as the same selection. For the same n and r there are always at least as many permutations as combinations: nPr = nCr × r!.

Why do some results show tiny rounding differences?expand_more

Computers store most decimals in binary floating point (IEEE 754 double precision), which can't represent numbers like 0.1 exactly — for example 0.1 + 0.2 is stored as 0.30000000000000004. Results are rounded for display, so these tiny differences rarely matter, but for exact work use fractions or the precision your course requires.

Do these calculators store the numbers I enter?expand_more

No. The calculations run in your browser and the numbers you enter aren't sent to or stored on our servers. Like most websites, the site itself uses analytics and advertising cookies — see our privacy policy for details.

Can I use these calculators for homework?expand_more

Yes — they're a good way to check your answers. Each page shows the formula, so you can follow the method, but follow your teacher's rules on rounding, significant figures and showing your working.
