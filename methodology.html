architecture Calculator Standards

How we build each calculator

Every calculator on CalculatePilot is built from a named, verifiable formula — not a proprietary algorithm or approximation we invented. The formula and its source are documented before development begins, and the calculator is verified against at least three known correct inputs (we calculate the expected output manually or from an authoritative reference, then confirm the tool produces the same result).

01

Named Verifiable Formula

Built from established equations published in academic, clinical or statutory sources, not proprietary heuristics.

02

3-Point Baseline Testing

Each calculator is checked against at least three known correct results before it is published.

03

Most-Cited Formula

Where several formulas exist, we use the most widely cited one and say which on the page.

Where a calculation has more than one accepted formula in common use (for example, several formulas exist for ideal weight, lean body mass, and VO2 max estimation), we use the most widely cited in peer-reviewed or professional reference literature, and the calculator page identifies which specific formula is in use and why.

Sources and references

The formulas behind our calculators fall into three groups: financial, health and mathematical.

account_balance

Financial Formulas

Standard TVM

Financial calculators are based on standard time-value-of-money formulas and are consistent with those used in professional financial planning software.

Amortization Algorithms: Fixed monthly compounding: M = P [ i(1 + i)^n ] / [ (1 + i)^n – 1]
Time Value of Money (TVM): Ordinary annuity and compound-growth formulas, with limits and rates taken from published IRS and HMRC guidance.
health_and_safety

Health & Nutrition

Published Medical Literature

Health calculators are built from formulas published in peer-reviewed medical and nutrition literature — the Mifflin-St Jeor equation for BMR (validated in Journal of the American Dietetic Association, 1990), the Boer formula for lean body mass, Institute of Medicine dietary reference intakes for fiber and pregnancy nutrition, and WHO/AHA guidelines for blood pressure categories and body composition assessment.

Mifflin-St Jeor BMR J Am Diet Assoc. 1990; 90(2):241-247
Boer Lean Body Mass Boer P. Am J Physiol. 1984
AHA / WHO Guidelines Hypertension Classifications 2017+

Mathematics

Standard Definitions & Algorithms

Math calculators implement standard mathematical definitions and algorithms — the Euclidean algorithm for GCD, the standard normal distribution approximation (Abramowitz and Stegun) for z-score percentiles, the standard cross-product and dot-product definitions for vectors. Where a calculation has nuanced edge cases (such as the modulo operation for negative numbers, which differs between mathematical and programming conventions), the page explains the difference explicitly.

What our calculators can and cannot do

Every calculator on this site produces an estimate, not a guaranteed outcome. Real-world results depend on factors no formula can fully capture — individual metabolism, specific market conditions, local tax rules, and dozens of other variables. The estimates are designed to be accurate enough to be genuinely useful for planning and decision-making, but they are not a substitute for professional advice on high-stakes decisions.

info Transparent Assumption Disclosures

Where a calculator makes a simplifying assumption that a user should know about — for example, assuming monthly compounding when some loans compound differently, or using a standard 365-day year for interest calculations — the relevant page states this plainly. We do not present simplified models as more precise than they are.

update Code Integrity & Maintenance

Corrections and updates

If a user identifies a genuine error in a formula or calculation, we review the report, correct the calculator if the error is confirmed, and note the correction. Corrections are applied to all pages using the affected formula simultaneously — because our calculators are built from a shared generator rather than individually edited HTML files, a single correction automatically propagates sitewide.

flag Report a Calculation or Formula Error

To report a potential error, email hello@calculatepilot.com with the calculator name, the inputs you used, the output you received, and what you believe the correct output should be. We review every report and respond to all genuine error flags.
