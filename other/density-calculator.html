Formulas

How the numbers are worked out.

science

Density

Mass per unit volume. Rearrange for mass or volume.

ρ = m ÷ V
m = ρ × V
V = m ÷ ρ
layers

Solid fill

For a part that is not fully solid, the effective density is the material density times the fill fraction.

effective ρ = ρ × fill%
water_drop

Floating and buoyancy

An object floats if its effective density is below the fluid's. Water at 20 °C is 0.998 g/cm³.

floats if ρ < 0.998 g/cm³
net force = (water displaced − mass) × g

Worked examples

Illustrative figures, each checked by hand. The first is the default setup.

Steel block

250 cm³ of steel at 7.85 g/cm³ (the default).

mass = 7.85 × 250 = 1,962.5 g = 1.9625 kg = 4.33 lb
displaces 250 g of water, so net downward pull is 1,712.5 g, and it sinks

Volume from mass

5 kg of aluminium 6061 at 2.70 g/cm³.

5,000 g ÷ 2.70 = 1,851.9 cm³ = 1.85 L

3D print

200 cm³ of PLA (1.24 g/cm³) printed at 20% infill.

effective density = 1.24 × 0.20 = 0.248 g/cm³
mass = 0.248 × 200 = 49.6 g
this is below 0.998, so it floats

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate density?expand_more

Divide mass by volume. A block of 1,962.5 g with a volume of 250 cm³ has a density of 7.85 g/cm³.

What is specific gravity?expand_more

The ratio of a material's density to the density of water. Water is 1, steel is about 7.85, and anything below 1 floats in water.

How do I convert g/cm³ to kg/m³?expand_more

Multiply by 1,000. Steel at 7.85 g/cm³ is 7,850 kg/m³.

Why does the fill percentage matter?expand_more

A 3D print or foam is not solid material. A part at 20% infill has roughly one fifth of the solid material's density plus its outer walls, so treat this as an estimate.

Does temperature change density?expand_more

Yes. Most materials expand when heated and become less dense. The change is small for solids and larger for liquids and gases.
