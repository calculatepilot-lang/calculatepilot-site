Common tipping guidelines

In the US, 15-20% is typical for sit-down restaurant service, with 20%+ common for excellent service. Tipping norms vary significantly by country, so adjust the percentage to fit local custom.

Tipping norms and considerations

Tipping percentage expectations vary by service type and region — 15-20% is standard for US sit-down restaurant service, while tipping norms for delivery, bars, and salons often differ. Outside the US, tipping customs vary dramatically, from expected in some countries to unusual or even considered impolite in others.

For large groups, some restaurants automatically add a mandatory gratuity (often 18-20%) to the bill — worth checking before adding an additional tip on top.

A worked example

A $92 bill with an 18% tip: tip amount equals $16.56, total bill equals $108.56. Split three ways, that's about $36.19 per person.

For exceptional service, bumping to 22% instead: tip equals $20.24, total equals $112.24 — about $3.68 more per person across a 3-way split, illustrating how small percentage changes add up on larger bills.

Tipping norms by context

In the US, typical tipping guidelines vary by service type: 15-20% for sit-down restaurant service, 10-15% for buffet or counter service, 20%+ for exceptional service, and different (often lower or non-existent) norms for delivery, bars, or salons depending on region and local custom.

Outside the US, tipping culture varies dramatically — some countries include service charges automatically, others consider tipping unnecessary or even slightly awkward, so it's worth checking local norms when traveling rather than assuming US conventions apply.

Frequently asked questions

Should I tip on the pre-tax or post-tax amount?

Either is acceptable; tipping on the pre-tax amount is slightly more common and is what most people mean by the bill total.

How is the per-person amount calculated?

The total bill plus tip is divided evenly by the number of people entered.

Should I tip on the amount before or after tax?

Tipping on the pre-tax subtotal is slightly more common and what most tip calculators (including this one) assume, though tipping on the post-tax total is also widely accepted.

Is 20% always the expected tip?

20% is common for good service in the US, but 15% remains acceptable for standard service, and some venues include an automatic gratuity — check your bill before adding an additional tip.

Is tipping expected everywhere?

No — tipping norms vary significantly by country and even by service type within the same country, so it's worth researching local customs, especially when traveling internationally.
