Tip & Split Bill Calculator

Work out the tip, sales tax and total, then split the check between any number of diners. The split runs in whole cents, so every share adds back to the exact total with no missing pennies.

Etiquette noteinfo

Tipping on the pre-tax subtotal is the common US custom: staff are tipped for the food and service, not for the sales tax. Some diners tip on the post-tax total, which adds a little.

Tipping around the world

Customs differ a lot. Always check whether a service charge is already on the bill.

United States · 15% – 20%

Expected in sit-down restaurants. Servers are often paid a lower base wage that assumes tips.

Status: expected

United Kingdom · 10% – 12.5%

Often added to the bill as a discretionary service charge. Ask for it to be removed if service was poor.

Status: optional or included

France and Germany · round up 5% – 10%

Service is included in menu prices in France (service compris) and customary in Germany. Rounding up or leaving small change is normal.

Status: included

Japan · 0%

Good service is part of the price, and leaving cash behind can cause confusion.

Status: not customary

Australia · 5% – 10%

Uncommon. Staff are paid an award wage, so tips are reserved for exceptional service.

Status: discretionary

How the split is calculated

Everything runs in whole cents so no fraction of a cent is ever lost.

percent

1. Tip

The tip is a percentage of the pre-tax subtotal, or of subtotal plus tax if you switch the option off.

tip = round(basis × tip% )
receipt_long

2. Total

Add the subtotal, the sales tax and the tip. Rounding options adjust the tip so the total lands on a whole amount.

total = subtotal + tax + tip
group

3. Split

Each diner gets the whole-cent quotient. The remaining cents go one each to the first diners, so shares sum to the total.

share = ⌊total ÷ n⌋ cents
extra = total mod n

Worked examples

Illustrative bills, each checked by hand. The first is the default setup.

Dinner for three

$85.00 subtotal, 8.25% sales tax, 18% tip on the subtotal, three diners.

tax = 85.00 × 8.25% = 7.01
tip = 85.00 × 18% = 15.30
total = 85.00 + 7.01 + 15.30 = 107.31
107.31 ÷ 3 = 35.77 each

How to split a bill with a tip

  • Enter the subtotal before tax, then choose a tip percentage that fits the service.
  • Add the sales tax rate so the total matches your receipt.
  • Set the number of diners for an equal split, or enter weights for an uneven one.
  • Pick a rounding style if you want to pay in whole dollars.

If the check already includes a service charge or automatic gratuity, you may not need to tip further. Set the tip to 0% to see the split of the bill alone.

Frequently asked questions

Should I tip on the pre-tax or post-tax amount?expand_more

Either is acceptable. Tipping on the pre-tax subtotal is the more common custom, because sales tax is a government charge rather than a payment for service. Tipping on the post-tax total adds a little more. Use the toggle to compare.

How is the per-person amount calculated?expand_more

The total bill plus tax and tip is divided by the number of diners in whole cents. Any leftover cents go to the first diners, one cent each, so the shares add up to the exact total.

What is an automatic gratuity for large parties?expand_more

Many restaurants add a fixed service charge, often 18% to 20%, for parties of six or more. Under IRS Revenue Ruling 2012-18 a mandatory charge like this is a service charge rather than a voluntary tip, so check the bill before adding more.

What does the rounding option do?expand_more

Exact cents keeps every amount to the cent. Round total moves the tip so the grand total is a whole dollar. Round tip rounds only the tip to a whole dollar.

How does the custom ratio split work?expand_more

Enter one weight per diner, such as 2, 1, 1. Each diner pays their weight divided by the total weight of the total bill, again in whole cents.
