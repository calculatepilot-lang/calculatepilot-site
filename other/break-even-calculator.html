Formulas

Cost-volume-profit basics

Break-even units

Q = fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost)

Contribution margin

ratio = (price − variable cost) ÷ price

Target profit

units = (fixed costs + target profit) ÷ contribution per unit

Operating leverage is total contribution ÷ operating income. It shows how much profit changes for a 1% change in sales.

Good to know

Margin, markup and break-even

Margin is profit as a share of the selling price, while markup is profit as a share of cost. The same sale gives a higher markup than margin, which is why they must not be swapped.

Break-even assumes a steady price and cost per unit. Discounts, volume pricing and step costs will move it.

Worked examples

Illustrative businesses, each checked by hand. The first is the default setup.

Break-even volume

Fixed costs $12,500, variable cost $24.50, price $65.

contribution = 65 − 24.50 = 40.50
12,500 ÷ 40.50 = 308.64
309 units, revenue 309 × 65 = $20,085

Target profit

Same business, $5,000 profit wanted.

(12,500 + 5,000) ÷ 40.50 = 432.1
433 units, revenue = $28,145

Margin to markup

Cost $100 and a 40% gross margin.

price = 100 ÷ (1 − 0.40) = $166.67
profit = 66.67
markup = 66.67 ÷ 100 = 66.67%

How to find your break-even point

  • Add up your fixed costs for the period, such as rent, salaries and subscriptions.
  • Work out the variable cost of one unit, and its selling price.
  • Divide fixed costs by the contribution per unit (price minus variable cost).
  • Round up to whole units, because you cannot sell part of a unit.

If the price is at or below the variable cost, you never break even, however much you sell. Raise the price, cut variable costs, or reduce fixed costs.

Frequently asked questions

What is the break-even point?expand_more

It is the sales volume at which total revenue equals total costs, so profit is exactly zero. Above it you make a profit and below it you make a loss.

What is the difference between margin and markup?expand_more

Margin is profit divided by the selling price, and markup is profit divided by the cost. A product costing $100 sold at $150 has a 33.3% margin but a 50% markup.

What is contribution margin?expand_more

It is the selling price minus the variable cost of a unit. It is the amount each sale contributes toward covering fixed costs and then profit.

What is the margin of safety?expand_more

It is how far sales can fall before you hit break-even, shown here as a percentage of your target volume. A bigger cushion means lower risk.

How do I lower my break-even point?expand_more

Raise the price, reduce variable costs per unit, or cut fixed costs. The sensitivity table shows how much a 5% or 10% change in price or variable cost moves the number of units.
