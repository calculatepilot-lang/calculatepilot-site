How this calculator works

Sleep occurs in cycles of roughly 90 minutes. Waking up at the end of a cycle, rather than in the middle of one, tends to feel more refreshing — this calculator counts back from your wake time in 90-minute increments, plus 15 minutes to fall asleep.

Bedtime = wake time − (cycles × 90 min) − 15 min
6 cycles (9 hours) is shown as the primary result; 5 and 4 cycles are shown as alternatives.

Why sleep cycles matter for how rested you feel

Waking up in the middle of a deep sleep phase (rather than at a natural cycle transition) is a common reason people feel groggy even after what seems like enough total sleep time. This is often called 'sleep inertia,' and timing your wake-up to align with the end of a complete cycle can help reduce it.

Individual sleep cycle length can vary somewhat from the 90-minute average used here — if these suggested times consistently don't feel optimal, experimenting with cycle lengths between 80-100 minutes may better match your personal pattern.

A worked example

If you need to wake at 6:30 AM, the 6-cycle (9-hour) bedtime works out to about 9:15 PM, the 5-cycle (7.5-hour) option is about 10:45 PM, and the 4-cycle (6-hour) option is about 12:15 AM.

Choosing between these options depends on your schedule constraints — 6 cycles is ideal when possible, but the 5-cycle option still provides a reasonably complete number of full sleep cycles when time is more limited.

Understanding sleep cycle science

A full sleep cycle moves through light sleep, deep sleep, and REM (dream) sleep in roughly 90-minute stages. Waking up during deep sleep tends to produce grogginess (sleep inertia), while waking at the boundary between cycles — closer to light sleep — tends to feel more natural and refreshed.

Most adults complete 4-6 full cycles in a typical night, which is why sleep recommendations often come in multiples of 1.5 hours rather than round numbers like 7 or 8 hours exactly.

Frequently asked questions

Why 90-minute cycles?

A full sleep cycle — including light sleep, deep sleep, and REM — takes roughly 90 minutes on average, though this varies somewhat by individual.

Is more sleep always better?

Most adults need 7-9 hours per night. Waking at the end of a complete cycle within that range tends to feel better than waking mid-cycle, even if total time is similar.

Is 6 sleep cycles (9 hours) too much sleep?

Most adults need 7-9 hours per night, so 6 cycles falls within a healthy range for many people — though individual needs vary, and consistently needing more or less than this range is worth noting.

Why do I still feel tired even after a full night's sleep?

Waking mid-cycle (rather than at a natural transition point) is one common cause, but sleep quality, stress, and underlying health factors can also affect how rested you feel independent of total sleep duration.

Why do sleep recommendations come in 90-minute increments?

Because a full sleep cycle takes roughly 90 minutes, and waking up at the end of a complete cycle (rather than mid-cycle) tends to feel more refreshing, even if total sleep time is similar.
