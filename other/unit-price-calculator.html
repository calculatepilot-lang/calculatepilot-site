Why bigger packages aren't always cheaper

It's a common assumption that buying in bulk always saves money, but that isn't guaranteed — retailers sometimes price larger package sizes at the same or even a higher per-unit cost, particularly for 'family size' or promotional packaging. Comparing unit price directly is the only reliable way to know for sure.

This matters most for products you buy regularly, where small per-unit savings compound meaningfully over time — for a one-off purchase the difference may not be worth worrying about, but for household staples it adds up.

Comparing different unit types

For this comparison to be meaningful, both quantities need to be in the same unit — comparing a price per ounce to a price per pound will give a misleading result unless converted to a common unit first.

Many stores now display unit pricing directly on shelf tags, but the units used aren't always consistent between similar products (e.g., price per ounce vs. price per count), which is exactly the kind of inconsistency this calculator helps cut through.

Frequently asked questions

Is the larger package always the better deal?

Not necessarily — retailers sometimes price larger packages at the same or even a higher per-unit cost, especially for promotional or 'family size' packaging, so it's worth checking unit price directly rather than assuming.

Do both products need to use the same unit of measurement?

Yes — for an accurate comparison, both quantities should be entered in the same unit (both in ounces, both in count, etc.); comparing different units directly will give a misleading result.
