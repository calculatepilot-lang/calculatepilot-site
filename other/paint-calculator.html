Formulas

How the paint is calculated

Net wall area

A = 2 × (L + W) × H − doors × 21 − windows × 15 − other

Gallons to buy

gal = ⌈ A × coats ÷ spread × 1.10 ⌉

Ceiling

A = L × W
Assumptions

What is included

Doors are taken as 3 × 7 ft (21 sq ft) and windows as 3 × 5 ft (15 sq ft). Use the custom field for larger openings such as sliding doors.

Waste of 10% covers spills, roller loss and touch-ups. Ceilings use the same number of coats. Trim is estimated at 6 in baseboards and 3.5 in casings, painted at 350 sq ft per gallon.

Very deep colour changes and rough textures may need extra coats or primer.

Worked examples

Illustrative rooms, each checked by hand. The first is the default setup.

18 × 14 ft room, 9 ft ceiling

2 doors, 2 windows, smooth drywall, 2 coats.

walls = 2 × 32 × 9 = 576
openings = 42 + 30 = 72
net = 504 sq ft
504 × 2 ÷ 400 × 1.10 = 2.77 gal → 3 gal

Ceiling of the same room

18 × 14 ft, 2 coats, smooth.

area = 252 sq ft
252 × 2 ÷ 400 × 1.10 = 1.39 gal
buy 2 gal (or 1 gal + 2 qt)

Textured walls

Same room, orange-peel texture at 350 sq ft per gallon.

504 × 2 ÷ 350 × 1.10 = 3.17 gal
buy 3 gal + 1 qt

How much paint do I need?

  • Measure the room length, width and ceiling height.
  • Count doors and windows so their area comes off the walls.
  • Choose the surface texture, which changes how far a gallon goes, and the number of coats.
  • Add ceiling, trim and primer if you are painting those too, then read the shopping list.

Buy all the paint for one room at the same time and mix cans together (boxing) so the colour matches from wall to wall.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a gallon of paint cover?expand_more

Manufacturers usually state 350 to 400 square feet per gallon on smooth, primed walls. Textured, porous or unprimed surfaces soak up more paint and can drop coverage to 250 to 300 square feet per gallon.

Do I need primer?expand_more

Use primer on new drywall, bare wood, patched areas, stains, and when moving from a glossy or oil-based paint to latex. A good paint-and-primer can replace it on already-painted, clean walls.

How many coats are needed?expand_more

Two coats give a durable, even finish for most jobs. One coat can work for touch-ups in the same colour, and three may be needed for a big colour change such as dark to light.

How do I measure a vaulted ceiling?expand_more

For a sloped ceiling, find the sloped length with the Pythagorean theorem (√(rise² + run²)) and multiply by the room length. Add the triangular gable wall, which is ½ × base × height.

How should I store leftover paint?expand_more

Seal the lid tightly, store the can upside down or on its side if the manufacturer allows, and keep it somewhere cool and dry above freezing. Label it with the colour, room and date.
