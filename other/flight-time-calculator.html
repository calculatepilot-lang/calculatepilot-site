Why actual flight times include more than cruise speed

A flight's total scheduled time includes more than just time spent cruising at top speed — taxiing to the runway, the climb to cruising altitude, and the descent and landing all take additional time beyond straightforward distance-divided-by-speed math. This calculator adds a buffer to approximate that overhead.

Commercial airliners typically cruise around 480-560 mph depending on aircraft type and route, so 500 mph is a reasonable default estimate for a typical jet — adjust it if you know your specific aircraft's typical cruise speed.

What this estimate doesn't account for

Real flight times are also affected by wind (tailwinds shorten flight time, headwinds lengthen it), air traffic control routing and holding patterns, and weather delays — this calculator provides a straightforward distance-and-speed estimate, not an official flight schedule.

For actual travel planning, always use the airline's published scheduled flight time, which already accounts for typical routing and historical performance on that specific route rather than a generic distance-based estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my calculated time different from what the airline shows?

This is a simplified estimate based on distance and average speed — actual scheduled flight times also account for typical wind patterns, routing, and historical performance on that specific route, which this calculator doesn't include.

What's a typical cruise speed for a commercial flight?

Most commercial jets cruise in the range of roughly 480-560 mph depending on the aircraft type, making 500 mph a reasonable general estimate if you don't know the specific aircraft.
