Where this breakdown comes from

This calculator uses commonly cited industry percentage allocations as a starting framework: roughly 45% for venue and catering (typically the largest expense by far), 10% photography and video, 8% each for attire, flowers, entertainment, and planning, with smaller allocations for stationery and favors, plus a buffer for unexpected costs.

These percentages are a general guideline, not a rule — actual allocation varies enormously based on personal priorities. Many couples intentionally over-allocate to one category that matters most to them (photography, for example) and cut back elsewhere.

Why a buffer category matters

Wedding budgets are notorious for running over their initial estimate — unexpected costs (vendor gratuities, last-minute changes, additional guests) are common enough that building in a deliberate buffer from the start tends to reduce financial stress compared to allocating 100% of the budget upfront with no cushion.

If a particular category matters more to you than the standard allocation suggests, it's reasonable to intentionally shift percentages toward it — this breakdown is a starting point for discussion, not a fixed formula every wedding should follow.

Frequently asked questions

Do all weddings need to follow this exact percentage breakdown?

No — this is a common starting framework, but couples often reallocate based on personal priorities, putting more toward what matters most to them (like photography or venue) and less toward other categories.

Why is venue and catering such a large percentage?

It's typically the single largest wedding expense by far, since it usually includes the space rental, food, and often beverages for all guests — which is why it commonly represents close to half of a total wedding budget.
