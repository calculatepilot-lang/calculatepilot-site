Financial Calculators

Mortgages, loans, taxes, retirement, and everything money-related — 43+ free financial calculators. Multi-currency support. No signup required.

Money decisions are hard to reverse. A loan term you accept today shapes your budget for years; a retirement contribution rate set in your thirties compounds into a very different number by your sixties. The calculators below exist to make those trade-offs visible before you commit rather than after.

They fall into a few natural groups. Borrowing tools — loan, auto loan, mortgage, amortization, HELOC — work out what a debt actually costs once interest is included, which is usually far more than the amount borrowed. Saving and investing tools project growth over time, where compounding does most of the work and starting early matters more than contributing heavily. Income and tax tools translate between gross and net, which is the gap that catches out most people changing jobs or going freelance. And financial health tools — debt-to-income, credit utilisation, emergency fund — measure where you stand against the benchmarks lenders actually use.

Getting useful answers

Three things trip people up more than any others when using financial calculators.

Compare APR, not the interest rate. The rate covers borrowing the principal alone. APR folds in most lender fees, so it reflects what the loan really costs. A 6.9% rate with a 3% origination fee is more expensive than a 7.4% rate with none, and only the APR makes that visible.

A lower monthly payment usually means a more expensive loan. Stretching a term reduces the payment and increases total interest, because you are borrowing the same money for longer. Both numbers are worth looking at before deciding which matters more to you.

Mortgage payments are quoted as PITI. Principal and interest are only part of it — property tax and homeowner's insurance are collected into escrow, and private mortgage insurance is added if your down payment was under 20%. Those can add several hundred dollars a month, and none of it reduces your balance.

Every calculator here shows the formula it uses. These are estimates for planning, not financial advice, and they cannot account for your full circumstances — for decisions of any size, talk to a qualified adviser.
