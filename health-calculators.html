Health & Fitness Calculators

BMI, calories, macros, pregnancy, and body-composition calculators grounded in standard clinical formulas. 30+ free health calculators, no signup.

Health metrics are useful precisely because they are crude. BMI cannot tell you whether you are healthy, but it can tell you whether your weight sits inside the range where population-level risk is lowest — and that is a reasonable starting point for a conversation with a doctor. The tools below are built the same way: quick estimates that give you a number to reason from, not a diagnosis.

They group into four areas. Body composition tools — BMI, body fat, lean mass, waist-to-height ratio — describe how weight is distributed, which matters more for health outcomes than weight alone. Energy and nutrition tools work out how much you burn and what to eat: BMR is your resting requirement, TDEE adds activity, and macro and protein targets divide that total. Fitness tools track training — one-rep max, target heart rate, VO2 max, running pace. And pregnancy tools cover due dates, ovulation timing and the changing nutritional needs of each trimester.

What these numbers can and cannot tell you

Every formula here is a population average applied to an individual, and that gap matters.

BMI is the clearest example. It uses only height and weight, so it cannot distinguish muscle from fat. A muscular athlete is frequently classed as overweight; someone sedentary with low muscle mass can sit in the normal range while carrying an unhealthy amount of visceral fat. Waist-to-height ratio is often more informative for exactly this reason, and it needs nothing more than a tape measure.

Calorie estimates carry a similar caveat. BMR formulas typically fall within about 10% of measured values for most people, which on a 2,000-calorie target is a 200-calorie spread. Treat the output as a starting point, then adjust based on what actually happens over two to three weeks.

These tools are for general information and are not medical advice. They cannot diagnose anything or account for medical conditions, medication or individual circumstances. If a result concerns you, or you are making a significant change to diet, exercise or medication, speak to a doctor or registered dietitian.
