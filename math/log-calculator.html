    For b > 0, b ≠ 1 and positive x and y.

    What is a logarithm?

    The logarithm log_b(x) answers “to what power must I raise b to get x?” If bʸ = x then y = log_b(x). The three most common bases are 10 (common logarithm, written log or log₁₀), e ≈ 2.71828 (natural logarithm, ln) and 2 (binary logarithm, log₂, used in computing).

    Characteristic and mantissa

    For a base-10 logarithm, the integer part (the characteristic) gives the order of magnitude and the fractional part (the mantissa) depends only on the digits. Because log₁₀ 500 = log₁₀ 5 + log₁₀ 100 = 0.69897 + 2, the characteristic is 2 and the mantissa 0.69897. Printed log tables listed mantissas only.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why can’t I take the logarithm of zero or a negative number?expand_more

    For a positive base, bʸ is always positive, so no real y gives zero or a negative result. As x approaches 0 from above, log_b(x) heads to −∞ (a vertical asymptote). In the complex numbers, logs of negative numbers exist: ln(−x) = ln x + πi; this page shows that principal value for a negative argument.

    What is the difference between log, ln and log₂?expand_more

    They are logarithms in different bases: log is base 10 in most school and engineering contexts, ln is base e, and log₂ is base 2. In some programming languages and mathematics texts, “log” means the natural logarithm, so check the convention.

    Why is the natural logarithm so important in calculus?expand_more

    Because d/dx ln x = 1/x with no extra constant. For any other base, d/dx log_b x = 1 ÷ (x ln b). The number e is the unique base for which the exponential function is its own derivative, which is why it appears in growth, decay and compound interest.

    How do I calculate a logarithm in a base my calculator lacks?expand_more

    Use change of base: log_b(x) = ln x ÷ ln b (or log₁₀ x ÷ log₁₀ b). For log₃ 500: ln 500 ÷ ln 3 = 6.2146 ÷ 1.0986 = 5.6568.

    What is an antilogarithm?expand_more

    It is the inverse operation: the antilog of y in base b is bʸ. If log₁₀ x = 2.69897 then x = 10^2.69897 ≈ 500. Use the Antilog tab.

    Why can’t the base be 1?expand_more

    Because 1 raised to any power is 1, so 1ʸ can never equal any other number and log₁(x) would be undefined. Also, ln 1 = 0 would make the change-of-base formula divide by zero.
