    Why quartiles instead of mean and standard deviation?

    Quartiles and the IQR are resistant statistics: a few extreme values barely move them, whereas the mean and standard deviation can shift a lot.

    Example with the default data n = 14

    With the outlier 95: mean = 35.14, median = 29
    Without it (n = 13): mean = 30.54, median = 28

    Dropping one extreme value moves the mean by about 4.6 but the median by only 1.

    Breakdown point 25%

    Up to a quarter of the data can be replaced by arbitrarily extreme values before the quartiles themselves are dragged away. The mean can be moved arbitrarily by changing a single value.

    Robust summaries

    What are quartiles and the IQR?

    Quartiles split a sorted data set into four parts holding roughly equal numbers of values. Q1 is the 25th percentile, Q2 is the median (50th percentile) and Q3 is the 75th percentile. The interquartile range, IQR = Q3 − Q1, is the spread of the middle half of the data.

    How the four methods place the cut points

    • Inclusive (R-7): rank L = 1 + p(n − 1), interpolating between neighbouring sorted values. Used by Excel QUARTILE.INC, R’s default and NumPy’s default.
    • Exclusive (R-6): rank L = p(n + 1), interpolating. Used by Excel QUARTILE.EXC, Minitab and SPSS; needs n ≥ 3.
    • Tukey’s hinges: split the sorted data at the median and take the median of each half; for an odd n the median belongs to both halves.
    • Moore & McCabe: as above, but for an odd n the median is left out of both halves.

    Tukey’s outlier fences

    Values below Q1 − 1.5 × IQR or above Q3 + 1.5 × IQR lie outside the inner fences and are flagged as possible (mild) outliers. Values beyond Q1 − 3 × IQR or Q3 + 3 × IQR lie outside the outer fences and are flagged as extreme. A flag is a prompt to investigate, not proof that a value is an error.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why does Excel give a different Q1 than my textbook?expand_more

    Excel’s QUARTILE.INC interpolates at rank 1 + 0.25(n − 1), while many textbooks take the median of the lower half. Both are legitimate definitions; the comparison table above shows the results side by side for your data.

    What is the interquartile range used for?expand_more

    It measures the spread of the middle 50% of the data and, unlike the range or standard deviation, is barely affected by extreme values. It is also the basis of Tukey’s outlier fences.

    How are mild and extreme outliers classified?expand_more

    A value beyond 1.5 × IQR from the nearer quartile is a mild outlier; beyond 3 × IQR it is an extreme outlier. The 1.5 and 3 multipliers are Tukey’s conventions rather than laws of nature.

    Can the lower fence be negative when all data is positive?expand_more

    Yes. The fence is a calculation from Q1 and the IQR, so it can fall below zero even if every value is positive. It simply means no value is low enough to be flagged at the bottom.

    What is the difference between quartiles and percentiles?expand_more

    Quartiles are specific percentiles: Q1 is the 25th, Q2 the 50th and Q3 the 75th. Percentile methods share the same definitional choices as the quartile methods on this page.

    How many values do I need?expand_more

    Quartiles can be computed from as few as two values with the inclusive and hinge methods, but they are only meaningful with a reasonable amount of data. The exclusive method requires at least three values.
