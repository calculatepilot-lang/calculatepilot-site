    Key formulas

    • Multiplication: (a+bi)(c+di) = (ac−bd) + (ad+bc)i.
    • Division: multiply by the conjugate: z₁/z₂ = z₁z̄₂ / |z₂|².
    • Modulus: |z| = √(a²+b²). Argument: θ = atan2(b, a), in (−π, π].
    • De Moivre's theorem: [r(cos θ + i sin θ)]ⁿ = rⁿ(cos nθ + i sin nθ).
    • nth roots: ⁿ√z has n values, at angles (θ+2πk)/n for k = 0, …, n−1, each with modulus |z|^(1/n).

    How to divide complex numbers: worked example

    Multiply the top and bottom by the conjugate of the denominator. For (3 + 4i) ÷ (1 − 2i), the conjugate of 1 − 2i is 1 + 2i.

    Top: (3 + 4i)(1 + 2i) = 3 + 6i + 4i + 8i² = 3 + 10i − 8 = −5 + 10i. Bottom: (1 − 2i)(1 + 2i) = 1 + 4 = 5. Result: (−5 + 10i) ÷ 5 = −1 + 2i.

    Polar form, modulus and argument

    For z = a + bi the modulus (also called the magnitude or absolute value) is |z| = √(a² + b²) and the argument is θ = atan2(b, a). Polar form is r(cos θ + i sin θ), often written r∠θ. Angles below are in degrees.

    Use atan2(b, a), not atan(b ÷ a). Plain arctangent cannot tell −3 − 4i from 3 + 4i, because both have b ÷ a = 4/3, but their angles differ by 180°.

    Square roots and nth roots

    Every non-zero complex number has two square roots and n different nth roots. For √(3 + 4i), the roots are 2 + i and −2 − i, because (2 + i)² = 4 + 4i + i² = 3 + 4i.

    The cube roots of 1 have modulus 1 and angles 0°, 120° and 240°:

    The four 4th roots of 1 are 1, i, −1 and −i, at 0°, 90°, 180° and 270°. For the cube roots of 8i, use modulus 8^(1/3) = 2 and angles (90° + 360°k) ÷ 3 = 30°, 150° and 270°.

    Powers of i

    The powers of i repeat every four: i, −1, −i, 1. To find iⁿ, divide n by 4 and use the remainder (a remainder of 0 means 1). For example, 27 ÷ 4 leaves a remainder of 3, so i²⁷ = i³ = −i.

    Notation: i, j, magnitude and phasors

    Mathematics writes the imaginary unit as i. Electrical engineering writes it as j so that i can stand for current. The result is the same: j² = −1. Modulus, magnitude and absolute value mean the same thing for a complex number, and argument, angle and phase mean the same thing. A phasor is a complex number in polar form, such as 5∠53.13°. When you multiply in polar form, multiply the moduli and add the angles: (3 + 4i)(1 − 2i) = (5∠53.13°)(√5∠−63.43°) = 11.18∠−10.3°, which equals 11 − 2i.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why does dividing complex numbers use the conjugate?expand_more

    Multiplying the denominator by its conjugate z̄ turns it into the real number |z|², eliminating i from the denominator — the complex equivalent of rationalizing a square root.

    What is the geometric meaning of multiplying complex numbers?expand_more

    Multiplication multiplies the moduli and adds the arguments — geometrically, it scales and rotates. This is exactly what De Moivre's theorem generalizes to powers and roots.

    Why does a complex number have n different nth roots?expand_more

    Because angles that differ by a full turn (2π) describe the same point, but raising to the nth power multiplies the angle by n — so n different starting angles, spaced 2π/n apart, all land on the same result.

    What is the complex conjugate used for?expand_more

    Besides division, z·z̄ = |z|² always gives a real number, which is essential for finding magnitudes, normalizing vectors, and in signal processing for computing power from a complex amplitude.

    How is Euler's formula related to polar form?expand_more

    Euler's formula e^(iθ) = cos θ + i sin θ lets polar form r(cos θ + i sin θ) be written compactly as r·e^(iθ) — the same number, and the form that makes multiplication, powers and roots most natural.

    How do you divide complex numbers?expand_more

    Multiply the numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator, which makes the denominator a real number. (3 + 4i) ÷ (1 − 2i) = (−5 + 10i) ÷ 5 = −1 + 2i.

    What is the magnitude or absolute value of a complex number?expand_more

    The modulus |a + bi| = √(a² + b²). For 3 + 4i it is √25 = 5. Magnitude, modulus and absolute value are the same thing.

    How do you convert a complex number to polar form?expand_more

    Find r = √(a² + b²) and θ = atan2(b, a), then write r(cos θ + i sin θ) or r∠θ. For 3 + 4i that is 5∠53.13°.

    How do you find the angle of a complex number?expand_more

    Use atan2(b, a), which gives the correct quadrant. For −3 − 4i the angle is −126.87°, not 53.13°.

    What is the square root of a complex number?expand_more

    There are two, opposite in sign. √(3 + 4i) = ±(2 + i), because (2 + i)² = 3 + 4i. The nth roots come from De Moivre's theorem.

    How do you find the nth roots of a complex number?expand_more

    Take the nth root of the modulus and use angles (θ + 360°k) ÷ n for k = 0 to n − 1. The cube roots of 1 are at 0°, 120° and 240°.

    What is the difference between i and j?expand_more

    Nothing mathematically. Engineers use j so that i can mean current. Both satisfy j² = i² = −1.

    How do you work out powers of i?expand_more

    They repeat every four: i, −1, −i, 1. Divide the exponent by 4 and use the remainder, so i²⁷ = i³ = −i.
