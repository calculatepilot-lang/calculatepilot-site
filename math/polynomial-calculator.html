    What is a polynomial?

    A polynomial is a sum of terms aₖxᵏ with non-negative integer powers, such as 3x⁴ − 2x + 7. Its degree is the highest power and the leading coefficient is the coefficient of that term. The Fundamental Theorem of Algebra says a degree-n polynomial has exactly n roots, counting multiplicity, in the complex numbers.

    Key theorems

    • Division algorithm: P(x) = Q(x)·D(x) + R(x) with deg R < deg D.
    • Remainder theorem: dividing by x − c leaves remainder P(c).
    • Factor theorem: (x − c) divides P(x) exactly when P(c) = 0.
    • Rational root theorem: for integer coefficients, any rational root p/q has p dividing the constant term and q dividing the leading coefficient.

    Frequently asked questions

    When can I use synthetic division instead of long division?expand_more

    When the divisor is linear, x − c (a monic linear factor). It needs only the coefficients and c, and is faster and less error-prone. For a divisor such as 2x − 6, divide the coefficients by 2 first (2x − 6 = 2(x − 3)) or use long division. For divisors of degree 2 or more, use long division.

    How does Horner’s scheme reduce the number of operations?expand_more

    It rewrites aₙxⁿ + … + a₀ as (…((aₙx + aₙ₋₁)x + aₙ₋₂)x + …)x + a₀, needing only n multiplications and n additions instead of computing each power separately. It is also more accurate in floating-point arithmetic.

    What does the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra guarantee?expand_more

    Every non-constant polynomial with complex coefficients has at least one complex root, and therefore exactly n roots counting multiplicity. Real polynomials may have complex roots, but they come in conjugate pairs a ± bi.

    Why are complex roots always in pairs?expand_more

    If a polynomial has real coefficients and a + bi is a root, its conjugate a − bi is also a root, because conjugation preserves real coefficients. So non-real roots of real polynomials appear in conjugate pairs.

    Is there a formula for the roots of every polynomial?expand_more

    Only up to degree 4 (the quadratic, cubic and quartic formulas). The Abel–Ruffini theorem shows there is no general algebraic formula for degree 5 or higher, so numerical methods — as used here — are needed.

    How accurate are the numerical roots?expand_more

    Roots are refined to about 12 significant digits. Repeated roots and very high degrees are less well conditioned, so their computed values can be slightly less accurate. Exact rational roots are found first, before any numerical work.
