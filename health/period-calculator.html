How this calculator works

  • Next period = first day of last period + cycle length; each following period adds another cycle length.
  • Period end = start + period length − 1 day.
  • Ovulation ≈ 14 days before the next period starts.

The regularity check uses the International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) 2018 parameters for normal menstruation in non-pregnant adults: a cycle every 24–38 days, bleeding for up to 8 days, and a difference between shortest and longest cycles of no more than 7–9 days (up to 7 days for ages 26–41, up to 9 days for ages 18–25 and 42–45).

A worked example

A last period starting 1 July with a 29-day cycle: the next period is estimated around 30 July, then 28 August. With a 5-day period, the first runs 30 July – 3 August. If the last three cycles were 27, 29 and 33 days, the variation is 6 days — within FIGO's normal limit.

When these predictions work less well

The calculator assumes your cycle repeats at the length you enter, so it is least reliable when something is changing your cycle.

  • Hormonal birth control. On the combined pill, bleeding is usually a scheduled withdrawal bleed rather than a natural period. Implants, injections and hormonal IUDs can make bleeding irregular, light or absent, so a "next period" date may not apply.
  • After a birth or while breastfeeding. Periods can stay away or be irregular for months.
  • Perimenopause. Cycles often become longer, shorter or skipped in the years before menopause.
  • Conditions that affect ovulation, such as polycystic ovary syndrome or thyroid problems, can make cycles long or unpredictable. Worth raising with a healthcare provider.

Most guidance counts day 1 as the first day of bleeding with a flow, not light spotting beforehand. Use the same rule every time so your cycle lengths are comparable.

When to talk to a doctor

Cycles often vary by a few days, especially in the first years after periods start and in the years before menopause. It's worth seeing a healthcare provider if your periods come less than 24 or more than 38 days apart, last more than 8 days, are very heavy, stop for 3 months or more when you're not pregnant, or suddenly become irregular. Tracking your start dates over several months makes these conversations easier.

Frequently asked questions

How long is a normal menstrual cycle?expand_more

Under FIGO's 2018 definitions, a normal adult cycle is 24 to 38 days long, counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next.

Why did my period come early or late?expand_more

Stress, illness, travel, weight changes, intense exercise and some medicines can shift ovulation, which moves your next period. A single early or late period is common; a persistent change is worth discussing with a doctor. If you could be pregnant, take a pregnancy test.

How accurate are period predictions?expand_more

They're most accurate for regular cycles. Even then, a few days either way is normal. Using your average from several recent cycles improves the estimate.
