How this calculator works

Calories in a drink come from two sources — the alcohol itself and any carbohydrate (sugar and starch):

  • Alcohol (g) = volume (mL) × ABV ÷ 100 × 0.789 (the density of ethanol)
  • Calories = alcohol (g) × 7 + carbs (g) × 4
  • US standard drinks = alcohol (g) ÷ 14 · UK units = alcohol (g) ÷ 8 (10 mL of pure alcohol)

Preset carb values are typical figures from USDA FoodData Central; check the label for your brand, as beers, ciders and cocktails vary widely.

By Suhaib Hassan · Reviewed 28 September 2026 against NIAAA, UK CMO and U.S. Dietary Guidelines · How CalculatePilot verifies formulas →

A worked example

A 12-oz (355 mL) regular beer at 5% ABV: 355 × 0.05 × 0.789 = 14.0 g of alcohol — exactly one US standard drink — giving 98 kcal. Its 13 g of carbs add 52 kcal, for about 150 kcal. Three beers twice a week is 900 kcal a week, or about 47,000 kcal a year.

What the guidelines say

The Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025–2030 advise consuming less alcohol for better overall health, and say pregnant women, people recovering from alcohol use disorder, and people whose medications or conditions interact with alcohol should avoid it completely. (The previous 2020–2025 edition set limits of 2 drinks or fewer a day for men and 1 or fewer for women.) The UK Chief Medical Officers advise drinking no more than 14 units a week, spread over three or more days.

Why alcohol calories are easy to overlook

At about 7 kcal per gram, alcohol is more energy-dense than carbohydrate or protein (4 kcal/g) and just below fat (9 kcal/g). Drinks are rarely tracked as closely as food, and sugary mixers, syrups and juices can double a cocktail's calories.

How strength changes the calories

Alcohol-only calories (alcohol grams × 7), before any carbs or mixers. Every extra 1% ABV adds about 20 kcal to a 12 oz drink and about 8 kcal to a 5 oz glass of wine.

Light beer vs regular beer

Using this calculator's presets, a regular 5% beer is about 150 kcal and a light 4.2% beer about 106 kcal, a saving of roughly 44 kcal per drink. Over 5 drinks a week that is about 11,000 kcal a year.

Mixers and cocktails

Spirits on their own have no carbs, but sugary mixers do. The spirit-and-regular-soda preset (7.5 oz at 8% ABV, 20 g carbs) comes to about 178 kcal: 98 from alcohol and 80 from sugar. For any cocktail, choose Custom and enter the carbs per drink from the recipe or label.

Frequently asked questions

Why does alcohol have more calories than carbs or protein?expand_more

Alcohol provides about 7 calories per gram, compared with 4 calories per gram for carbohydrates and protein — a big part of why drinks add up faster than their serving size might suggest.

Are cocktails always higher in calories than beer or wine?expand_more

Often, but not always — it depends on the recipe. Cocktails with sugary mixers, syrups or cream can be far higher in calories than a spirit with soda water or a glass of dry wine.

What is a standard drink?expand_more

In the United States, one standard drink contains about 14 grams (0.6 fl oz) of pure alcohol — roughly a 12-oz beer at 5%, a 5-oz glass of wine at 12%, or a 1.5-oz shot of 40% spirits. A UK unit is smaller, at 8 grams.

Does the calculator include the calories from mixers?expand_more

Only if you enter them. Choose Custom and add the carbs per drink, or use the spirit-and-soda preset, which adds 20 g of sugar (80 kcal) to the alcohol calories.

How many calories are in a standard drink?expand_more

A US standard drink holds 14 g of pure alcohol, which is about 98 kcal from the alcohol alone. Carbs in beer, wine or mixers come on top.

Does light beer really save calories?expand_more

Yes. With this calculator's presets, a light 4.2% beer is about 106 kcal against 150 kcal for a regular 5% beer, a saving of about 44 kcal per drink.

Which alcoholic drink has the fewest calories?expand_more

Per standard drink the alcohol calories are about the same, roughly 98 kcal. Straight spirits and hard seltzer have the fewest carbs, so they come out lowest, but drinking more of them erases the saving.

Why do my results differ from a drink label?expand_more

Labels include the actual carbs and sugar of the brand, which vary widely. Enter the carbs from the label under Custom for a closer figure.
