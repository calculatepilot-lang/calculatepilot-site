How this calculator works

It uses the Widmark formula, the classic method for estimating blood alcohol:

  • BAC (%) = alcohol (g) ÷ (body weight (g) × r) × 100 − 0.015 × hours
  • r (the body-water distribution factor) = 0.68 for men and 0.55 for women, Widmark's average values
  • Alcohol (g) = standard drinks × 14 g, or drinks × size (mL) × ABV × 0.789
  • 0.015% per hour is an average elimination rate; real rates vary from person to person

A worked example

A 176 lb (80 kg) man who has had 4 standard drinks (56 g of alcohol) over 2 hours: 56 ÷ (80,000 × 0.68) × 100 = 0.103%, minus 0.015 × 2 = about 0.073%. At 0.015% an hour, it would take roughly another 5 hours to fall to zero.

This is an estimate — not a safety determination

Real BAC depends on food, drinking speed, medications, health, body composition and individual metabolism, so your true BAC could be higher or lower than this estimate. Alcohol also keeps being absorbed for a while after your last drink, so BAC can still be rising. Impairment starts well below legal limits: NHTSA notes that even at 0.02% people show some loss of judgment and a decline in the ability to do two things at once. No calculator can tell you it's safe to drive. If you've been drinking, use a rideshare, taxi, public transport or a sober designated driver.

The legal limit for drivers 21 and over is 0.08% in every US state except Utah (0.05%); commercial drivers are limited to 0.04%, and drivers under 21 face zero-tolerance laws.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is a BAC calculator?expand_more

It gives a rough, population-average estimate. Two people drinking the same amount can have very different BACs because of food, drinking speed, body composition, medications and metabolism. Only a breath or blood test measures BAC.

How long does it take for BAC to reach zero?expand_more

The body removes alcohol at roughly 0.015% per hour on average, and nothing — coffee, cold showers or exercise — speeds this up. A BAC of 0.08% takes about 5 hours or more to clear.

What counts as a standard drink?expand_more

In the US, a standard drink contains about 14 grams (0.6 fl oz) of pure alcohol — roughly a 12-oz beer at 5%, a 5-oz glass of wine at 12%, or a 1.5-oz shot of 40% spirits. Craft beers, large pours and cocktails often contain more.
