What is BMI?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a number calculated from a person's weight and height. It is commonly used as a screening measure to group adult weight status. BMI does not directly measure body fat and should be considered alongside other health information.

How to calculate BMI

The metric formula is weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. With imperial measurements, pounds and inches can be converted using the equivalent formula.

BMI = weight(kg) / height(m)²
Imperial inputs are converted automatically before calculating.

BMI categories for adults

For standard adult BMI screening, the commonly used categories are:

BMICategory
Below 18.5Underweight
18.5–24.9Healthy weight
25.0–29.9Overweight
30.0 or aboveObesity

These categories are screening ranges. A BMI result should not be treated as a diagnosis or as a complete assessment of an individual's health.

What does your BMI result mean?

A BMI result can help provide a quick starting point for discussing weight and health. It does not account for muscle mass, body composition, bone density, fat distribution, medical history, physical activity, sleep, or laboratory findings. Someone with substantial muscle mass, for example, may have a higher BMI without having the same body-fat pattern as another person with the same BMI.

When BMI may need extra context

BMI is not interpreted the same way for every population. Children and teenagers require age- and sex-specific BMI-for-age assessment rather than adult BMI categories. Pregnancy, eating disorders, conditions affecting height, and some other circumstances also require appropriate clinical guidance.

For adults, waist circumference and other health measures can provide additional information about body-fat distribution and health risk. BMI should therefore be considered as one measurement rather than a standalone health verdict.

Worked BMI example

For an adult who is 5'7" (67 inches) and weighs 160 lb, the imperial calculation is (160 ÷ 67²) × 703, which is approximately 25.1. That result falls in the standard adult overweight screening category.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI?

For standard adult screening, a BMI from 18.5 to 24.9 is commonly classified as healthy weight. This range is not a guarantee of health and does not replace an individual assessment.

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

No. BMI is a useful population and screening measure, but it cannot distinguish muscle from fat or show where fat is stored. It should be interpreted with other relevant health information.

Can I use this BMI calculator for children?

No. This page uses adult BMI categories. Children and teenagers need BMI-for-age assessment using age- and sex-specific growth references.

Does BMI diagnose obesity or another disease?

No. BMI is a screening measure. A healthcare professional can consider BMI together with medical history, physical findings, health behaviours and other relevant measurements.

How is the healthy-weight range calculated?

For a given height, the calculator applies the standard adult BMI range of 18.5 to 24.9 to estimate the corresponding weight range. It is an estimate based on BMI, not a personalized target weight.

Formula used

Standard BMI equation: weight(kg) ÷ height(m)², using the World Health Organization's adult screening categories (see table above). Imperial inputs are converted automatically before calculating.

Limitations

  • Does not distinguish muscle mass from fat mass, or show where fat is distributed in the body
  • Not appropriate for children, teenagers, or pregnant individuals — these groups need age/sex-specific or clinical reference ranges
  • Does not account for bone density, frame size, sex-based body composition differences, or ethnicity-specific risk thresholds
  • A screening measure only — not a diagnosis of any health condition

When to seek professional advice

If your BMI falls outside the healthy-weight range, or if you have concerns about your weight, body composition, or related health risks, discuss your individual circumstances with a doctor, registered dietitian, or other qualified healthcare professional — they can consider BMI alongside your medical history and other measurements.
