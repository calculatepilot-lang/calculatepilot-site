How this calculator works

The planner uses a simplified static approximation that about 3,500 kcal equals one pound of body weight (about 7,700 kcal per kg). It is useful for showing the arithmetic behind a target, but it does not predict an individual’s actual weight trajectory:

  • By rate: daily deficit = weekly loss × 3,500 ÷ 7; daily target = TDEE − deficit; weeks = amount to lose ÷ weekly loss.
  • By goal date: weekly loss needed = amount to lose ÷ weeks available; the deficit and daily target follow from that.

For context, the 2013 AHA/ACC/TOS adult overweight and obesity guideline discusses a 500–750 kcal/day deficit and calorie ranges of roughly 1,200–1,500 kcal/day for women and 1,500–1,800 kcal/day for men within structured treatment approaches. These are not universal safe minimums or individualized targets. This planner flags estimated targets below 1,200 kcal/day for women or 1,500 kcal/day for men, and rates above about 1% of body weight per week, as prompts for caution—not as a diagnosis or personal prescription.

A worked example

Maintenance of 2,200 kcal, 180 lb now, 160 lb goal, 1 lb a week: deficit = 1 × 3,500 ÷ 7 = 500 kcal, so the target is 1,700 kcal a day, and 20 lb at 1 lb a week takes about 20 weeks. A 10-week goal would mathematically require 2 lb a week and a 1,000 kcal daily deficit, producing a 1,200 kcal target. That is an aggressive scenario; this calculator cannot determine whether it is appropriate for an individual.

Why the real timeline is usually longer

The 3,500 kcal rule is a useful planning tool, but it overestimates long-term loss: as you get lighter, your maintenance calories fall, so a fixed intake produces a shrinking deficit. Research by Hall and colleagues at the NIH showed weight loss slows over time for this reason. Recalculate every few weeks with an updated TDEE, or use our Calorie Calculator, which projects your weight week by week as maintenance changes.

The CDC notes that people who lose weight gradually and steadily — about 1 to 2 pounds a week — are more likely to keep it off.

Frequently asked questions

Why 3,500 calories per pound of fat?expand_more

It's a simplified static approximation used for arithmetic illustrations. It does not capture all changes in energy expenditure, body composition, water or adherence, so it should not be treated as a forecast of an individual's results.

Should I recalculate as I lose weight?expand_more

Yes — as your weight decreases, your maintenance calories typically decrease too, so recalculating every few weeks keeps your target accurate rather than relying on a calculation made at your starting weight.

Is there one safe calorie minimum for everyone?expand_more

No single calorie minimum is appropriate for every adult. The 2013 AHA/ACC/TOS guideline discusses calorie ranges in the context of adult overweight and obesity treatment; those values are not universal safe minima or individualized prescriptions. This planner uses 1,200 kcal/day and 1,500 kcal/day as caution flags. Very-low-calorie diets require qualified clinical supervision.
