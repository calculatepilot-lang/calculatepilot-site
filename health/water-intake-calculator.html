Where this guideline comes from

This calculator uses a common rule of thumb: roughly half your body weight in ounces as a daily baseline, with additional water added for exercise — about 12 ounces per 30 minutes of activity to replace fluid lost through sweat.

This is a general guideline rather than a precise medical recommendation — actual fluid needs vary with climate, individual sweat rate, diet (foods with high water content contribute too), and overall health, including certain medical conditions that require different fluid management.

Signs you may need more water

Thirst is a reasonable but imperfect indicator — by the time you feel thirsty, mild dehydration may already be underway. Urine color is often a more immediate signal: pale yellow generally indicates good hydration, while darker yellow suggests you may need more fluids.

Hot climates, high altitude, illness (particularly fever, vomiting, or diarrhea), and pregnancy or breastfeeding all increase fluid needs beyond a standard baseline calculation.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include water from food and other beverages?

This calculator estimates total water needs, which can be met through a combination of plain water, other beverages, and water-rich foods — not exclusively from drinking water alone.

Can I drink too much water?

Yes, though it's uncommon — drinking excessive amounts of water in a short period can dilute sodium levels in the blood, a condition called hyponatremia. This is generally only a concern with very large volumes far beyond typical daily recommendations.
