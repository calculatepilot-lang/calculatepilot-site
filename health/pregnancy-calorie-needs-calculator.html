Where these trimester guidelines come from

This calculator applies commonly cited guidelines (based on ACOG recommendations for a singleton pregnancy with a starting normal pre-pregnancy weight): no significant increase in the first trimester, roughly 340 additional calories per day in the second trimester, and roughly 450 additional calories per day in the third trimester.

These are general guidelines, not personalized medical advice — actual needs vary based on your pre-pregnancy weight and BMI, activity level, whether you're carrying multiples, and any pregnancy-specific health conditions. Always follow guidance from your own healthcare provider, who can account for your complete individual situation.

Quality matters alongside quantity

The additional calories needed during pregnancy are relatively modest compared to popular myths about 'eating for two' — the emphasis in most clinical guidance is on nutrient-dense foods (protein, iron, folate, calcium) rather than simply eating significantly more overall.

This calculator estimates calorie quantity only — it doesn't address the specific nutrient needs that become especially important during pregnancy, which are generally best discussed with an OB-GYN or a registered dietitian familiar with your individual health history.

Frequently asked questions

Do I really need to 'eat for two' during pregnancy?

Not in the way that phrase suggests — the actual additional calorie need is relatively modest (roughly 340-450 extra calories per day in the second and third trimesters), with an emphasis on nutrient quality rather than simply doubling food intake.

Do these guidelines apply to a twin pregnancy?

No — this calculator reflects singleton pregnancy guidelines. Multiple pregnancies typically require higher calorie and nutrient adjustments, which should be discussed directly with your healthcare provider.

How many extra calories do you need when pregnant?

According to IOM guidelines: no extra calories in the first trimester, approximately 340 extra calories per day in the second trimester, and approximately 450 extra calories per day in the third trimester, on top of your pre-pregnancy maintenance calories.

How many calories should a pregnant woman eat per day?

Most pregnant women need 1,800-2,400 calories per day depending on their pre-pregnancy weight, activity level, and trimester. This calculator gives a personalised estimate based on your specific inputs rather than a generic number.
