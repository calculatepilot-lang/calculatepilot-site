How this calculator works

Method 1 — height ÷ wrist ratio (r). Divide your height by your wrist circumference (same unit), then classify (Grant, 1980):

  • Men: r above 10.4 = small · 9.6–10.4 = medium · below 9.6 = large
  • Women: r above 11.0 = small · 10.1–11.0 = medium · below 10.1 = large

Method 2 — MedlinePlus wrist chart (U.S. National Library of Medicine), by height band:

  • Women under 5′2″: small under 5.5″ · medium 5.5–5.75″ · large over 5.75″
  • Women 5′2″–5′5″: small under 6″ · medium 6–6.25″ · large over 6.25″
  • Women over 5′5″: small under 6.25″ · medium 6.25–6.5″ · large over 6.5″
  • Men over 5′5″: small 5.5–6.5″ · medium 6.5–7.5″ · large over 7.5″ (the chart doesn't cover men 5′5″ and under)

The ideal-weight panel uses the Hamwi formula with the common ±10% frame adjustment, as a reference only.

Why frame size matters for weight goals

Ideal weight charts and BMI categories are based on averages that don't account for skeletal frame size — someone with a larger bone structure will naturally weigh more at the same body fat percentage than someone with a smaller frame. That's why frame size is sometimes used to adjust weight targets.

A worked example

A woman 5′5″ (65 in) tall with a 6.25″ wrist: 65 ÷ 6.25 = 10.4, which is a medium frame for women (10.1–11.0). On the MedlinePlus chart she falls in the 5′2″–5′5″ band, where 6–6.25″ is also medium, so both methods agree.

Measuring your wrist correctly

Measure around the wrist at the narrowest point, just below the wrist bone, using a flexible tape measure snug but not compressing the skin. Elbow breadth, measured with calipers, is another frame-size method used in research, but it needs equipment most people don't have. Like other simple anthropometric methods, the wrist method is a general estimate, not a clinical measurement.

The ratio method scales with height. For someone 170 cm tall, the cut-offs work out to these wrist sizes:

How frame size shifts a reference ideal weight

The calculator’s ideal-weight panel starts from the Hamwi formula (men 106 lb for 5 ft plus 6 lb per extra inch; women 100 lb plus 5 lb per inch) and then applies the common ±10% frame adjustment.

ExampleSmall frame (−10%)Medium frameLarge frame (+10%)
Man, 5′10″149.4 lb (67.8 kg)166 lb (75.3 kg)182.6 lb (82.8 kg)
Woman, 5′5″112.5 lb (51.0 kg)125 lb (56.7 kg)137.5 lb (62.4 kg)

These are reference points, not goals. Body composition, age and health matter far more than a single formula.

Frame size, body shape and body type are different things

  • Frame size describes skeletal build (small, medium or large) relative to height.
  • Body shape describes how weight is distributed, for example through waist and hip measurements.
  • Somatotypes such as ectomorph, mesomorph and endomorph are an older, informal way of describing physique and are not the same as this wrist-based measure.

Adult frame size is fairly stable. Gaining or losing weight or muscle changes your body composition, but your bones, and so your wrist measurement, stay close to the same.

Frequently asked questions

Does frame size change how much I should weigh?expand_more

It's one factor some ideal-weight frameworks consider — a larger frame generally supports a somewhat higher healthy weight than a smaller frame at the same height, commonly by about 10% either way, though it's just one of many relevant factors.

Where exactly should I measure my wrist?expand_more

At the narrowest point of the wrist, just below the wrist bone — roughly where a watch strap would sit — using a flexible tape measure snug against the skin.

Why do the two methods sometimes disagree?expand_more

The ratio method scales smoothly with height, while the MedlinePlus chart uses broad height bands with fixed wrist ranges. Near a band edge they can land in neighbouring categories; if they differ, you're likely near the border between the two sizes.

What wrist size is a small, medium or large frame?expand_more

It depends on your sex and height. At 170 cm, a man is roughly small under 16.3 cm, medium to 17.7 cm and large above that; a woman is roughly small under 15.5 cm, medium to 16.8 cm and large above that. The calculator shows the exact bands for your own height.

Can my frame size change?expand_more

Not meaningfully once you are fully grown. Weight, fat and muscle change, but your skeletal frame, and therefore your wrist measurement, stays close to the same.

Is frame size the same as body shape or body type?expand_more

No. Frame size is about skeletal build relative to height. Body shape is about how weight is distributed, and somatotypes such as ectomorph or endomorph are an older informal way of describing physique.

Does frame size affect BMI?expand_more

BMI only uses height and weight, so it does not adjust for frame size. Frame size is sometimes used as extra context when judging a weight range, but it does not change your BMI.

What if the ratio and the chart give different frame sizes?expand_more

That usually means you are close to the border between two sizes. The ratio method changes smoothly with height while the chart uses fixed height bands, so near a band edge either label is reasonable.
