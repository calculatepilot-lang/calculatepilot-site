Formulas

How the numbers are worked out.

view_in_ar

Dimensional weight

Volume in cubic inches divided by the divisor your carrier uses.

DIM weight = L × W × H ÷ divisor
balance

Billable weight

The larger of actual and dimensional weight, rounded up to the next whole pound.

billable = ⌈ max(actual, DIM) ⌉
payments

Shipping cost

Billable weight times your rate, plus fuel, residential, insurance and signature charges.

cost = billable × rate × (1 + fuel) + fees

Worked examples

Illustrative figures, each checked by hand. The first is the default setup.

16 × 12 × 10 in, 8.5 lb

Divisor 139, rate $1.55 per billable lb.

16 × 12 × 10 = 1,920 cu in
1,920 ÷ 139 = 13.81 lb
billable = 14 lb, 5.5 lb over actual
14 × 1.55 = $21.70 before fees

Using a shorter box

The same items in a 16 × 12 × 8 in box.

16 × 12 × 8 = 1,536 cu in
1,536 ÷ 139 = 11.05 lb → 12 lb
saves 2 billable lb, or $3.10 at $1.55

Retail divisor 166

The original box at retail rates.

1,920 ÷ 166 = 11.57 lb
billable = 12 lb
a higher divisor means a lower dimensional weight

How to estimate shipping cost

  • Measure the length, width and height of the packed box and weigh it.
  • Enter the divisor your carrier uses for dimensional weight.
  • Enter your carrier rates per billable pound and any surcharges.
  • Compare carriers and check whether a smaller box lowers the billable weight.

Carriers publish their own rate tables, zones, surcharges and dimensional rules, and they change often. The rates here are placeholders that you should replace with your quotes. Always confirm with the carrier or your shipping software.

Frequently asked questions

What is dimensional weight?expand_more

Dimensional (DIM) weight is a size-based weight that carriers use to price bulky, light packages. It is the box volume divided by a divisor, and you are billed for whichever is larger, DIM weight or actual weight.

Which divisor should I use?expand_more

Many carriers use 139 cubic inches per pound for daily or commercial rates and 166 for retail rates. USPS applies dimensional weight only to larger packages. Check your own carrier agreement, or choose a custom divisor.

How can I reduce dimensional weight?expand_more

Use a box that fits the item snugly, cut the tallest dimension first, and avoid extra void fill. The table shows how much a smaller box would save.

Why does the zone matter?expand_more

The farther a package travels, the higher its zone and its rate. Zones are set by the distance between the ZIP codes, so the same parcel costs more to ship across the country than across town.

Do residential deliveries cost more?expand_more

Many carriers add a residential delivery surcharge on top of the base rate. Enter the fee on your rate card, or set it to zero for commercial addresses.
