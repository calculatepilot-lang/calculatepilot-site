Formulas

How the cost is calculated

Monthly commute days

days = days per week × weeks per year ÷ 12

Monthly cost

cost = 2 × distance × days × (fuel price ÷ mpg + wear) + (tolls + parking) × days

Time value

time = 2 × minutes ÷ 60 × days × hourly value
Ownership costs

Fuel is only part of it

Depreciation, insurance, maintenance, tires and registration all rise with the miles you drive or with owning the car at all. A per-mile figure such as the IRS rate bundles these together.

Worked examples

Illustrative commutes, each checked by hand. The first is the default setup.

18.5 mi each way, 5 days

28.5 MPG, $3.55 gas, $0.092 wear, $16.50 tolls and parking.

days = 5 × 48 ÷ 12 = 20
miles = 37 × 20 = 740
fuel = 740 ÷ 28.5 × 3.55 = $92.18
wear = 740 × 0.092 = $68.08
tolls + parking = 16.50 × 20 = $330.00
total = $490.26 a month

Value of time

45 minutes each way at $42 an hour.

90 min × 20 days = 1,800 min = 30 h
30 × 42 = $1,260 a month

Two remote days a week

Same commute, 3 days on site.

per day = 490.26 ÷ 20 = $24.51
days saved = 2 × 48 ÷ 12 = 8
saving = 8 × 24.51 = $196.10 a month

How to work out the cost of a commute

  • Enter the one-way distance, how many days a week you go in, and how many weeks a year you work.
  • Add your vehicle's fuel economy and the local fuel or electricity price.
  • Include tolls and parking, and either a wear cost per mile or the IRS standard rate.
  • Optionally value your time, then compare with a transit pass, rideshare or working from home.

Only fuel, tolls and parking are cash costs each day. Wear, depreciation and time are real but paid over a longer period, which is why the tool shows them separately.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a daily commute cost?expand_more

It depends on distance, vehicle, fuel price, tolls and parking. A 37-mile round trip in a 28.5 MPG car with $3.55 gas costs about $4.60 in fuel per day, more when wear, tolls and parking are included. Enter your numbers above for an exact figure.

Should I use the IRS mileage rate?expand_more

The IRS business rate is designed to cover the full running cost of a vehicle, including depreciation and insurance, so it gives a higher and more complete per-mile figure than fuel alone. Note that it is a tax rate for business travel, and commuting from home to a regular workplace is generally not deductible.

Is my commute time worth counting?expand_more

It is not a cash cost, but it is a real trade-off. Valuing an hour of your time at a fraction of your wage shows what a shorter commute or a remote day is worth. Turn the option off to see cash costs only.

How is an electric vehicle handled?expand_more

Switch to Electric and enter miles per kWh and your price per kWh. Energy cost per mile is price divided by miles per kWh, and wear and other costs work as before.

When is transit cheaper than driving?expand_more

When a monthly pass costs less than your monthly driving cost, including parking and tolls. The comparison panel shows the difference, and parking charges are usually what tips it.
