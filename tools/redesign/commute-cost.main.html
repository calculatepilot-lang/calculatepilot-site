Formulas

How the cost is calculated

Monthly commute days

days = days per week × weeks per year ÷ 12

Monthly cost

cost = 2 × distance × days × (fuel price ÷ mpg + wear) + (tolls + parking) × days

Time value

time = 2 × minutes ÷ 60 × days × hourly value
Ownership costs

Fuel is only part of it

Depreciation, insurance, maintenance, tires and registration all rise with the miles you drive or with owning the car at all. A per-mile figure such as the IRS rate bundles these together.

Worked examples

Illustrative commutes, each checked by hand. The first is the default setup.

18.5 mi each way, 5 days

28.5 MPG, $3.55 gas, $0.092 wear, $16.50 tolls and parking.

days = 5 × 48 ÷ 12 = 20
miles = 37 × 20 = 740
fuel = 740 ÷ 28.5 × 3.55 = $92.18
wear = 740 × 0.092 = $68.08
tolls + parking = 16.50 × 20 = $330.00
total = $490.26 a month

45 minutes each way at $42 an hour.

90 min × 20 days = 1,800 min = 30 h
30 × 42 = $1,260 a month

Two remote days a week

Same commute, 3 days on site.

per day = 490.26 ÷ 20 = $24.51
days saved = 2 × 48 ÷ 12 = 8
saving = 8 × 24.51 = $196.10 a month

How to work out the cost of a commute

  • Enter the one-way distance, how many days a week you go in, and how many weeks a year you work.
  • Add your vehicle's fuel economy and the local fuel or electricity price.
  • Include tolls and parking, and either a wear cost per mile or the IRS standard rate.
  • Optionally value your time, then compare with a transit pass, rideshare or working from home.

Only fuel, tolls and parking are cash costs each day. Wear, depreciation and time are real but paid over a longer period, which is why the tool shows them separately.

Job B pays $6,000 more but costs $2,079 more in fuel and wear, leaving about $3,921 extra a year before tax, tolls and parking, for 240 extra hours on the road. That is about $16.34 for each extra hour, so ask whether the extra pay beats what that time is worth to you.

Is commuting time paid or deductible?

The US Department of Labor's rule at 29 CFR 785.35 says normal travel from home to work is not working time, so it is not paid under federal wage law. The IRS also generally treats the cost of commuting between home and a regular workplace as a personal expense, so it is not deductible for most employees. Travel between work sites during the day is treated differently, so check IRS guidance or a tax adviser.

Pre-tax commuter benefits

If your employer offers qualified transportation benefits, you can set aside pre-tax pay for a transit pass, vanpool or parking. The 2026 federal limit is $340 a month for transit and vanpool combined and $340 a month for parking. As an example, $120 a month of pre-tax transit at a 22% federal income tax rate avoids about $26.40 of federal income tax a month. Benefits depend on your employer, so ask HR.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a daily commute cost?expand_more

It depends on distance, vehicle, fuel price, tolls and parking. A 37-mile round trip in a 28.5 MPG car with $3.55 gas costs about $4.60 in fuel per day, more when wear, tolls and parking are included. Enter your numbers above for an exact figure.

Should I use the IRS mileage rate?expand_more

The IRS business rate is designed to cover the full running cost of a vehicle, including depreciation and insurance, so it gives a higher and more complete per-mile figure than fuel alone. Note that it is a tax rate for business travel, and commuting from home to a regular workplace is generally not deductible.

Is my commute time worth counting?expand_more

It is not a cash cost, but it is a real trade-off. Valuing an hour of your time at a fraction of your wage shows what a shorter commute or a remote day is worth. Turn the option off to see cash costs only.

How is an electric vehicle handled?expand_more

Switch to Electric and enter miles per kWh and your price per kWh. Energy cost per mile is price divided by miles per kWh, and wear and other costs work as before.

When is transit cheaper than driving?expand_more

When a monthly pass costs less than your monthly driving cost, including parking and tolls. The comparison panel shows the difference, and parking charges are usually what tips it.

How do I calculate my commute cost?expand_more

Round-trip miles × days × (fuel price ÷ MPG + wear per mile), plus tolls and parking for each day. At 18.5 miles each way, 28.5 MPG, $3.55 gas and $0.092 wear that is about $160.26 a month before tolls and parking.

How much does it cost to commute 20 miles each way?expand_more

At 28.5 MPG, $3.55 gas and $0.092 wear, fuel and wear come to about $2,079 a year over 240 days. At the IRS rate of 76 cents a mile it is $7,296.

What is the AAA cost per mile to drive?expand_more

AAA's 2026 Your Driving Costs study puts the average new vehicle at $12,863 a year, or about 86 cents a mile at 15,000 miles, including depreciation, insurance and finance charges. Fuel is 17.3 cents of that and maintenance, repairs and tires 11.7 cents.

Should I use the AAA rate, the IRS rate or fuel only?expand_more

Fuel only shows the cash you spend each trip. The IRS rate (76 cents from 1 July 2026) and AAA's all-in figure also count depreciation and insurance, which matter if the commute adds miles to a car you would otherwise barely drive.

How many hours a year do I spend commuting?expand_more

One-way minutes × 8 for a 5-day week over 48 weeks. A 45-minute commute is 360 hours a year, or 9 forty-hour working weeks.

Is commuting time paid?expand_more

Under 29 CFR 785.35, normal travel from home to work is not worktime. Travel between work sites during the day can be treated differently.

Can I deduct commuting costs?expand_more

Generally no. The IRS treats travel between home and a regular workplace as personal. Check IRS guidance or a tax adviser for your situation.

What is the 2026 limit for pre-tax commuter benefits?expand_more

$340 a month for transit and vanpool combined and $340 a month for parking. Your employer must offer the benefit.

Is a long commute worth a higher salary?expand_more

Subtract the extra fuel and wear from the extra pay, then divide by the extra hours. In the example above $6,000 more pay leaves about $3,921 for 240 extra hours, roughly $16.34 an hour.
