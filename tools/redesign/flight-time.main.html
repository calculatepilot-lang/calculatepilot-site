Block time and airborne time

Airline schedules use block time, from pushback at the departure gate to arriving at the arrival gate. It is longer than the time in the air because of taxiing, climbing and approach. Real flights also follow airways, not perfect great-circle arcs, so scheduled times are often a little longer than this estimate.

Formulas

How the numbers are worked out.

public

Great-circle distance

The shortest path between two points on the Earth, by the haversine formula, with Earth radius 6,371 km.

a = sin²(Δφ/2) + cos φ₁ cos φ₂ sin²(Δλ/2)
d = 2R · asin(√a)
speed

Ground speed and time

True airspeed plus the wind along the route. Airborne time is distance over ground speed.

ground speed = TAS + wind
air time = distance ÷ ground speed
schedule

Gate-to-gate and arrival

Airborne time plus taxi and approach minutes. Arrival is shown in the destination time zone on the departure date.

block = air time + buffer
arrival = departure + block

How to estimate flight time

  • Pick the origin and destination airports.
  • Choose an aircraft type and the wind along the route. Eastbound jet-stream tailwinds are commonly 40 to 100 kt.
  • Set the taxi and approach allowance, then enter the departure date and local time.
  • Read the gate-to-gate time, local arrival and time zone change.

Estimates only. Actual flight time depends on the routing, winds aloft, air traffic control and the aircraft. Use the airline's schedule for planning connections.

Frequently asked questions

How is flight time calculated?expand_more

The great-circle distance is divided by ground speed (aircraft speed plus wind along the route). Taxi, climb and approach time is then added to give a gate-to-gate figure.

Why is the flight home longer?expand_more

In the middle latitudes the jet stream blows west to east, so eastbound flights get a tailwind and westbound flights a headwind. Transatlantic westbound trips are often about an hour longer.

What is block time?expand_more

The time from leaving the departure gate to arriving at the arrival gate. It is what airline timetables show.

How long does jet lag last?expand_more

A common rule of thumb is about one day per time zone crossed when travelling east and about two-thirds of a day per zone travelling west. It varies a lot between people.

Why does the arrival day sometimes change?expand_more

Crossing the date line or a long eastbound flight can land on a different calendar day in the destination time zone. For jet lag, a 17 hour clock difference counts as 7 hours the other way round the world.
