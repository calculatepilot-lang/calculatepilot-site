Formulas

ROAS and ACoS

Profit before ads

P = price ÷ (1 + VAT) − costs

Break-even ROAS

price ÷ P = 1 ÷ margin before ads

Break-even ACoS

P ÷ price = 1 ÷ ROAS

Target ROAS

price ÷ (P − target margin × net revenue)
Good to know

Where the number goes wrong

Ad platforms report revenue with VAT included, so working from the net price makes break-even look easier than it is. Returns, discounts and free shipping also cut the profit you keep, so put them in the cost fields.

Break-even ROAS covers per-sale costs only. Fixed costs such as salaries and software need the Break-Even Calculator as well.

Worked examples

Illustrative figures, each checked by hand. The first is the default product.

One product

Price $60 including 20% VAT, product cost $14, shipping $5, 3% payment fee, $1 other.

net revenue = 60 ÷ 1.20 = 50.00
costs = 14 + 5 + 1 + (0.03 × 60 = 1.80) = 21.80
profit before ads = 50 − 21.80 = 28.20
break-even ROAS = 60 ÷ 28.20 = 2.13, ACoS = 47.0%

With a 10% target margin

Same product, keeping 10% of net revenue as profit.

ad budget per sale = 28.20 − (0.10 × 50) = 23.20
target ROAS = 60 ÷ 23.20 = 2.59, ACoS = 38.7%

Two products blended

Product A as above (100 units) and Product B at $30, cost $8, shipping $4, 3% fee (200 units).

Product B profit = 25 − 12.90 = 12.10
revenue = 6,000 + 6,000 = 12,000
profit before ads = 2,820 + 2,420 = 5,240
blended ROAS = 12,000 ÷ 5,240 = 2.29

How to find your break-even ROAS

  • Take the selling price the customer pays, including VAT or sales tax.
  • Remove the tax to get net revenue, then subtract product cost, shipping, payment fees and any other cost per sale.
  • Divide the selling price by the profit that is left. That is your break-even ROAS.
  • Run campaigns above that number. Raise it to the target ROAS if you also want a set profit margin.

If profit before ads is zero or negative, no ROAS breaks even. Raise the price or cut costs first.

ROAS, ACoS and CPA

ROAS is revenue ÷ ad spend. ACoS is ad spend ÷ revenue, so ACoS = 1 ÷ ROAS. Cost per acquisition (CPA) is ad spend ÷ sales, and your break-even CPA equals the profit before ads on one sale, here $28.20.

Frequently asked questions

What is break-even ROAS?expand_more

It is the return on ad spend at which an ad earns back exactly what it cost. Break-even ROAS = selling price ÷ profit per sale before ads, which is the same as 1 ÷ the profit margin before ads. A product with a $60 price and $28.20 profit before ads breaks even at a ROAS of 2.13.

How do I calculate break-even ROAS?expand_more

Work out the profit per sale before ads: net revenue after VAT, minus product cost, shipping, payment fees and other costs. Then divide the selling price by that profit. If a $60 sale leaves $28.20 before ads, break-even ROAS is 60 ÷ 28.20 = 2.13.

What is break-even ACoS and how does it relate to ROAS?expand_more

ACoS (advertising cost of sales) is ad spend divided by sales, so it is 1 ÷ ROAS. Break-even ACoS is the highest ACoS you can run without losing money. A break-even ROAS of 2.13 is a break-even ACoS of 47.0%.

Should the selling price include VAT?expand_more

Yes, enter the price the customer pays, because ad platforms report revenue with VAT or sales tax included. The calculator removes the tax before working out your profit, so your ROAS target is not overstated. Set the VAT rate to 0 if you do not charge tax.

What is a good ROAS?expand_more

There is no single good number, because it depends on your margin. A 20% margin before ads needs ROAS 5.00 just to break even, while a 50% margin needs only 2.00. A good ROAS is one comfortably above your own break-even ROAS.

How is target ROAS different from break-even ROAS?expand_more

Break-even ROAS only covers costs. Target ROAS also leaves the profit you want: it takes the profit before ads, subtracts your target margin on net revenue, and divides the price by what is left for ad spend. A 10% target margin raises the example product from ROAS 2.13 to 2.59.

How do I work out ROAS for several products?expand_more

Add each product with its own price, costs and units sold. The calculator blends them by units, so the combined ROAS is total revenue ÷ total profit before ads. Use it for a campaign that promotes a mix of products, then check each product's own ROAS in the table.

Do returns and refunds change break-even ROAS?expand_more

They do. Returns lower the revenue you keep, so the real profit per sale is smaller than the listed price suggests. Add the average return cost per sale to the Other cost per unit field to include it.

Does a ROAS above break-even always mean the ads are worth running?expand_more

Not always. It means each sale covers its own costs and ad spend, but it ignores fixed costs such as salaries, software and rent. Use the Break-Even Calculator to see how many sales you need to cover those as well.
