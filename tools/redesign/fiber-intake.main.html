How this calculator works

The calorie-based target is 14 g of fiber per 1,000 kcal you eat — the basis the Institute of Medicine (now the National Academies) used to set the Adequate Intake, and the figure used in the 2020–2025 Dietary Guidelines for Americans. The 2025–2030 edition advises prioritizing fiber-rich whole grains, vegetables and fruit without setting a gram target. The age and sex reference is the IOM Adequate Intake itself:

The two figures are usually close for someone eating a typical amount for their age and sex, but diverge if you eat notably more or less — the calorie-based figure moves with your intake, the reference does not. The UK's SACN recommends 30 g a day for adults.

A worked example

A 35-year-old woman eating 2,000 kcal a day: 2,000 ÷ 1,000 × 14 = 28 g of fiber. Her IOM reference is 25 g. If she currently eats 15 g, she has a 13 g gap — about one cup of cooked lentils would close it on its own.

Why most people fall short — and how to close the gap

Most people in Western diets eat well below these targets, largely because of low intake of whole grains, legumes, fruits and vegetables. Increase fiber gradually over a few weeks rather than all at once, and drink enough water alongside it, to help avoid bloating and gas. A varied mix of beans and lentils, whole grains, fruit with edible skins, vegetables, nuts and seeds works better than relying on one high-fiber food.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I get two different fiber numbers?expand_more

The calorie-based figure (14 g per 1,000 calories) scales with your actual intake, while the IOM reference is a population guideline by age and sex that doesn't account for individual calorie needs — they're usually similar but can differ for atypical calorie intakes.

What happens if I increase fiber too quickly?expand_more

A sudden large increase can cause digestive discomfort like bloating or gas. Increasing gradually over a few weeks, along with adequate water, generally helps your digestive system adjust.

Can I get too much fiber?expand_more

The IOM did not set an upper limit for fiber from food. Very high intakes can cause discomfort, and people with some digestive conditions may be advised to limit fiber, so follow any guidance from your doctor or dietitian.
