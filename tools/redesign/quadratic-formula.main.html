    Projectile height

    A ball is thrown up at 20 m/s from 1.5 m: h(t) = −4.9t² + 20t + 1.5. When does it land, and how high does it go?

    Δ = 20² − 4(−4.9)(1.5) = 429.4

    peak at t = 20 ÷ 9.8 = 2.041 s, height 21.908 m

    What is the quadratic formula?

    Any equation ax² + bx + c = 0 with a ≠ 0 has solutions x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ 2a. It comes from completing the square on the general equation. The expression under the root, Δ = b² − 4ac, is the discriminant.

    The parabola behind the equation

    • Vertex: (h, k) with h = −b ÷ 2a and k = c − b² ÷ 4a. It is the minimum if a > 0 and the maximum if a < 0.
    • Axis of symmetry: x = h; the roots sit symmetrically on either side of it.
    • Focus and directrix: (h, k + 1 ÷ 4a) and y = k − 1 ÷ 4a.
    • Forms: standard ax² + bx + c; factored a(x − x₁)(x − x₂); vertex a(x − h)² + k.

    Frequently asked questions

    What happens if a = 0?expand_more

    The equation is no longer quadratic: it becomes bx + c = 0, a linear equation with the single solution x = −c ÷ b (if b ≠ 0). The quadratic formula divides by 2a, so it cannot be used. This calculator tells you the linear solution instead.

    How do complex roots appear on a graph?expand_more

    When Δ < 0 the parabola does not touch the x-axis, so there are no real roots. The complex roots p ± qi have the real part p = −b ÷ 2a, which is the axis of symmetry, and the imaginary part q = √(−Δ) ÷ 2a, which measures how far the vertex is from the axis.

    Why is the vertex’s x-coordinate the midpoint of the roots?expand_more

    The two roots are −b ÷ 2a ± √Δ ÷ 2a, so their average is −b ÷ 2a — exactly the vertex’s x-coordinate. A parabola is symmetric about its axis.

    What is the sum and product of the roots?expand_more

    By Vieta’s formulas, x₁ + x₂ = −b ÷ a and x₁ · x₂ = c ÷ a. They let you check your answers: for x² − 5x + 6 the roots 2 and 3 add to 5 and multiply to 6.

    When should I use factoring or completing the square instead?expand_more

    Factoring is quickest when the roots are integers or simple fractions. Completing the square derives the formula and gives the vertex form. The quadratic formula always works, whatever the coefficients.

    Can the coefficients be decimals or fractions?expand_more

    Yes. Enter decimals directly. Exact radical forms are shown when the coefficients are integers; otherwise you get precise decimal roots.
