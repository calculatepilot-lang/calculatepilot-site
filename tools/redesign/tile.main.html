Grout coverage guide

Square feet covered by one 25 lb bag of grout at 3/8 in tile thickness, from the same formula as the calculator including a 25% allowance for waste. Bag labels vary by brand.

Tile size1/16 in joint1/8 in joint3/16 in joint1/4 in jointTypical notch

Large-format tile (any edge 15 in or more) needs a larger notch, a flat substrate and careful lippage control. Follow the tile and mortar manufacturer's instructions.

Formulas

How the estimate is worked out

Tiles

tiles = ⌈ area ÷ ((L + j)(W + j) ÷ 144) × (1 + waste) ⌉

Grout

lb = area × 144(L + W)·T·j ÷ (L·W) ÷ 1728 × 112 × 1.25

Thin-set

bags = ⌈ area × (1 + waste) ÷ coverage per 50 lb bag ⌉

L and W are the tile length and width in inches, T is thickness and j is the joint width. 112 lb/ft³ is a typical density for packed sanded grout.

Layout

Waste by pattern

Straight grid · 10% — few cuts along the walls.
Running bond · 12% — offset joints add end cuts.
Diagonal or herringbone · 18% — many angled cuts at the edges.
Complex mosaic · 20% — curves and heavy nipping.

Keep a few spare tiles for future repairs, since later batches may differ in shade.

How many tiles do I need?

  • Measure the area to be tiled, or enter a known total in square feet.
  • Enter the tile size and the grout joint, since the joint slightly increases the space each tile covers.
  • Choose your layout. Diagonal and herringbone patterns waste more than a straight grid.
  • Round up to whole boxes and buy from a single lot for consistent colour.

This is an estimate. Uneven floors, walls with many niches, and patterns that need matching can all raise waste, so ask your installer for a final count.

Frequently asked questions

How much extra tile should I buy?expand_more

Ten percent is the usual minimum for a straight layout. Add more for diagonal, herringbone or mosaic patterns, and for rooms with many corners or obstacles.

How much grout do I need?expand_more

It depends on tile size, thickness and joint width. Small tiles and wide joints need much more grout than large tiles with narrow joints. This tool works from the joint volume and adds 25% for waste and uneven surfaces.

Sanded or unsanded grout?expand_more

Sanded grout is normally used for joints of 1/8 inch and wider because it resists shrinking and cracking. Unsanded grout suits narrower joints and soft or polished stone that sand could scratch. Epoxy grout resists stains and chemicals.

What trowel notch should I use?expand_more

Small tiles usually take a 1/4 in square notch, mid-size tiles a 1/4 × 3/8 in notch, and large-format tiles a 1/2 in square notch or larger. Always follow the mortar bag instructions and check for full coverage under the tile.

Why does tile thickness matter?expand_more

Thicker tile has deeper joints, so it takes more grout. The tile-and-joint size also changes how many tiles fit in an area.
