Formulas

How the cost is calculated

Energy

kWh per day = watts × hours ÷ 1,000

Cost

cost = kWh per day × days × rate

Carbon

kg CO₂ = kWh × emissions factor

If only volts and amps are listed, watts = volts × amps. For example, 120 V × 1.5 A = 180 W.

Standby power

Vampire loads

Devices in standby can add roughly 5% to 10% of a home's electricity use. At your rate, a device idling all year costs:

2.5 W (smart TV)—per year
8 W (set-top box)—per year
15 W (console or AV)—per year
1 W (charger)—per year

Smart power strips, sleep modes and unplugging chargers cut standby waste.

How to work out what an appliance costs

  • Find the power in watts on the label, or multiply volts by amps.
  • Estimate hours of use per day and days per month.
  • Find your electricity rate per kWh on your bill.
  • Multiply energy by the rate. Use a plug-in power meter for the most accurate reading.

Your bill may also include fixed charges and tiered or time-of-use rates, so the true cost of an extra appliance can differ from a flat-rate estimate.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find my appliance's wattage?expand_more

Check the nameplate on the back or underside. If it lists volts and amps, multiply them to get watts. For devices that cycle, such as refrigerators and air conditioners, a plug-in power meter shows real use over time.

What is the difference between peak and off-peak rates?expand_more

Time-of-use plans charge more when demand is high, often late afternoon and evening, and less overnight. Running an EV charger, dishwasher or dryer off-peak can cut its cost noticeably if your plan has these tiers.

How is carbon output estimated?expand_more

Energy in kWh is multiplied by an emissions factor for the grid. The US average is roughly 0.37 kg of CO₂ per kWh, while the marginal or non-baseload rate is higher at about 0.5 kg. Cleaner grids are much lower. Edit the factor to match your area.

Why does a 1,500 W heater not use 1.5 kWh every hour?expand_more

Thermostats switch the heating element off once the room reaches the target temperature. If it runs 30 minutes in an hour, it uses 0.75 kWh. The calculator assumes constant use unless you reduce the hours.

What is a kilowatt-hour?expand_more

A kilowatt-hour is the energy used by a 1,000 watt device running for one hour. It is the unit your electricity bill is based on.
