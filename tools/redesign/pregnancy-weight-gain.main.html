How this calculator works

Pre-pregnancy BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². The Institute of Medicine (IOM) 2009 guidelines, endorsed by ACOG, then set the total gain:

A worked example

At 5′5″ (1.651 m) and 140 lb (63.5 kg) before pregnancy, BMI = 63.5 ÷ 1.651² = 23.3 — normal weight — so the recommended total gain is 25–35 lb, a final weight of about 165–175 lb. At 20 weeks, the path suggests a gain of roughly 7–12 lb so far.

Why it matters

Gaining within the recommended range is linked with fewer complications for mother and baby. Gaining too much raises the chance of gestational diabetes, a large baby and keeping weight on after birth; too little is linked with preterm birth and low birth weight. The IOM did not set a twin range for underweight women because of limited evidence. This is information, not medical advice — your care team may set a different target for you.

Frequently asked questions

How much weight should I gain in the first trimester?expand_more

The IOM report assumes a small first-trimester gain of about 1.1–4.4 lb (0.5–2 kg). Most of the gain comes in the second and third trimesters.

Why are twin ranges higher?expand_more

Carrying two babies needs more weight gain to support their growth. The IOM twin ranges are provisional, and no range was set for underweight women because the evidence was too limited.

What if I'm gaining more or less than the range?expand_more

Weight gain varies week to week. If you're consistently above or below the range, talk to your care team — they can look at your diet, activity and health and suggest changes safely.
