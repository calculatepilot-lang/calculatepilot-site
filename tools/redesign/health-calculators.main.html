Frequently asked questions

Why does BMI have different cut-offs for Asian populations?expand_more

A 2004 WHO expert consultation found that many Asian populations have a higher proportion of body fat and higher risk of type 2 diabetes and heart disease at a lower BMI than European populations. It kept the international categories but proposed additional public-health action points of 23 (increased risk) and 27.5 (high risk) for Asian populations.

Why check waist-to-height ratio as well as BMI?expand_more

BMI can't tell muscle from fat or show where fat is stored. Waist-to-height ratio reflects fat around the middle, which is more closely linked to heart and metabolic risk. A common guide is to keep your waist to less than half your height (a ratio below 0.5).

Is the 3,500-calorie rule accurate for weight loss?expand_more

Only as a rough starting point. The idea that a 3,500-calorie deficit always removes one pound of fat assumes your energy use stays constant, but it falls as you lose weight, so loss usually slows over time. Treat any weight-loss timeline as an estimate and adjust it from your actual progress.

Do these calculators store my health data?expand_more

No. The calculations run in your browser and the numbers you enter aren't sent to or stored on our servers. Like most websites, the site itself uses analytics and advertising cookies — see our privacy policy for details.

Are these calculators medical advice?expand_more

No. They use published formulas to give estimates for general information. Results can be less accurate for children, pregnant women, older adults and athletes; talk to a doctor or registered dietitian about decisions for your health.
