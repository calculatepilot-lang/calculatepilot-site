    What is a sequence?

    A sequence is an ordered list of numbers a₁, a₂, a₃, … generated by a rule. An arithmetic sequence adds a fixed amount each step; a geometric sequence multiplies by a fixed ratio; Fibonacci and Lucas numbers are defined recursively, each term the sum of the two before it.

    Frequently asked questions

    What's the difference between a sequence and a series?expand_more

    A sequence is the ordered list of terms itself (a₁, a₂, a₃, …). A series is the sum of those terms (a₁ + a₂ + a₃ + … + aₙ). This calculator's "partial sum" is the series formed from the first n terms of the sequence.

    When does a geometric series converge as n → ∞?expand_more

    Exactly when |r| < 1. The infinite sum then equals a₁ / (1 − r). If |r| ≥ 1, the terms don't shrink to zero and the sum diverges (grows without bound, or oscillates).

    How are huge Fibonacci numbers computed exactly?expand_more

    By fast doubling: identities like F(2k) = F(k)·[2F(k+1) − F(k)] let you compute F(n) in about log₂(n) big-integer steps instead of n additions, and BigInt arithmetic keeps every digit exact, however large.

    What is the golden ratio's connection to Fibonacci numbers?expand_more

    The ratio of consecutive Fibonacci numbers Fₙ₊₁/Fₙ converges to the golden ratio φ = (1+√5)/2 ≈ 1.6180339887 as n grows — this calculator shows how quickly that ratio settles.

    What does a "custom recurrence" mean here?expand_more

    A second-order linear recurrence aₙ = p·aₙ₋₁ + q·aₙ₋₂, defined by two seed values and two coefficients. Fibonacci (p=q=1, seeds 0,1) and Lucas (p=q=1, seeds 2,1) are special cases; changing p, q or the seeds produces other well-known families, such as the Pell numbers (p=2, q=1).

    Can n be very large?expand_more

    Yes — arithmetic and geometric terms compute instantly for any n; Fibonacci, Lucas and custom recurrences use exact BigInt arithmetic up to n = 5000, computed with fast doubling or matrix exponentiation rather than a slow term-by-term loop.
