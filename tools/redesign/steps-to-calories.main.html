How this calculator works

  • Distance = steps × stride length. Without a measured stride, it uses the common rule of thumb of stride ≈ 41.4% of height.
  • Time = distance ÷ walking speed (2.5, 3.1, 3.7 or 4.2 mph, the middle of each pace band).
  • Calories = MET × 3.5 × weight (kg) ÷ 200 × minutes, with walking METs from the 2024 Adult Compendium: 3.0 (2.5 mph), 3.8 (2.8–3.4 mph), 4.8 (3.5–3.9 mph) and 5.5 (4.0–4.4 mph). Net calories use MET − 1.

To measure your stride, walk 10 normal steps on a flat surface, measure the distance and divide by 10.

A worked example

10,000 steps for someone 5′8″ (68 in) weighing 170 lb (77.1 kg) at a moderate pace: stride ≈ 68 × 0.414 = 28.2 in, so distance ≈ 4.44 miles. At 3.1 mph that's about 86 minutes, and 3.8 × 3.5 × 77.1 ÷ 200 × 86 ≈ 441 kcal — about 325 kcal above what they'd burn resting.

How many steps a day?

The 10,000-steps goal began as a 1960s Japanese pedometer marketing campaign rather than a clinical study. A 2022 meta-analysis of 15 studies (Paluch et al., Lancet Public Health) found risk of death fell as daily steps rose, levelling off at about 6,000–8,000 steps a day for adults 60 and older and 8,000–10,000 for younger adults. Any increase from a low baseline helps.

Frequently asked questions

How many calories does 10,000 steps burn?expand_more

For a typical adult, roughly 300–500 calories, depending mostly on body weight, stride length and pace. Heavier people and faster walkers burn more.

Why does walking speed change the calories per step?expand_more

Faster walking uses more energy per minute (a higher MET) but covers the same steps in less time. Overall, brisk walking burns somewhat more per step than strolling, and uphill walking more again.

How long is 10,000 steps?expand_more

For most adults it's roughly 4–5 miles (6.5–8 km), taking about 1.5 hours at a moderate pace — spread over a whole day for most people.
