Mathematical Formula & Methodology

Standard residential amortization uses an ordinary annuity present value model where the periodic payment is calibrated so the remaining debt equals exactly zero at term maturity:

M = P · [ r(1 + r)n ] / [ (1 + r)n − 1 ]
M: Monthly Payment
P: Principal Amount
r: Periodic Rate (Rate/12)
n: Total Months (Term × 12)

Lender disclosures are governed by the Truth in Lending Act (Regulation Z, 12 CFR Part 1026) and will include fees this estimate leaves out. Escrow amounts are held constant; real property tax and insurance bills usually rise over time.

How this calculator works

This calculator uses the standard fixed-rate amortization formula for principal and interest, then adds property tax, home insurance, HOA dues and PMI so you see the full housing cost — not only the loan payment. The loan amount is the home price minus your down payment. PMI is estimated at your chosen rate (0.50% of the loan per year by default) only when you put down less than 20%, and it drops off automatically once the balance reaches 78% of the original home value. Extra principal payments are applied every month and shorten the loan.

How to use this mortgage calculator

  1. Enter the price and down payment. Enter the home purchase price and your down payment. The loan amount updates as you type.
  2. Set the rate and loan term. Enter the interest rate and choose a 30-year, 15-year or 5/1 ARM loan.
  3. Add taxes, insurance, HOA and PMI. Enter annual property tax, annual homeowners insurance, monthly HOA dues, and PMI if your down payment is under 20%.
  4. Try an extra payment. Enter a monthly extra principal payment to see the payoff time and interest saved.
  5. Read the payment and schedule. Read your total monthly payment and the full amortization schedule below the inputs.

What goes into a monthly mortgage payment

Most lenders quote PITI: principal, interest, taxes, and insurance.

  • Principal — the portion of each payment that reduces the outstanding balance.
  • Interest — the cost of borrowing. Early payments on a 30-year loan are mostly interest.
  • Property tax — county assessments, usually collected monthly into escrow.
  • Homeowners insurance — hazard coverage, often escrowed with taxes.
  • PMI — required on conventional loans below 20% down, typically 0.3–1.5% of the loan per year.
  • HOA dues — set by the association, not the lender.

Biweekly payment power: 26 half-payments a year equal 13 full payments and can shorten a 30-year loan by about 4–6 years. You can model the same effect by entering one-twelfth of a payment as extra principal.

Under the federal Homeowners Protection Act, you can ask your servicer to cancel PMI when the scheduled balance reaches 80% of the original value (with a good payment record and no junior liens), it ends automatically at 78% if you are current, and it must end after the midpoint of the loan term. FHA and VA loans follow different rules. Extra principal payments bring the date forward.

What biweekly payments save

Paying half the monthly payment every two weeks makes 26 half-payments, or 13 full payments, a year. On the $320,000 loan at 6.5%, this pays the loan off in about 24.2 years instead of 30 and cuts total interest from $408,142 to about $314,146, saving about $93,996. Confirm your servicer applies each half-payment promptly and does not charge a fee.

Stress-testing a 5/1 ARM

Suppose a 5/1 ARM on the same $320,000 starts at 5.75%, then resets to 7.75% after year 5 (the calculator models one reset). Payment in the first five years: $1,867.43. The balance after 60 payments is about $296,839, and the payment then rises to about $2,242.11, which is $374.68 more. Compared with a 6.5% fixed loan ($2,022.62), the ARM is cheaper for five years but would cost more afterwards in this scenario. Test your own reset rate in the ARM option.

Why a fixed-rate payment can still rise

Principal and interest stay fixed, but taxes and insurance usually do not. Starting from $4,200 of tax and $1,400 of insurance, and assuming tax grows 3% a year and insurance 5% a year (assumptions, not forecasts):

YearTax + insurance per month
1$466.67

The calculator holds these constant, so treat your result as the first-year payment.

Costs the calculator leaves out

  • Closing costs: paid once at purchase. Your lender's Loan Estimate lists them.
  • Maintenance and repairs: a common rule of thumb is to budget about 1% of the home's value a year.
  • Utilities, moving and furnishing.

Before you pay the mortgage off early

  • Prepayment penalty: check your loan documents; some loans charge one for early payoff.
  • Opportunity cost and liquidity: money in the house is harder to reach in an emergency, and it may earn more elsewhere. See extra payments vs investing.
  • Tax deduction: mortgage interest is deductible only if you itemize, and under IRS Publication 936 (2025 edition) the limit for debt taken on after 15 December 2017 is $750,000 ($375,000 if married filing separately). Check the rules for the year you file.

Key takeaways

  • Your monthly mortgage payment with taxes and PMI is principal, interest, property tax, insurance, HOA dues and PMI if you put less than 20% down.
  • On the default example ($450,000 home, 20% down, 6.75% for 30 years), the payment is $2,801.62 a month with no PMI.
  • A higher rate, a lower down payment and a longer term all raise the payment or the total interest. Extra principal shortens the loan.
  • The result is an estimate, not a lender quote. Closing costs and maintenance are not included.

Frequently asked questions

How is a monthly mortgage payment calculated?expand_more

Fixed-rate amortization for principal and interest, then monthly tax and insurance. Estimate only — not a lender quote.

Does this include PMI, HOA fees, or closing costs?expand_more

HOA is optional. Estimated PMI appears below 20% down. Closing costs are not included.

How much house can I afford?expand_more

A common starting point is 28–36% of gross income. Use the House Affordability Calculator.

Should I choose a 15-year or 30-year mortgage?expand_more

15-year usually costs less interest and more per month. 30-year keeps the payment lower. The 15-Year Fixed card above shows how much interest you'd save at your inputs.

Does a larger down payment lower my monthly payment?expand_more

Yes. It reduces the loan amount and often removes PMI at 20% down.

Are these figures a formal loan offer?expand_more

No. Confirm rates and fees with a lender quote.

Should I pay extra on my mortgage or invest that money instead?expand_more

On a $400,000 loan at 7%, $300/month extra saves $168,391 in interest and pays off 7.8 years early — a guaranteed 7% return. Investing the same $300/month at 8% would grow to ~$450,389 over the same period. See the full extra-payments-vs-investing breakdown for the math.

When exactly does PMI come off my loan?expand_more

By federal law, PMI automatically ends at 78% of your home's original value; you can request removal yourself at 80%. This calculator drops PMI at the 78% point, and extra payments bring that date forward.

How much does a 0.5% higher mortgage rate cost?expand_more

On a $320,000 30-year loan, going from 6.5% to 7.0% raises the monthly principal and interest from $2,022.62 to $2,128.97, about $106 more, and total interest by about $38,286.

How much interest does a 15-year mortgage save?expand_more

On a $320,000 loan, a 15-year at 5.75% costs $2,657.31 a month and $158,316 in total interest, versus $2,022.62 and $408,142 for a 30-year at 6.5%, a saving of about $249,826 (example rates).

How much PMI will I pay with 5% down?expand_more

On a $400,000 home at 6.5% with PMI at 0.5% a year, about $158 a month, ending after about 135 months when the scheduled balance reaches 78% of the original value, for roughly $21,375 in total.

Do biweekly mortgage payments really save money?expand_more

Yes, because 26 half-payments equal 13 full payments a year. On a $320,000 loan at 6.5% this pays off in about 24.2 years and saves about $93,996 in interest.

What happens to a 5/1 ARM payment after five years?expand_more

It resets to the then-current rate. In our example, 5.75% rising to 7.75% on a $320,000 loan lifts the payment from $1,867.43 to about $2,242.11.

Is it better to make a one-time extra payment or extra monthly payments?expand_more

Earlier and more frequent extra principal saves more. On a $320,000, 6.5% loan, $10,000 once in month 1 saves about $53,943 in interest, while $200 every month saves about $105,429.

How much does a yearly extra mortgage payment save?expand_more

On a $320,000 loan at 6.5%, $2,000 extra each year pays the loan off in about 24.4 years and saves about $88,832 in interest.

Why does my mortgage payment go up when my rate is fixed?expand_more

Principal and interest stay the same, but property tax and homeowners insurance usually rise. If tax grows 3% and insurance 5% a year, $466.67 a month in year 1 becomes about $637.66 in year 10.

Can I deduct mortgage interest?expand_more

Only if you itemize deductions. IRS Publication 936 (2025) caps the debt at $750,000 ($375,000 if married filing separately) for loans taken after 15 December 2017.

What costs does a mortgage calculator not include?expand_more

Closing costs, maintenance and repairs (a common rule of thumb is about 1% of home value a year), utilities, moving and furnishing. Ask your lender for a Loan Estimate for closing costs.
