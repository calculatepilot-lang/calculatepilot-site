How this calculator works

  • Monthly payment: M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n), where P is the loan amount, r the monthly rate (APR ÷ 12) and n the number of payments.
  • Each month: interest = balance × r; principal = payment − interest; the balance falls by the principal plus any extra payment.
  • Crossover: the first month where the principal part of the payment is larger than the interest part.

Payments cover principal and interest only — property tax, insurance, PMI and HOA fees aren't included (use the Mortgage Calculator for those). Figures are rounded to the cent; your lender's schedule may differ by a few cents.

A worked example

A $300,000 loan at 6.5% for 30 years: r = 0.065 ÷ 12 = 0.0054167 and n = 360, so the payment is $1,896.20 a month. The first payment is $1,625.00 interest and just $271.20 principal. Over 30 years you'd pay about $382,633 in interest, and principal only overtakes interest at payment 233 — nearly 20 years in. Adding $200 a month pays the loan off 6 years 11 months early and saves about $103,450 in interest.

Why your first payment is mostly interest

Interest is charged on the remaining balance, which is largest at the start. As the balance shrinks, less of each payment goes to interest and more to principal. On a 30-year loan at today's rates, the crossover usually comes after the halfway point, and extra principal payments early on move it much sooner.

Amortizing vs interest-only vs balloon loans

  • Fully amortizing (what this calculator builds): every payment covers interest and some principal, and the balance reaches zero at the end. $1,896.20 a month on the example loan.
  • Interest-only: payments cover only interest, so the balance does not fall. On the example loan that is $1,625.00 a month, which is $271.20 less than the first amortizing payment but pays nothing toward the principal.
  • Balloon: payments are calculated on a long schedule but the whole remaining balance is due at an earlier date. With a 30-year schedule and a balloon after 5 years, you would owe about $280,833 after 60 payments of $1,896.20.

Adjustable-rate loans and lines of credit do not follow a single fixed schedule, because the payment changes when the rate does. Use a fixed-rate loan with this tool.

Amortization in accounting is a different thing

Businesses also use "amortization" to mean spreading the cost of an intangible asset, or certain start-up costs, over time. That is a deduction schedule, not a loan balance, and this calculator does not compute it. One U.S. example: you can elect to deduct up to $5,000 of business start-up costs in the first year, reduced by the amount your costs exceed $50,000, and the remaining costs must be amortized (see IRS Publication 583 and the Instructions for Form 4562). Ask a tax adviser how it applies to you.

Frequently asked questions

Why does most of my early payment go to interest?expand_more

Interest is calculated on the remaining loan balance, which is largest at the start of the loan — as the balance shrinks with each payment, less interest accrues and more of the payment goes toward principal.

Does paying extra toward principal reduce total interest?expand_more

Yes, significantly — reducing the balance early means less interest accrues on it for the rest of the loan. Enter an extra monthly payment above to see the interest and time saved. Check that your lender applies extra payments to principal and doesn't charge a prepayment penalty.

Is this a full amortization schedule?expand_more

Yes. Switch the schedule between annual totals and every monthly payment, each showing principal, interest and the remaining balance, and use Print to save it.

How much of my loan is paid off after 5 years?expand_more

On a $300,000, 6.5%, 30-year loan, about 6.4%: $19,167 of principal, leaving a balance of about $280,833. After 10 years it is 15.2%.

Is a one-time extra payment or a monthly extra payment better?expand_more

Both help, but earlier and repeated extra principal saves more. On the example loan, $10,000 once in month 1 saves about $53,602, while $200 every month saves about $103,449.

What is the difference between an amortizing and an interest-only loan?expand_more

An amortizing loan pays down principal with each payment and ends at a zero balance. An interest-only loan pays only interest, so the balance stays the same: $1,625 a month on $300,000 at 6.5%.

What is a balloon payment?expand_more

A large lump sum due at the end of a shorter term, after smaller payments calculated on a longer schedule. With a 30-year schedule and a 5-year balloon on the example loan, about $280,833 is due after 60 payments.

Does this calculator handle business amortization of intangible assets?expand_more

No. It builds loan schedules only. Accounting amortization spreads an asset or cost over its useful or allowed life, and the tax rules are set separately. Ask a tax adviser.
