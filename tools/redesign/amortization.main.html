How this calculator works

  • Monthly payment: M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n), where P is the loan amount, r the monthly rate (APR ÷ 12) and n the number of payments.
  • Each month: interest = balance × r; principal = payment − interest; the balance falls by the principal plus any extra payment.
  • Crossover: the first month where the principal part of the payment is larger than the interest part.

Payments cover principal and interest only — property tax, insurance, PMI and HOA fees aren't included (use the Mortgage Calculator for those). Figures are rounded to the cent; your lender's schedule may differ by a few cents.

A worked example

A $300,000 loan at 6.5% for 30 years: r = 0.065 ÷ 12 = 0.0054167 and n = 360, so the payment is $1,896.20 a month. The first payment is $1,625.00 interest and just $271.20 principal. Over 30 years you'd pay about $382,637 in interest, and principal only overtakes interest at payment 233 — nearly 20 years in. Adding $200 a month pays the loan off 6 years 11 months early and saves about $103,450 in interest.

Why your first payment is mostly interest

Interest is charged on the remaining balance, which is largest at the start. As the balance shrinks, less of each payment goes to interest and more to principal. On a 30-year loan at today's rates, the crossover usually comes after the halfway point, and extra principal payments early on move it much sooner.

Frequently asked questions

Why does most of my early payment go to interest?expand_more

Interest is calculated on the remaining loan balance, which is largest at the start of the loan — as the balance shrinks with each payment, less interest accrues and more of the payment goes toward principal.

Does paying extra toward principal reduce total interest?expand_more

Yes, significantly — reducing the balance early means less interest accrues on it for the rest of the loan. Enter an extra monthly payment above to see the interest and time saved. Check that your lender applies extra payments to principal and doesn't charge a prepayment penalty.
