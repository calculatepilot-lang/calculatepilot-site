      • checkCash reserves: depending on the loan and your profile, lenders may require about 2–6 months of mortgage payments (PITI) left in the bank after closing.
      • checkSeasoned funds: lenders typically review two months of bank statements; large, unexplained deposits need a paper trail.
      • checkGift letters: money from family usually needs a signed letter confirming it's a gift, not a loan, plus proof of the transfer.

      Why 20% is a common target

      Putting down 20% typically avoids private mortgage insurance (PMI) on a conventional loan. That can save a meaningful amount over the life of the loan. Many programs still allow lower down payments.

      How down payment size affects your options

      Down payment size sets your loan amount. It also affects which loan programs you qualify for and whether you pay for mortgage insurance. Conventional loans typically require PMI below 20% down. Government-backed programs such as FHA and VA have their own down payment and insurance rules.

      A larger down payment can also strengthen your offer in a competitive market. Sellers often see buyers with more cash down as lower risk.

      A worked example: 15% vs 20% down

      On a $425,000 home at 15% down: the down payment is $63,750, leaving a loan amount of $361,250.

      Compare that to 20% down on the same home: $85,000 down and a $340,000 loan. You pay $21,250 more upfront, but the loan is $21,250 smaller. It also likely avoids PMI, which could save more over time than the extra cash.

      Worked example: cash to close on a $400,000 home

      These are the calculator defaults: a $400,000 home with 20% down, 3% closing costs, $5,000 for inspection, appraisal and moving, and an $8,500 reserve.

      • Down payment: $400,000 × 20% = $80,000.
      • Closing costs: $400,000 × 3% = $12,000.
      • Inspection, appraisal and moving: $5,000. Post-closing reserve: $8,500.
      • Total cash to close: $105,500.

      With $35,000 saved today in a 4.50% APY account, you would need about $2,687 a month for 24 months. That includes about $6,021 of interest earned along the way.

      Down payment assistance options

      Many buyers do not save the full 20%. They use down payment assistance programs, gift funds from family, or low-down-payment loans. Some of those loans allow as little as 3-5% down.

      The trade-off for a smaller down payment is usually private mortgage insurance (PMI) and a larger loan. Both raise the monthly payment. Weigh that against buying sooner, since home prices and rates may change while you keep saving. Once you've settled on a down payment, run the numbers through the Mortgage Calculator to see your full monthly payment.

      Key takeaways

      • How much to save for a house is more than the down payment. Add closing costs, inspection and moving costs, and a cash reserve.
      • On a $400,000 home with 20% down, the defaults add up to about $105,500 of cash to close.
      • Putting down 20% typically avoids PMI on a conventional loan. Many programs allow 3-5% down, with PMI until you build equity.
      • Waiting to reach 20% can cost more if home prices rise. Compare PMI paid with the price increase before you decide.

      Frequently asked questions

      Can I buy a home with less than 20% down?expand_more

      Yes — many conventional and government-backed loan programs allow down payments as low as 3-5%, though you'll typically pay for mortgage insurance until you build enough equity.

      Do I need 20% down to buy a home?expand_more

      No — many loan programs allow down payments as low as 3-5%, though anything below 20% on a conventional loan typically requires paying for private mortgage insurance (PMI).

      Where should down payment savings be kept?expand_more

      Since it's typically needed within a few years, many people keep down payment savings in lower-risk accounts like high-yield savings rather than volatile investments, to avoid the risk of a market downturn right before buying.

      Are there loan programs that allow less than 20% down?expand_more

      Yes — many conventional and government-backed programs (FHA, VA, USDA where applicable) allow down payments well below 20%, sometimes as low as 3%, typically with mortgage insurance required until sufficient equity is reached.

      How much should I save for a down payment?expand_more

      A common target is 20% of the purchase price, because it typically avoids PMI on a conventional loan. Many buyers put down less, from 3% to 10%. Also budget for closing costs, inspection and moving costs, and a cash reserve after closing.

      How much cash do I need to close on a $400,000 home?expand_more

      With the calculator defaults, about $105,500. That is an $80,000 down payment (20%), $12,000 of closing costs (3%), $5,000 for inspection, appraisal and moving, and an $8,500 post-closing reserve.

      How much does PMI cost with 5% down?expand_more

      On a $400,000 home at 5% down, the calculator estimates about $158 a month in PMI. Your real cost depends on your credit score, loan type and lender.

      Can I use gift money for my down payment?expand_more

      Often yes. Lenders usually ask for a signed gift letter saying the money is a gift, not a loan, plus proof of the transfer. Rules differ by loan program, so check with your lender.
