    ruleThe Mechanics of Paycheck Withholding

    Knowing how payroll taxes are calculated helps you avoid a surprise balance due — or an unnecessarily large refund — at tax time.

    • checkSocial Security wage cap: the 6.2% Social Security tax applies only to the first $184,500 of wages in 2026. Medicare (1.45%) has no cap.
    • checkAdditional Medicare Tax: employers withhold an extra 0.9% on wages above $200,000 in a year, whatever your filing status.
    • checkPre-tax isn't all the same: traditional 401(k) deferrals cut income tax but not FICA; Section 125 health premiums, payroll HSA contributions and commuter benefits cut both.
    • checkForm W-4 (2020+): allowances are gone. You adjust withholding with dollar amounts — credits for dependents in Step 3 and extra withholding in Step 4(c).

    How this calculator works

    Federal income tax withholding is estimated by annualizing your pay. Start with annual wages and subtract pre-tax deductions and the 2026 standard deduction for your filing status. Run the result through the 2026 IRS tax brackets, subtract any Form W-4 Step 3 credits, then divide by your number of paychecks. Social Security (6.2% up to $184,500) and Medicare (1.45%, plus 0.9% above $200,000) are applied to wages after Section 125 deductions. State tax uses a flat rate on your wages after pre-tax deductions. Real employer withholding follows IRS Publication 15-T, so individual paychecks can differ by a few dollars.

    How to use this paycheck calculator

    1. Enter your gross pay. Enter your gross compensation as an annual salary or an hourly rate. For hourly pay, also enter your hours per week.
    2. Choose your pay frequency and filing status. Choose weekly, bi-weekly, semi-monthly or monthly paychecks, then choose your filing status.
    3. Add a state tax rate. Choose a state or enter your own effective state income tax rate.
    4. Enter your pre-tax deductions. Enter your traditional 401(k) or 403(b) deferral, health insurance premiums, HSA or FSA contribution and commuter benefits.
    5. Add post-tax items and read your net pay. Enter any Roth 401(k), other post-tax deductions, W-4 Step 3 credits or extra withholding, then read your net pay per paycheck.

    What counts as a pre-tax deduction

    Pre-tax deductions reduce your taxable income before tax is calculated. Common examples include traditional 401(k) contributions, health insurance premiums and health savings account (HSA) contributions. Post-tax deductions, like Roth 401(k) contributions or wage garnishments, don't reduce taxable income, so they're entered separately.

    Getting this distinction right matters for accuracy. Entering a post-tax deduction as pre-tax would understate your taxable income and your estimated tax, which throws off the take-home figure. For a federal-only estimate on a known taxable income, use the Tax Calculator.

    Worked example with the calculator defaults

    The calculator opens on an $85,000 salary paid bi-weekly (26 paychecks), with a 4% state income tax rate. Pre-tax deductions are a 6% traditional 401(k), $120 of health insurance, $75 for an HSA and $30 of commuter benefits per paycheck.

    • Gross pay per paycheck: $85,000 ÷ 26 = $3,269.23.
    • Pre-tax deductions: $196.15 + $120.00 + $75.00 + $30.00 = $421.15, leaving $2,848.08 of taxable federal and state wages.
    • Federal income tax withholding: $286.96. State income tax at 4%: $113.92.
    • Social Security (6.2%): $188.74. Medicare (1.45%): $44.14.
    • Net take-home pay: $2,214.31 per paycheck (67.7% of gross), or $57,572.02 a year.

    Key takeaways

    • This 2026 paycheck calculator estimates take-home pay per paycheck after federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, state tax and your deductions.
    • Pre-tax deductions such as a traditional 401(k), health insurance and an HSA reduce your taxable wages. Post-tax items such as a Roth 401(k) do not.
    • On the defaults, an $85,000 salary paid bi-weekly nets $2,214.31 per paycheck, or about $57,572 a year.
    • It is an estimate. Real employer withholding follows IRS Publication 15-T, so a paycheck can differ by a few dollars.

    Frequently asked questions

    Which tax figures does this calculator use?expand_more

    2026 federal tax brackets and standard deductions from IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, the 2026 Social Security wage base of $184,500, and Medicare at 1.45% plus 0.9% on wages above $200,000. State income tax uses a flat rate you choose, since state rules vary widely.

    Where can I find my actual effective tax rate?expand_more

    Divide the total tax withheld on a recent pay stub or tax return by your gross income for that period — that gives a reasonably accurate personal effective rate to use here.

    How much is the take-home pay on an $85,000 salary in 2026?expand_more

    On the calculator defaults (paid bi-weekly, a 4% state tax rate and $421.15 of pre-tax deductions per paycheck), net pay is $2,214.31 per paycheck. That is about $57,572 a year, or 67.7% of gross pay.

    How is federal withholding on a paycheck estimated?expand_more

    The calculator annualizes your pay. It subtracts pre-tax deductions and the 2026 standard deduction for your filing status, applies the 2026 IRS tax brackets, subtracts any Form W-4 Step 3 credits, then divides by the number of paychecks.

    What are the Social Security and Medicare rates in 2026?expand_more

    For 2026, Social Security is 6.2% on wages up to $184,500. Medicare is 1.45% on all wages, plus 0.9% above $200,000. Both apply to wages after Section 125 deductions.

    Why is my real paycheck different from this estimate?expand_more

    Real employer withholding follows IRS Publication 15-T, so individual paychecks can differ by a few dollars. State rules, other income types and benefits not modeled here can also change the amount.
