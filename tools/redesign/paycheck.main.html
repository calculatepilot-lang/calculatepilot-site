    ruleThe Mechanics of Paycheck Withholding

    Knowing how payroll taxes are calculated helps you avoid a surprise balance due — or an unnecessarily large refund — at tax time.

    • checkSocial Security wage cap: the 6.2% Social Security tax applies only to the first $184,500 of wages in 2026. Medicare (1.45%) has no cap.
    • checkAdditional Medicare Tax: employers withhold an extra 0.9% on wages above $200,000 in a year, whatever your filing status.
    • checkPre-tax isn't all the same: traditional 401(k) deferrals cut income tax but not FICA; Section 125 health premiums, payroll HSA contributions and commuter benefits cut both.
    • checkForm W-4 (2020+): allowances are gone. You adjust withholding with dollar amounts — credits for dependents in Step 3 and extra withholding in Step 4(c).

    How this calculator works

    Federal income tax withholding is estimated by annualizing your pay: annual wages minus pre-tax deductions minus the 2026 standard deduction for your filing status, run through the 2026 IRS tax brackets, less any Form W-4 Step 3 credits, then divided by your number of paychecks. Social Security (6.2% up to $184,500) and Medicare (1.45%, plus 0.9% above $200,000) are applied to wages after Section 125 deductions. State tax uses a flat rate on your wages after pre-tax deductions. Real employer withholding follows IRS Publication 15-T, so individual paychecks can differ by a few dollars.

    What counts as a pre-tax deduction

    Pre-tax deductions reduce your taxable income before tax is calculated — common examples include traditional 401(k) contributions, health insurance premiums, and health savings account (HSA) contributions. Post-tax deductions, like Roth 401(k) contributions or wage garnishments, don't reduce taxable income, so they're entered separately.

    Getting this distinction right matters for accuracy — including a post-tax deduction as pre-tax would understate your taxable income and therefore your estimated tax, throwing off the final take-home figure. For a federal-only estimate on a known taxable income, use the Tax Calculator.

    Frequently asked questions

    Which tax figures does this calculator use?expand_more

    2026 federal tax brackets and standard deductions from IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, the 2026 Social Security wage base of $184,500, and Medicare at 1.45% plus 0.9% on wages above $200,000. State income tax uses a flat rate you choose, since state rules vary widely.

    Where can I find my actual effective tax rate?expand_more

    Divide the total tax withheld on a recent pay stub or tax return by your gross income for that period — that gives a reasonably accurate personal effective rate to use here.
