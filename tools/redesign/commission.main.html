How this calculator works

  • Flat rate: gross commission = sale amount × rate.
  • Tiered (marginal): each rate applies only to the part of the sale within its band, like tax brackets — so crossing a threshold never lowers your total.
  • Take-home = gross commission × your split − fees per deal. The brokerage or house keeps the rest of the gross.
  • Sales volume for a goal = income goal ÷ (take-home per dollar of sales), using the current sale's take-home rate.

A worked example

A $400,000 sale at a flat 3% gives $12,000 of gross commission. On a 70/30 split, you keep $8,400 and the brokerage keeps $3,600. With the tiered example (5% to $100,000, 3% to $300,000, 2% above), the same sale earns $5,000 + $6,000 + $2,000 = $13,000 gross — an effective rate of 3.25%.

How commission splits and rates work

In real estate and many sales roles, the commission on a deal is split between the individual and their brokerage or employer by a pre-agreed percentage — a "70/30 split" means you keep 70%. Splits often improve as you hit production milestones, and some firms use caps or flat desk fees instead. Real estate commissions are negotiable; since August 2024, National Association of REALTORS® rules no longer allow offers of compensation to buyers' agents on the MLS, and buyers sign written agreements with their agents.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 70/30 commission split typical?expand_more

Split arrangements vary widely by brokerage, industry and experience — 70/30 is a commonly cited example, but actual splits range broadly and are often negotiable, especially as your production increases.

Does this calculator handle tiered commission structures?expand_more

Yes. Choose "Tiered" and set up to three bands. Each rate applies only to the portion of the sale inside its band, the way marginal tax brackets work.

How do I calculate commission on a sale?expand_more

Multiply the sale amount by the commission rate — for example, $50,000 × 5% = $2,500. Then multiply by your split percentage and subtract any per-deal fees to get your take-home.
