The 2,080-Hour Rule vs Time Actually Worked

Payroll converts salary to an hourly rate by dividing by 2,080 hours (52 weeks × 40 hours). That treats paid holidays and vacation as working time. Take them out and your pay per hour actually worked is higher — which is why extra vacation days are worth weighing like a raise.

W-2 Salary vs 1099 Contractor Equivalent

A contractor pays both halves of Social Security and Medicare (an extra 7.65% on top of what an employee pays), gets no paid leave, and funds their own benefits and retirement. To match an employee package, the contract rate has to cover all of that from billable hours only.

Parity rate = (total package + 7.65% × base pay) ÷ hours actually worked. Contract rates well above the W-2 hourly figure are normal — they also need to cover gaps between contracts and business costs.

How this conversion works

This calculator assumes 52 working weeks a year unless you change it. If you take unpaid time off, your actual annual take-home will be somewhat lower than the figure shown here.

Gross pay vs. take-home pay

The figures here are gross pay: your earnings before taxes, retirement contributions and other deductions. Take-home (net) pay is typically 70-80% of gross for most US employees. The exact percentage depends heavily on your tax bracket, state of residence and benefit elections. For take-home pay, use the paycheck calculator.

When you compare job offers or negotiate pay, converting between hourly and annual figures puts offers on equal footing. This helps most when one role is quoted hourly and another as a salary.

A worked example

At $32/hour and 40 hours/week, annual salary works out to 32 times 40 times 52, or $66,560, with a weekly figure of $1,280 and monthly of about $5,547.

Going the other direction, a $75,000 annual salary at 40 hours/week works out to roughly $36.06/hour — useful for comparing an hourly job offer against a salaried one on equal footing.

When comparing job offers, look beyond the base salary. Employer 401(k) matching, health insurance quality, paid time off and bonus structures can add substantial value. A simple hourly-to-annual conversion doesn't capture it.

Worked example with the calculator defaults

The calculator opens on a $90,000 annual salary at 40 paid hours a week for 52 weeks. It adds 5 unpaid extra hours a week, 30 paid days off, a 4% 401(k) match, $7,200 of health and dental subsidy, a 10% bonus and $5,000 of equity.

  • Hourly rate: $90,000 ÷ 2,080 hours = $43.27, which is $1,730.77 a week and $7,500 a month.
  • Hours actually worked: 2,080 paid hours − 240 hours of paid time off + 260 unpaid extra hours = 2,100. That gives $42.86 per hour at your desk.
  • Total package: $90,000 base + $14,000 bonus and equity + $10,800 benefits = $114,800, or 1.276 times base pay.
  • Contractor parity rate: $57.95 an hour, which is 34% above the base hourly rate.

Key takeaways

  • Payroll converts an annual salary to hourly pay by dividing by 2,080 hours, which is 52 weeks times 40 hours.
  • Your real hourly wage is lower with unpaid extra hours and higher if you count paid time off as time away from work.
  • On the defaults, a $90,000 salary is $43.27 an hour on paid hours and a $114,800 total package.
  • These are gross figures before tax. Use the paycheck calculator for take-home pay.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include taxes?expand_more

No — this shows gross pay only. Actual take-home pay depends on your tax bracket, deductions, and location.

How many work weeks does this assume per year?expand_more

52 weeks. If you have unpaid vacation or leave, reduce the annual figure proportionally.

How do I estimate my actual take-home pay?expand_more

Subtract federal and state income tax, Social Security, Medicare, and any pre-tax deductions like 401(k) contributions or health insurance from your gross pay — typically leaving 70-80% as net pay, depending on your situation.

Is a higher hourly rate always better than a salary?expand_more

Not necessarily — salaried roles often include benefits like paid time off and health insurance that hourly roles may lack, so total compensation matters more than the base rate alone.

Does this show take-home pay or gross pay?expand_more

Gross pay — before taxes and deductions. Actual take-home pay is typically lower, depending on your tax bracket, benefits elections, and location.

How is overtime pay calculated?expand_more

In the US, non-exempt employees are generally entitled to 1.5x their regular hourly rate for hours worked beyond 40 in a week, though this calculator does not include overtime in its standard conversion.

How do I convert $90,000 a year to an hourly rate?expand_more

Divide by 2,080 paid hours, which is 52 weeks times 40 hours. $90,000 ÷ 2,080 = $43.27 an hour. That is $1,730.77 a week and $7,500 a month.

What is my real hourly wage after unpaid overtime?expand_more

On the defaults, 5 unpaid extra hours a week add 260 hours, so a $90,000 salary works out to $38.46 an hour over 2,340 hours. Counting only hours at work and excluding 30 paid days off, it is $42.86 an hour.

What is total compensation?expand_more

Total compensation is base pay plus bonus, equity and benefits. On the defaults, a $90,000 salary comes to a $114,800 total package, which is 1.276 times base pay.

What is the contractor parity rate?expand_more

It is the 1099 hourly rate that compares with a W-2 salary. On the defaults it is $57.95 an hour, which is 34% above the $43.27 base hourly rate, billing 2,100 hours.
