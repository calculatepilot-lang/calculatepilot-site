How this calculator works

A MET (metabolic equivalent) expresses an activity's energy cost as a multiple of resting, where 1 MET = 3.5 mL of oxygen per kg per minute. Using the standard ACSM conversion:

  • Calories per minute = MET × 3.5 × weight (kg) ÷ 200
  • Net calories (above what you'd burn resting anyway) = (MET − 1) × 3.5 × weight (kg) ÷ 200
  • Intensity (Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans): light < 3 METs, moderate 3–5.9, vigorous ≥ 6

MET values come from the 2024 Adult Compendium of Physical Activities. If you add sex, age and height, the calculator also shows a corrected MET — the Compendium's recommended adjustment that divides 3.5 by your predicted resting rate (Harris-Benedict), since the standard 3.5 overstates resting metabolism for many adults. It can move an activity into a higher intensity band; calorie totals stay the same.

A worked example

A 75 kg (165 lb) person running at 6 mph (MET 9.3) for 30 minutes: 9.3 × 3.5 × 75 ÷ 200 = 12.2 kcal a minute, or about 366 kcal. About 39 kcal of that would have been burned at rest anyway, so the net extra is about 327 kcal. The same 30 minutes of Hatha yoga (MET 2.3) is about 91 kcal.

How the session counts toward activity guidelines

Adults are advised to get 150–300 minutes of moderate activity a week, or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity, or an equivalent mix, plus muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days. A minute of vigorous activity counts roughly the same as two minutes of moderate activity.

Why MET-based estimates are approximate

The Compendium was built to standardize activity intensities for research, not to measure one person's exact burn. Fitness, technique, terrain and body composition all shift the real figure. Heart-rate-based wearables can be more personal, though they have their own error.

Running: why a mile costs about the same at any speed

Running faster burns more per minute, but you also finish sooner. At 6 mph (MET 9.3) a 75 kg person burns about 122 gross calories per mile. At 5 mph (MET 8.5) it is about 134. Per mile, running costs a little more than walking: about 122 calories against about 100 at 3 mph.

Should you eat back the calories you burn?

Not automatically. Exercise estimates are averages, and fitness trackers can be 10–20% off in either direction. If you set your daily calorie target with an activity level that already includes your workouts, those calories are already counted. Eating them back would count them twice. Use your Calorie Calculator target, track your weekly average weight, and adjust from real results.

Why other calculators show slightly different numbers

This calculator multiplies MET × 3.5 × kg ÷ 200 per minute, which is the ACSM form and equals 1.05 × MET × kg per hour. Some calculators use 1 kcal per kg per hour for each MET, which gives about 5% less. Others use different MET tables or a heart-rate model. Pick one tool and use it consistently when you compare sessions.

Frequently asked questions

What is a MET?expand_more

A MET (metabolic equivalent of task) compares an activity's energy use with sitting at rest. 1 MET is resting; running at 6 mph is 9.3 METs, meaning it uses roughly 9.3 times as much energy per minute.

What's the difference between gross and net calories burned?expand_more

Gross calories include what your body would have burned at rest during that time. Net calories subtract that resting amount, which is a fairer figure when deciding how much extra energy exercise used.

Why is my fitness tracker's number different?expand_more

Trackers combine heart rate, movement and their own algorithms, while this calculator uses average MET values for each activity. Both are estimates — use one method consistently to track changes over time.

How many calories does walking a mile burn?expand_more

About 100 gross calories for a 75 kg (165 lb) person at roughly 3 mph, and about 108 at 3.5 to 4 mph. Net of resting calories it is about 73 to 89. Heavier people burn more per mile.

How many calories do 10,000 steps burn?expand_more

For a 75 kg person with a 0.75 m stride at about 3 mph, 10,000 steps takes around 1.55 hours and burns roughly 465 gross calories (about 343 net). Lighter people and shorter strides burn less.

How many calories does a 30-minute run burn?expand_more

At 6 mph (MET 9.3) a 60 kg person burns about 293 calories in 30 minutes, a 75 kg person about 366 and a 90 kg person about 439.

How many calories do I burn in an hour?expand_more

Multiply the per-minute figure by 60. A 75 kg person burns about 299 an hour walking at 3 mph, about 630 an hour cycling at 12–13.9 mph, and about 732 an hour running at 6 mph.

Does body weight change how many calories I burn?expand_more

Yes. The formula multiplies by weight, so a 90 kg person burns 50% more than a 60 kg person doing the same activity for the same time.

Should I eat back the calories I burn exercising?expand_more

Not automatically. The estimates are averages and wearables can be off by 10–20%. If your calorie target already includes your activity level, eating back workout calories counts them twice.

Why does another calories burned calculator give a different answer?expand_more

Calculators use different MET tables and formulas. This one uses MET × 3.5 × kg ÷ 200 per minute. Calculators that use 1 kcal per kg per hour for each MET show about 5% less.

Is this calculator suitable for steps or walking?expand_more

Yes, choose a walking activity and enter your time. To convert steps to time, divide the steps by your steps per minute, which depends on stride length and pace.
