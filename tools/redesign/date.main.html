Formulas

How the counts work

Calendar days

days = (end − start) ÷ 86,400,000 ms (+1 if the end day is included)

Working days

count each date d in [start, end) where d is not a weekend, holiday or extra day off

Leap year

(y mod 4 = 0 and y mod 100 ≠ 0) or y mod 400 = 0
Holidays

What is skipped

US federal holidays are the eleven in 5 U.S.C. 6103. A holiday that falls on a Saturday is observed on Friday and one on a Sunday on Monday. UK, Canada and Australia lists cover the common national holidays, and moving rules such as Easter are calculated.

Regional and one-off holidays are not included. Add them in the extra days off box.

How to count days between two dates

  • Pick the start and end dates and decide whether the end day counts.
  • For calendar days, subtract the dates. Leap days are handled automatically.
  • For business days, remove weekends and any public holidays that fall on weekdays.
  • To find a deadline, use Add or subtract days with the business-day option.

Contracts and laws define how days are counted. Check whether the first or last day is included, and which holidays apply, before relying on a date.

Frequently asked questions

Is the end date included in the count?expand_more

By default no: the count runs from the start date up to, but not including, the end date. Turn on Include the end day to add one. Legal deadlines often specify the rule, so check yours.

How are business days counted?expand_more

Every date in the range that is not a Saturday, Sunday, chosen public holiday or extra day off counts as a working day. A holiday that falls on a weekend is not deducted twice.

Which US holidays are skipped?expand_more

The eleven federal holidays: New Year's Day, Martin Luther King Jr. Day, Washington's Birthday, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, Independence Day, Labor Day, Columbus Day, Veterans Day, Thanksgiving and Christmas Day, with weekend observance rules.

How does the tool handle leap years?expand_more

Dates are counted on the real Gregorian calendar, so 29 February is included in leap years. A year is a leap year if divisible by 4, except centuries not divisible by 400.

Why is the next working day useful?expand_more

It shows when work resumes after the interval ends, skipping weekends and holidays. It is handy for delivery windows, notice periods and project handovers.
