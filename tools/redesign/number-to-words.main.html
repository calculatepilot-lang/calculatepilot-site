    How the conversion works

    The integer part is split into groups of three digits (triads) from the right. Each triad is spelled out as a number from 1–999, and paired with its scale word — thousand, million, billion, and so on — then the groups are joined together.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why do "billion" mean different things in different countries?expand_more

    The US and modern UK use the "short scale," where each new name is 1000× the last (million, billion = 10⁹, trillion = 10¹²). Much of continental Europe traditionally uses the "long scale," where billion = 10¹² and milliard fills the 10⁹ spot — this calculator supports both.

    Why do checks write out amounts in words?expand_more

    Under the Uniform Commercial Code and similar laws elsewhere, if the written words and the numeric figure on a check disagree, the words legally control — a safeguard against a single altered digit changing the amount.

    How are cents handled on a check?expand_more

    Conventionally written as a fraction over 100, e.g. "and 75/100 Dollars," rather than spelled out — reducing ambiguity, since the fraction format is instantly recognizable.

    What's the difference between a cardinal and an ordinal number?expand_more

    Cardinal numbers count quantity (one, two, three); ordinal numbers describe position or rank (first, second, third). Only the final word of a spelled-out number changes form for the ordinal — "twenty-three" becomes "twenty-third."

    Is there a limit to how large a number this can spell out?expand_more

    Named scale words are supported up to the short-scale decillion (10³³) / long-scale equivalent; beyond that, the number is shown in scientific notation instead, since no widely agreed English name exists.
