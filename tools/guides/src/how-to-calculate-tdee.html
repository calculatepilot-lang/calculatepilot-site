How to Calculate Your TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure)

Your TDEE is the calories you burn in a day. Here is the formula, the activity multipliers and two worked examples you can copy.

→ Use the free TDEE Calculator

Short answer: to calculate your TDEE, work out your resting calories (BMR) with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, then multiply by an activity factor from 1.2 to 1.9. A 40-year-old woman, 70 kg and 165 cm, who exercises 3 to 5 days a week has a BMR of about 1,370 and a TDEE of about 2,124 calories a day.

TDEE = BMR × activity multiplier
BMR (men) = 10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × age + 5
BMR (women) = 10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × age − 161

What TDEE is made of

TDEE stands for total daily energy expenditure: every calorie your body uses in 24 hours. It has three parts.

  • Resting energy (BMR). Breathing, circulation, temperature control and cell repair. This is the largest share, and our BMR Calculator estimates it on its own.
  • Digesting food. The thermic effect of food is commonly estimated at about 10% of daily energy use.
  • Movement. Planned exercise plus everyday movement such as walking, standing and fidgeting.

Want to see how the first two compare? Read BMR vs TDEE: what is the difference.

Step 1: estimate your BMR

Use your weight in kilograms, height in centimeters and age in years. For the 40-year-old woman above (70 kg, 165 cm):

BMR = 10 × 70 + 6.25 × 165 − 5 × 40 − 161 = 700 + 1,031 − 200 − 161 = about 1,370 calories.

Step 2: choose an activity multiplier

SedentaryDesk job, little or no exercise1.21,644
Lightly activeExercise 1–3 days a week1.3751,884
Moderately activeExercise 3–5 days a week1.552,124

The gap between the lowest and highest row is almost 1,000 calories, so this single choice matters more than any other input. If you are between two levels, pick the lower one.

Step 3: multiply

TDEE = 1,370 × 1.55 = about 2,124 calories a day. Eating around that amount should keep this person's weight steady.

A second example: a man who trains lightly

  1. A 35-year-old man, 80 kg, 178 cm, exercising 1 to 3 days a week.
  2. BMR = 10 × 80 + 6.25 × 178 − 5 × 35 + 5 = about 1,743 calories.
  3. TDEE = 1,743 × 1.375 = about 2,396 calories a day.

Which BMR formula should you use?

Mifflin-St Jeor is the usual default for adults. A systematic review by Frankenfield and colleagues found it the most likely of the common equations to land close to measured resting energy use. If you know your body-fat percentage, the Katch-McArdle and Cunningham equations use lean body mass instead of weight, height and age. They are worth comparing for very lean or muscular people, though they are not guaranteed to be more accurate for any individual. You can switch between all four equations in the TDEE Calculator, and estimate your lean mass with the Lean Body Mass Calculator.

How to check your TDEE against real life

A TDEE figure is a starting estimate. To calibrate it:

  1. Eat roughly the calculated amount for two to three weeks and keep your routine steady.
  2. Weigh yourself under similar conditions and compare weekly averages, not single days.
  3. If your weight is rising, your true TDEE is probably lower than estimated. If it is falling, it is probably higher. Adjust by a small amount and repeat.

Using your TDEE for a goal

  • Lose weight: start with a modest deficit below TDEE. The Weight Loss Calorie Calculator turns your TDEE into a daily target and timeline, and our guide explains how many calories to eat to lose 1 pound a week.
  • Maintain: eat close to TDEE and re-check every few weeks.
  • Build muscle: a small surplus alongside resistance training, with enough protein. See how much protein you need per day and split calories with the Macro Calculator.

Common mistakes

  • Picking an activity level that is too high. Three gym sessions on top of a desk job is usually “lightly” or “moderately” active, not “very active”.
  • Counting exercise twice. The multiplier already includes your workouts, so do not add calories from a fitness tracker on top.
  • Treating the result as exact. Real needs vary from person to person.
  • Never recalculating. TDEE falls as you lose weight and rises as you gain.

Quick habit: put your TDEE in your phone notes, then re-run the TDEE Calculator every time your weight moves by about 5 kg (10 lb).

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my TDEE?

Estimate your BMR with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, then multiply it by an activity factor between 1.2 (sedentary) and 1.9 (extra active). The result is your estimated maintenance calories per day.

What activity multiplier should I use?

Most people choose too high. Use 1.2 for a desk job with little exercise, 1.375 for light exercise 1 to 3 days a week, 1.55 for moderate exercise 3 to 5 days a week, and only 1.725 or 1.9 if you train hard most days or have a physical job.

Is TDEE the same as maintenance calories?

Yes, in practice. TDEE is the estimated number of calories you burn in a day, so eating about that amount keeps your weight roughly stable.

How accurate is a TDEE calculator?

It is an estimate, not a measurement. Individual metabolism and daily movement vary, so compare your result with your average weight trend over two to three weeks and adjust.

How often should I recalculate TDEE?

Recalculate when your weight, activity or training changes meaningfully, and every few weeks during a weight-loss or weight-gain phase.

Should I eat my TDEE or below it?

Eating around your TDEE maintains weight. A modest deficit below it is a common starting point for weight loss, but the right size depends on you. See the guide on calories to lose a pound a week.
