How Much Water Should You Drink a Day?

The official intake figures, how to adjust for exercise and heat, and easy habits that keep you hydrated.

→ Use the free Water Intake Calculator

Short answer: the National Academies set an adequate intake of about 2.7 liters of total water a day for women and 3.7 liters for men. About 80% comes from drinks, which works out to roughly 2.2 liters (9 cups) for women and 3.0 liters (13 cups) for men, plus more when you exercise or it is hot.

The European Food Safety Authority sets lower adult values (2.5 L for men and 2.0 L for women), so there is no single exact answer. Treat these as a starting point, then adjust to your body, climate and activity. The Water Intake Calculator does this for you, including extra fluid for exercise.

Adding water for exercise

Extra fluid (L) = minutes of exercise ÷ 60 × sweat rate (L per hour)

Take a 30-year-old man who exercises 45 minutes a day. His drinks target is about 3.0 liters. At an assumed sweat rate of 0.5 L an hour, 45 minutes adds about 0.4 liters, so about 3.3 liters (113 oz, 14 cups) on exercise days. Sweat rates vary a lot, so to measure yours, weigh yourself before and after a workout. The American College of Sports Medicine advises drinking about 1.25 to 1.5 liters for every kilogram lost. Signs you are drinking enough

  • Urine is pale yellow.
  • You rarely feel very thirsty.
  • You are not getting headaches or dizziness from heat or exercise.

Hot weather, high altitude, fever, vomiting or diarrhea, and pregnancy or breastfeeding all raise needs. People with heart, kidney or liver conditions may be told to limit fluids, so follow your doctor's advice.

Does coffee, tea, soda or food count?

Yes. Every beverage counts toward your total, and food supplies about 20% of the day's water, especially fruit and vegetables. Plain water is still the best default because it adds no sugar or calories. Six quick habits to drink more water

  1. Drink a glass when you wake up before coffee.
  2. Keep a bottle at your desk and refill it at set times.
  3. Drink with every meal and snack.
  4. Add a glass for each workout, plus more when it is hot.
  5. Flavor it naturally with lemon, cucumber or mint if plain water is boring.
  6. Use your urine color as a check instead of counting every glass.

Pairing hydration with other small habits works well. A daily walk raises your energy use too, so see how many steps burn 100 calories and how your daily energy needs fit together in how many calories you should eat a day.

Frequently asked questions

How much water should I drink a day?

The National Academies set an adequate intake of about 2.7 liters of total water for women and 3.7 liters for men. About 80 percent comes from drinks, so roughly 2.2 liters (9 cups) for women and 3.0 liters (13 cups) for men.

Does coffee, tea and other drinks count?

Yes. All beverages, including coffee and tea, contribute to your total, and food supplies about 20 percent of daily water.

Is the 8 glasses a day rule accurate?

Eight 8-ounce glasses (about 1.9 liters) is a simple rule of thumb, not an official recommendation. The National Academies figure for drinks is higher for most adults.

How do I know if I am drinking enough?

Pale-yellow urine and rarely feeling thirsty are usual signs of good hydration. Darker urine suggests you need more.

Can you drink too much water?

Yes, though it is uncommon. Drinking very large amounts in a short time can dilute blood sodium (hyponatremia), mainly a risk in long endurance events.

How much extra water do I need when I exercise?

Add the fluid you sweat out. Sweat rates vary widely, roughly 0.3 to 2.4 liters an hour according to the American College of Sports Medicine, so weigh yourself before and after a workout to find yours.
