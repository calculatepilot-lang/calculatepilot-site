How Many Calories Should You Eat to Lose 1 Pound a Week?

The short version is a 500-calorie daily deficit. Here is the math, a chart by maintenance level and the limits of the rule.

→ Use the free Weight Loss Calorie Calculator

Short answer: to lose about 1 pound a week, eat about 500 calories a day below your maintenance level (your TDEE). If you maintain your weight at 2,200 calories, that means about 1,700 a day. The rule behind it is that a pound of body weight is roughly 3,500 calories.

Daily deficit = Pounds per week × 3,500 ÷ 7
Daily target = TDEE − Daily deficit

Worked example

  1. Your maintenance is 2,200 calories (find yours with the TDEE Calculator).
  2. For 1 lb a week, the deficit is 1 × 3,500 ÷ 7 = 500 calories.
  3. Daily target = 2,200 − 500 = 1,700 calories.
  4. Losing 20 pounds at 1 lb a week takes about 20 weeks.

Why the real timeline is often longer

The 3,500-calorie rule is a useful planning tool, but it overestimates long-term loss. As you get lighter, your maintenance calories fall, so a fixed intake creates a smaller deficit over time. Research by Hall and colleagues at the NIH shows weight loss slows for this reason. Recalculate your TDEE every few weeks as your weight changes.

How fast is too fast?

The CDC notes that people who lose weight gradually and steadily, about 1 to 2 pounds a week, are more likely to keep it off. Faster plans mean bigger deficits, which can be hard to sustain and may leave you short on nutrients.

Common mistakes

  • Guessing maintenance calories. Start from an estimate of your own TDEE, not a generic 2,000.
  • Setting the deficit too large. A very low target is hard to sustain and may be unsafe.
  • Not adjusting over time. Maintenance falls as you lose weight.
  • Counting exercise calories twice. If your TDEE already includes activity, don't add it back.

Frequently asked questions

How many calories should I cut to lose 1 pound a week?

About 500 calories a day below your maintenance level, based on the 3,500 calories-per-pound planning rule.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

Subtract your target deficit from your TDEE. With a 2,200 calorie maintenance and a 500 calorie deficit, that is about 1,700 calories.

Is the 3,500 calorie rule accurate?

It is a useful approximation but overestimates long-term loss, because your maintenance calories fall as you lose weight.

What is the safest rate of weight loss?

The CDC notes that about 1 to 2 pounds a week is a gradual, steady rate linked with keeping weight off. Talk to a healthcare professional before starting a diet.
