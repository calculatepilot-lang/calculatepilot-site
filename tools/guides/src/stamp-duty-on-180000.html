Stamp Duty on £180,000: What You Pay in 2026

The exact SDLT bill on a £180,000 home in England or Northern Ireland, for a main home, a first-time buyer, a second home and a non-UK resident, with the band-by-band working.

→ Use the free UK Stamp Duty Calculator

Short answer: Stamp duty on a £180,000 property in England and Northern Ireland is £1,100, or £0 for a first-time buyer. A second home or buy-to-let costs £10,100. These figures use the rates in force from 1 April 2025.

At £180,000 the first £125,000 is free and the remaining £55,000 is taxed at 2%.

How the £1,100 is worked out

Stamp duty is marginal: each slice of the price is charged at the rate for its own band, not the whole price at one rate. For £180,000 the standard working is:

BandRateTaxable sliceTax
£0 to £125,0000%£125,000£0
£125,000 to £180,0002%£55,000£1,100

The bill is the sum of the Tax column, £1,100. That is an effective rate of 0.61% of the price.

First-time buyer stamp duty on £180,000

A first-time buyer pays £0 (nil up to £300,000, then 5% to £500,000). That saves £1,100 against the standard rates.

Second home or buy-to-let on £180,000

The higher rates add 5% to every band, so the same purchase as an additional residential property costs £10,100, which is £9,000 more than a main home. The surcharge applies when you buy a residential property for £40,000 or more and still own another one at the end of the purchase day. If you are replacing your main home you may be able to reclaim the surcharge: see our guide to stamp duty on a second home or buy-to-let.

What a little more or less would cost

Each extra £10,000 on the price adds about £200 of stamp duty at this level (the marginal rate is 2%). Moving from £180,000 to £205,000 would raise the main-home bill from £1,100 to £1,600, a difference of £500.

Frequently asked questions

How much stamp duty do you pay on £180,000?

In England and Northern Ireland, the standard SDLT on £180,000 is £1,100 (an effective rate of 0.61%). A first-time buyer pays £0, and an additional property such as a second home or buy-to-let pays £10,100.

Is stamp duty on £180,000 different for a second home?

Yes. The higher rates add 5% to every band, so a second home or buy-to-let at this price costs £10,100, which is £9,000 more than a main home.

Do non-UK residents pay more stamp duty on £180,000?

Yes. A further 2% applies to all residential bands, making the main-home figure £4,700. A non-UK resident buying an additional property pays £13,700.

Does this apply in Scotland or Wales?

No. Scotland charges LBTT and Wales charges LTT, with different bands and rates. These figures are for England and Northern Ireland only.
