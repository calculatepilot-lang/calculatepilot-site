529 Plan vs Taxable Brokerage Account for College Savings

The tax shelter is the 529's big advantage. Here is what it is worth and what you give up.

→ Use the free 529 College Savings Calculator

Short answer: Saving $250 a month for 18 years at 6% grows to about $96,838, of which $42,838 is growth. At a 15% capital-gains rate, that growth would cost about $6,426 in tax in a taxable account, but the 529 is tax-free if used for qualified expenses. A taxable account is more flexible if the money may not go to education.

This simple illustration assumes no yearly taxes on dividends or turnover in the taxable account. In practice those would reduce the taxable account further, but fees and investment choices differ by plan.

How they compare

  • Tax on growth: 529 earnings are federally tax-free for qualified education expenses.
  • State benefits: more than 30 states offer a deduction or credit for contributions, usually with a limit.
  • Flexibility: non-qualified 529 withdrawals pay income tax and a 10% additional tax on the earnings; a taxable account can be used for anything.
  • Options: 529 plans offer a menu of portfolios, often age-based; a taxable account can hold anything you choose.

Who may prefer a taxable account

  • You are unsure the money will go to education.
  • You have already used up the benefits of a 529 and want extra flexibility.

Many families split savings between both. Model the 529 side with the 529 College Savings Calculator and read the 2026 rules.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 529 better than a taxable account for college?

For college costs, usually yes on tax: the growth is federally tax-free if used for qualified expenses. A taxable account offers more flexibility.

How much tax does a 529 save?

In our example, $250 a month for 18 years at 6% produces $42,838 of growth. At a 15% rate, the tax avoided is about $6,426.

What if my child doesn't go to college?

You can change the beneficiary, use it for other qualified expenses, or roll up to $35,000 to a Roth IRA under the rules. Otherwise the earnings are taxed plus 10%.

Do I get a state tax deduction for a 529?

Many states offer one, often limited to their own plan or a capped amount. Check your state.
