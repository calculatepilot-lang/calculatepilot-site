Savings Account Types Compared: HYSA, CD, Money Market

Four ways to save cash and how they differ.

→ Use the free CD vs HYSA Calculator

Short answer: High-yield savings and money market accounts have variable rates and open access. CDs have fixed rates and a penalty. All are insured to $250,000 at FDIC banks or NCUA credit unions.

Insurance

Deposits at FDIC-insured banks and NCUA-insured credit unions are covered up to $250,000 per depositor, per institution, per ownership category. This applies to all four account types.

Money market account vs money market fund

A money market account is a bank deposit and is insured. A money market fund is an investment product, is not FDIC insured, and can lose value in rare cases.

Why the rate gap matters

$20,000 earns $800 a year at 4.00% and $20 a year at 0.10%. A difference of 3.9 percentage points is $780 a year on that balance.

How to choose

  • Need the money any time: high-yield savings.
  • Want to write checks or use a debit card: money market account.
  • Will not need it for a set time and want a fixed rate: CD.
  • Check fees and minimums before you open any account.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a HYSA and a money market account?

Both have variable rates and open access. A money market account may add check writing or a debit card, and often needs a higher minimum balance.

Are all savings accounts FDIC insured?

Accounts at FDIC-insured banks are covered up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category. Credit unions have NCUA coverage.

Is a money market fund the same as a money market account?

No. A money market fund is an investment product and is not FDIC insured.

Which account has the highest rate?

It changes with the market, so compare current APYs. CDs and HYSAs usually pay more than traditional savings.
