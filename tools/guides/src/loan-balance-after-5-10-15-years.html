How Much of Your Loan Is Paid Off After 5, 10 and 15 Years?

Equity builds slowly at first because early payments are mostly interest. The table shows exactly how slowly.

→ Use the free Amortization Calculator

Short answer: On a $300,000, 6.5%, 30-year loan, you have repaid only 6.4% of the principal ($19,167) after 5 years, 15.2% after 10 years and 27.4% after 15 years. The halfway point in principal arrives in year 21 or so, well after the halfway point in time.

Why it starts slowly

Interest is charged on the balance. In payment 1, $1,625.00 of the $1,896.20 is interest and only $271.20 is principal. Principal does not overtake interest until payment 233, almost 20 years in.

What this means

  • Selling or refinancing in the first few years leaves most of the balance still owed.
  • Extra principal early shifts the whole curve. See lump sum vs monthly extra payments.
  • A shorter term repays principal faster, at a higher payment. See short vs long loan terms.

Frequently asked questions

How much of my loan is paid off after 5 years?

On a $300,000, 6.5%, 30-year loan, about 6.4% ($19,167). The remaining balance is about $280,833.

When do I own half of my loan?

In this example, you have repaid 44.3% after 20 years and 67.7% after 25, so the halfway point in principal comes around year 21.

Why is so little principal repaid early?

Interest is charged on the full balance, which is largest at the start, so most of each early payment is interest.

Does the balance schedule change with extra payments?

Yes. Extra principal lowers the balance faster, which reduces future interest and shortens the loan.
