2026 401(k) Contribution Limits: Standard, Catch-Up and Super Catch-Up

The numbers that decide how much you can put in this year, by age, and what changes for higher earners.

→ Use the free 401(k) Calculator

Short answer: For 2026 you can defer up to $24,500 into a 401(k). Age 50 and over can add an $8,000 catch-up ($32,500 total), and ages 60 to 63 can add $11,250 instead ($35,750 total). The combined employee and employer cap is $72,000, not counting catch-up.

2026 limits at a glance

Your age in 2026Employee limit
Under 50$24,500
50 to 59, and 64 or older$32,500 ($24,500 + $8,000 catch-up)
60 to 63$35,750 ($24,500 + $11,250 super catch-up)
  • Combined limit. Total contributions from you and your employer are capped at $72,000 for 2026, not counting catch-up.
  • Roth catch-up for higher earners. If your prior-year FICA wages from your employer were above $150,000, your catch-up contributions must be made as Roth contributions, if your plan offers a Roth option.
  • IRA limit. The 2026 IRA limit is $7,500, separate from your 401(k) limit.

What the limits mean in practice

The employee limit applies to what you defer from your pay, across all of your 401(k) plans (traditional and Roth combined). Employer matching and profit-sharing contributions do not count toward it. To max out, divide the limit by your pay periods: $24,500 over 26 biweekly paycheques is about $942 each.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 401(k) contribution limit for 2026?

$24,500 for your own deferrals. Age 50 and over can add $8,000, and ages 60 to 63 can add $11,250 instead.

What is the 401(k) super catch-up?

Workers aged 60 to 63 can contribute an extra $11,250 in 2026 instead of the standard $8,000 catch-up, for a total of $35,750.

Does my employer match count toward the $24,500 limit?

No. Employer contributions are separate, but total additions from you and your employer are capped at $72,000 in 2026, not counting catch-up.

Do I have to make Roth catch-up contributions?

Only if your prior-year FICA wages from the employer were above $150,000, and your plan offers a Roth option.
