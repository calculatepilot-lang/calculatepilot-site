Stamp Duty on a Second Home or Buy-to-Let: The 5% Surcharge Explained

How the extra 5% works, when it applies, what it costs at common prices and how to claim it back if you are replacing your main home.

→ Use the free UK Stamp Duty Calculator

Short answer: If you own another residential property when you complete on a purchase of £40,000 or more, you usually pay 5% extra Stamp Duty Land Tax on top of the standard rates in England and Northern Ireland. That applies to second homes and buy-to-lets, and a refund is possible if you sell your previous main home within 3 years.

When the higher rates apply

The higher rates apply when you buy a residential property for £40,000 or more and you own, or part-own, another residential property at the end of the purchase day. It is the whole transaction that is affected: if you buy jointly and any buyer must pay the higher rates, everyone's purchase is charged at them. Your spouse or civil partner's properties are counted too, and so is property held for children under 18.

It covers a buy-to-let, a holiday home and a second home. It also catches a new main home if you still own your old one on completion day.

How much it costs

The higher rates are the standard residential rates plus 5% on every band. Standard rates in England and Northern Ireland are 0% to £125,000, 2% to £250,000, 5% to £925,000, 10% to £1.5 million and 12% above.

If you are not resident in the UK, a further 2% applies on top of all residential rates. At £300,000 that makes a second home £26,000.

What does not count

  • Properties worth less than £40,000.
  • Mixed residential and non-residential property.
  • Caravans, houseboats and mobile homes, unless permanently fixed.
  • Leases of 7 years or less, and some long leases owned by someone else.

Replacing your main home: avoid or reclaim the surcharge

The 5% does not apply if the new property replaces your previous main home and you sold that home before completion. If you have not sold it by completion, you pay the higher rates, then you can claim a refund if you sell your previous main home within 3 years (36 months) of buying the new one.

  1. Pay the higher rates when you complete and file the SDLT return.
  2. Sell your previous main home within 3 years.
  3. Claim the refund. For sales on or after 29 October 2018, the claim must be made within 12 months of the later of the sale date and the filing date of the new home's return.

There is no refund if you or your spouse still own any part of the previous home. Exceptional circumstances, such as government restrictions, can extend the 3 years for purchases on or after 1 January 2017.

Frequently asked questions

Do you pay stamp duty on a second home?

Yes. In England and Northern Ireland you usually pay 5% extra SDLT on top of the standard rates when you buy a residential property for £40,000 or more and own another residential property at the end of the purchase day.

Do buy-to-let landlords pay the 5% stamp duty surcharge?

Yes. A buy-to-let is an additional residential property, so the higher rates normally apply, and non-UK residents pay a further 2%.

Can you get the 5% stamp duty surcharge back?

If you replace your main home and sell the previous one within 3 years, you can claim back the higher-rate part. For sales on or after 29 October 2018 the claim must be made within 12 months of the later of the sale or the new home's SDLT filing date.

Does the surcharge apply to Scotland and Wales?

No. Scotland charges an 8% Additional Dwelling Supplement under LBTT and Wales has separate higher LTT rates. Both are covered in our comparison guide.
