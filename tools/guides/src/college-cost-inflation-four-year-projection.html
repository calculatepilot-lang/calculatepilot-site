College Cost in 18 Years: Four-Year Projections at 3% and 5% Inflation

The sticker price today is not the number to save for. This table shows why.

→ Use the free 529 College Savings Calculator

Short answer: At 5% yearly growth, four years at an in-state public college that costs $114,000 today would cost about $274,355 when a newborn enrolls at 18; at 3% it would be about $194,077. To fund the 4% version ($230,943) from birth at a 6% return takes about $596 a month; starting at 10 takes about $1,880.

Using this wisely

  • These are projections from today's estimates, not forecasts. Growth rates have varied and recent increases have been slower.
  • Most families set a coverage target below 100% and combine savings, income, scholarships and aid.
  • Revisit the numbers every year as the actual target gets clearer.

Frequently asked questions

How much will college cost in 18 years?

At 5% yearly growth, four years at an in-state public college costing $114,000 today would be about $274,355; at 3%, about $194,077.

What inflation rate should I use for college costs?

Use a planning assumption between roughly 3% and 5% and revisit it. College prices have risen faster than general inflation over the long run, but increases have slowed recently.

How much should I save monthly for college?

For $230,943 (in-state public at 4% growth) with a 6% return, about $596 a month from birth, or about $1,880 a month starting at age 10.

Do I have to save 100% of the cost?

No. Many families target a share, such as 50% or 75%, and use the calculator's coverage setting to see the monthly amount.
