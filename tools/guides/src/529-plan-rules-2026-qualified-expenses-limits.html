529 Plan Rules for 2026: Qualified Expenses, Gift Limits and Withdrawals

What a 529 can pay for, how much you can put in without gift tax, and what happens if you withdraw for something else.

→ Use the free 529 College Savings Calculator

Short answer: In 2026 a 529 can pay up to $20,000 a year for K-12 tuition, plus college costs and recognised credential programs. You can contribute $95,000 per child at once ($190,000 for a couple) using the five-year gift election. Non-qualified withdrawals pay income tax and a 10% additional tax on the earnings.

What a 529 can pay for

  • College: tuition, fees, books and — for students enrolled at least half-time — room and board.
  • K-12 tuition: up to $20,000 a year per beneficiary in 2026 (previously $10,000). Check whether your state follows the federal treatment.
  • Credentialing: tuition, fees, books, supplies and exam costs for recognised credential programs, including some continuing education to maintain a credential.

Contribution and gift limits

Rule (2026)Single giverMarried couple (gift splitting)
Annual gift tax exclusion per child$19,000$38,000
Five-year election, one lump sum per child$95,000$190,000

With the five-year election you make no other gifts to that child during the period. Each state sets its own total balance cap for accounts.

Withdrawals that are not qualified

  • The earnings portion is subject to income tax plus a 10% additional tax.
  • Your contributions come back without tax or penalty.
  • Exceptions to the 10% tax include the beneficiary's death or disability and certain scholarships.

Frequently asked questions

Can a 529 pay for private school before college?

Yes. In 2026 up to $20,000 a year per beneficiary can go to K-12 tuition tax-free federally. State rules may differ.

How much can I put into a 529 at once?

A single giver can contribute up to $95,000 per child using the five-year election, or $190,000 for a married couple, with no other gifts to that child in the period.

What is the penalty for a non-qualified 529 withdrawal?

The earnings are taxed as income and carry a 10% additional tax. The contribution portion is not taxed.

Do these rules apply to every state?

The federal rules do. Many states have their own deductions, balance limits and treatment of K-12 withdrawals, so check your plan.
