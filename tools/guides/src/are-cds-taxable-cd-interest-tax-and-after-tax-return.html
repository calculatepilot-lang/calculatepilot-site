Are CDs Taxable? CD Interest Tax and After-Tax Return

How CD interest is taxed and how to work out what you keep.

→ Use the free CD Calculator

Short answer: CD interest is taxable income. After-tax yield = APY × (1 − tax rate), so a 4.50% APY in the 24% bracket is 3.42%.

How CD interest is taxed

  • CD interest is taxed as ordinary income at your federal rate, and often at state rate too.
  • You are usually taxed in the year the interest is credited, not only at maturity.
  • Your bank sends Form 1099-INT, normally when you earn $10 or more in a year.
  • For a CD longer than one year you generally report the interest each year, even if it is paid at maturity.
  • CDs held in an IRA are not taxed until you withdraw.

After-tax return

After-tax yield = APY × (1 − tax rate). $10,000 at 4.50% APY for 12 months earns $450:

A two-year example

$10,000 at 4.50% APY for 24 months earns $450.00 in year one and $470.25 in year two (10,450 × 0.045), $920.25 in total. In the 24% bracket the tax is $108.00 and $112.86, so you keep $699.39.

Comparing with tax-free interest

A taxable yield equal to a tax-free 4.50% is 4.50% ÷ (1 − 0.24) = 5.92% in the 24% bracket. Interest from US Treasury securities is exempt from state and local income tax, which can matter in high-tax states.

Check your answer

  • After-tax yield is always lower than the APY unless your tax rate is 0%.
  • Add state tax by using your combined rate.
  • Your tax rate is the bracket for your next dollar of income, not your average rate.

Frequently asked questions

Is CD interest taxable?

Yes. It is ordinary income, taxed in the year it is credited, and reported on Form 1099-INT.

Do I pay tax on a CD before it matures?

Generally yes. Interest is usually taxable in the year it is credited, even if you cannot withdraw it without a penalty.

How do I work out the after-tax yield?

Multiply the APY by (1 − your tax rate). At 4.50% APY and 24%, it is 3.42%.

Are CDs in an IRA taxed?

Not until you withdraw. In a traditional IRA, withdrawals are taxed as income, and in a Roth IRA, qualified withdrawals are tax-free.
