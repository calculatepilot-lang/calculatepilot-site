48 vs 60 vs 72-Month Car Loan: What You Can Afford

How loan term changes your payment, total interest and the car a fixed payment can buy.

→ Use the free Car Affordability Calculator

Short answer: A longer loan lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest. On $24,000 at 6.5%, 72 months costs $5,048 in interest against $3,320 at 48 months, a difference of $1,728.

Same loan, different terms

A $24,000 loan at 6.5% APR (the amount financed on a $30,000 car with 20% down), next to what a fixed $400 monthly payment can borrow.

What it shows

  • Going from 48 to 72 months cuts the payment from about $569 to about $403 but adds about $1,728 of interest.
  • A $400 payment supports about $6,900 more loan over 72 months than over 48 months.
  • Longer terms make a pricier car look affordable, but the extra interest and the longer time in debt are the cost.

Which term to choose

  • 48 months or less if the payment fits the 10% rule.
  • 60 months if you need a lower payment, but check the total car cost.
  • 72 months or more only with a large down payment, because you may owe more than the car is worth for longer.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 60-month car loan too long?

It is common, but it goes beyond the 4-year limit of the 20/4/10 rule and adds interest compared with 48 months.

How much more does a 72-month car loan cost?

On $24,000 at 6.5%, about $1,728 more interest than a 48-month loan.

Does a longer term let me afford a more expensive car?

On paper it does, because the payment is lower, but you pay more interest and stay in debt longer.

What term does the calculator use by default?

48 months, the strict 4-year cap. You can change it to 36, 60 or 72 months.
