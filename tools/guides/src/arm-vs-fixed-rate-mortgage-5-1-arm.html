5/1 ARM vs Fixed-Rate Mortgage: What Happens When the Rate Resets

A lower starting rate is the pitch. The reset is the risk. Here is one worked scenario.

→ Use the free Mortgage Calculator

Short answer: In an example on a $320,000 loan, a 5/1 ARM at 5.75% costs $1,867.43 a month for five years versus $2,022.62 on a 6.5% fixed, saving about $155 a month. If the rate resets to 7.75%, the payment rises to about $2,242.11, $219 above the fixed payment. The scenario is illustrative.

The scenario

$320,000 loan. A 30-year fixed at 6.5% vs a 5/1 ARM that starts at 5.75% for 5 years, then resets once to 7.75% for the remaining 25 years. These rates are examples, not quotes.

When an ARM can make sense

  • You expect to sell or refinance before the reset.
  • You can afford the payment at a higher rate, and you know the loan's rate caps.

Questions to ask the lender

  • What index and margin set the new rate?
  • What are the initial, periodic and lifetime caps?
  • When does the first adjustment happen?

Frequently asked questions

What is a 5/1 ARM?

A mortgage with a fixed rate for the first 5 years that then adjusts once a year.

How much could my payment rise after a 5/1 ARM resets?

In our example, from $1,867.43 to about $2,242.11 on a $320,000 loan if the rate goes from 5.75% to 7.75%.

Is an ARM cheaper than a fixed-rate mortgage?

For the intro period, often yes. After the reset it can cost more, depending on rate changes.

What should I check in an ARM offer?

The index and margin, the adjustment caps (initial, periodic and lifetime), and when the first adjustment happens.
