How to Calculate the Break-Even Point: Formula and Examples

The break-even formula in units and dollars, with a worked example, a profit target and what happens when price or cost moves.

→ Use the free Break-Even Calculator

Short answer: Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost per unit). Fixed $12,500, price $65, variable $24.50: contribution $40.50, so 12,500 ÷ 40.50 = 308.6, rounded up to 309 units ($20,085). A $5,000 profit target needs 433 units.

The formula

  • Contribution per unit = price − variable cost per unit
  • Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit
  • Break-even revenue = break-even units × price
  • Target-profit units = (fixed costs + target profit) ÷ contribution per unit

Worked example

Fixed costs $12,500 a month, variable cost $24.50 a unit, price $65.

StepCalculationResult
Contribution per unit$65 − $24.50$40.50
Break-even units$12,500 ÷ $40.50 = 308.6309 units (round up)
Break-even revenue309 × $65$20,085
Units for a $5,000 profit($12,500 + $5,000) ÷ $40.50 = 432.1433 units
Revenue for a $5,000 profit433 × $65$28,145

Why you round up

You cannot sell part of a unit, and 308 units would leave you slightly short of covering fixed costs, so the break-even point is the next whole number.

What happens if price or cost moves by 10%

ChangeContributionBreak-even units
Base case$40.50309
Price +10% ($71.50)$47.00266
Price −10% ($58.50)$34.00368
Variable cost +10% ($26.95)$38.05329
Variable cost −10% ($22.05)$42.95292

A 10% price change moves break-even more than a 10% cost change because price is the larger number in the contribution.

When you can never break even

If the price is at or below the variable cost, each sale loses money and no volume will cover fixed costs. Raise the price, cut variable costs, or reduce fixed costs.

Frequently asked questions

What is the break-even formula?

Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ (selling price − variable cost per unit).

How do I calculate break-even revenue?

Multiply break-even units by the selling price, or divide fixed costs by the contribution margin ratio.

Why round break-even up?

Because you cannot sell a fraction of a unit, and rounding down would leave fixed costs slightly uncovered.

What should I include in fixed costs?

Costs that do not change with volume in the period, such as rent, salaries, insurance and subscriptions.
