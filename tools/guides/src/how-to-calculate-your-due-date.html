How Do You Calculate Your Due Date?

The standard method is simple: 40 weeks from your last period. Here are the rule, tables, cycle adjustments and IVF dating.

→ Use the free Due Date Calculator

Short answer: add 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP). This is Naegele's rule. A last period that started on 1 March 2026 gives a due date of 6 December 2026. Only a small share of babies arrive on the exact day, so treat it as an estimate.

Due date = first day of LMP + 280 days
From conception: due date = conception date + 266 days

If your cycle isn't 28 days

Ovulation shifts with cycle length, so adjust by the difference from 28 days. A 30-day cycle adds 2 days; a 32-day cycle adds 4. With a last period starting 1 March 2026 and a 32-day cycle, the estimate moves from 6 December 2026 to 10 December 2026.

Dating from conception or IVF

  • Conception date: add 266 days (38 weeks).
  • IVF transfer: add 266 days and subtract the embryo's age: 263 days for a day-3 transfer, 261 for day 5, 260 for day 6. A day-5 transfer on 15 March 2026 gives 1 December 2026.

Gestational age is counted from the first day of your last period, so at conception you are already about two weeks pregnant.

How accurate is a due date?

Relatively few babies arrive exactly on their due date; most are born within a couple of weeks either side. The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) considers a first-trimester ultrasound the most accurate way to date a pregnancy, and a provider may change your due date if the scan and your LMP date differ by more than a set number of days. IVF dates are the most precise because the embryo's age is known.

Common mistakes

  • Counting from conception instead of your last period. The 280-day rule starts at the first day of the LMP.
  • Ignoring cycle length. A longer or shorter cycle moves the estimate.
  • Treating the date as exact. It is a midpoint of a range, and your care team confirms your dates.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate a due date?

Add 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of your last menstrual period. For a 28-day cycle, that is Naegele's rule.

How do you calculate a due date from conception?

Add 266 days (38 weeks) to the conception date.

How accurate is a due date?

It is an estimate. Most babies are born within a couple of weeks of it, and a first-trimester ultrasound is considered the most accurate way to date a pregnancy.

Does cycle length change the due date?

Yes. Adjust by the difference from a 28-day cycle, for example +2 days for a 30-day cycle.
