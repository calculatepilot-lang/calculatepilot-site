Target Heart Rate by Age: Chart and Zones

Heart rate zones for moderate and vigorous exercise by age, and a more personal method that uses your resting heart rate.

→ Use the free Target Heart Rate Calculator

Short answer: estimate your maximum heart rate as 220 minus your age, then aim for 50–70% of it for moderate exercise and 70–85% for vigorous exercise. A 45-year-old has a max of about 175 bpm, a moderate zone of about 88–123 bpm and a vigorous zone of about 123–149 bpm.

Max heart rate ≈ 220 − age
Target = max heart rate × intensity (50–70% moderate, 70–85% vigorous)

A more personal method: Karvonen

The Karvonen method uses your heart rate reserve (max minus resting heart rate), so it adjusts for fitness. The American College of Sports Medicine classes 40–59% of reserve as moderate and 60–89% as vigorous.

  1. A 45-year-old with a resting heart rate of 65 and a max of 175.
  2. Reserve = 175 − 65 = 110 bpm.
  3. Moderate zone (40–59%) = 65 + 110 × 0.40 to 0.59 = 109–130 bpm.
  4. Vigorous zone (60–89%) = 131–163 bpm.

Is 220 minus age accurate?

It is a population average, so an individual can be 10 or more beats off. The Tanaka (208 − 0.7 × age) and Gellish (207 − 0.7 × age) equations fit large datasets better, especially over 40. For a 45-year-old, Tanaka gives about 176 instead of 175. A supervised test is the only way to know your true maximum.

How much exercise do you need?

Adults are advised to get 150–300 minutes of moderate or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity a week. Moderate sessions build an aerobic base and are easy to repeat. Vigorous sessions give more fitness per minute but are harder to sustain. Most plans mix both. Quick habits

  • Use the talk test. If you can talk but not sing, you are probably at a moderate effort.
  • Check your resting heart rate on waking, three mornings in a row, and take the average.
  • Start with brisk walks. See how many steps burn 100 calories.

Frequently asked questions

What is my target heart rate by age?

Using 220 minus age for maximum heart rate, moderate exercise is about 50 to 70 percent and vigorous exercise about 70 to 85 percent. At 45, moderate is roughly 88 to 123 beats per minute.

How do I calculate maximum heart rate?

The common estimate is 220 minus your age. The Tanaka formula (208 minus 0.7 times age) fits large datasets better, especially after 40, and a supervised test gives a true value.

What is the Karvonen method?

It uses heart rate reserve (max minus resting heart rate): target = resting heart rate + reserve x intensity. Because it uses your resting rate, it adjusts for fitness.

What heart rate is best for fat burning?

Both moderate and vigorous exercise burn fat. What matters most is total weekly activity, so pick an intensity you can repeat consistently.

Do medicines change my target heart rate?

Yes. Beta-blockers and some other medicines lower heart rate, so these zones can be inaccurate. Ask your doctor what to use if you take heart medication or have a heart condition.
