How to Calculate Body Fat Percentage With the U.S. Navy Method

The Navy formulas for men and women, where to place the tape, and worked examples.

→ Use the free Body Fat Calculator

Short answer: The U.S. Navy method estimates body fat from circumferences. For a man, 495 ÷ (1.0324 − 0.19077 × log10(waist − neck) + 0.15456 × log10(height)) − 450, in cm. A 90 cm waist, 38 cm neck and 178 cm height give 20.1%.

The formulas (centimetres)

Men: 495 ÷ (1.0324 − 0.19077 × log10(waist − neck) + 0.15456 × log10(height)) − 450

Women: 495 ÷ (1.29579 − 0.35004 × log10(waist + hip − neck) + 0.22100 × log10(height)) − 450

If you measure in inches, multiply by 2.54 first. 35.4 in is 90 cm.

Where to measure

  1. Neck: just below the larynx.
  2. Waist: at the navel for men, at the narrowest point for women.
  3. Hips (women only): at the widest point.
  4. Height: without shoes.

Keep the tape snug without compressing the skin, and use the same spots every time.

Worked examples

PersonMeasurementsResult
ManWaist 90, neck 38, height 17820.1%
Same manWaist 10026.7%
Same manWaist 8516.4%
WomanWaist 78, hips 100, neck 32, height 16531.4%

Ten centimetres on the waist moves the result by almost seven points, so measure carefully.

Limits

The Navy method is a field estimate. Its error varies between people, and it is less reliable for very muscular builds. Use it to track a trend with the same technique, not as a replacement for a clinical scan.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Navy body fat formula?

A logarithmic equation using waist, neck and height (and hips for women) to estimate body fat.

Do I need hip measurements?

Only women. The women's formula includes the hips.

How accurate is the Navy method?

It is a field estimate with meaningful error, so use it for trends.

Can I use inches?

Convert to centimetres first by multiplying by 2.54, or use the calculator's unit toggle.
