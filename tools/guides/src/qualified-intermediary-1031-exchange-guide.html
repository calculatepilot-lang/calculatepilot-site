Qualified Intermediary for a 1031 Exchange: What It Does and How to Choose

The qualified intermediary holds your sale proceeds so you never touch the cash. Here is the role, the restrictions and a checklist before you sign.

→ Use the free 1031 Exchange Calculator

Short answer: A qualified intermediary (QI) is a third party who holds the proceeds from the sale of your property and uses them to buy the replacement property, so you never receive the cash. Your own agents and related parties cannot serve as your QI, and you should hire one before the sale closes.

What a qualified intermediary does

In a delayed 1031 exchange, you sell the relinquished property and buy the replacement later. If you received the sale proceeds, the exchange could fail and the gain would be taxable. The QI avoids that by:

  1. Taking an assignment of your contract to sell and buy.
  2. Holding the sale proceeds in an account you cannot reach.
  3. Paying the money toward the replacement property when you close on it.
  4. Giving you the paperwork to document the exchange.

Who cannot be your QI

Related parties and your agents, such as your attorney, accountant or real estate broker, are disqualified persons under the IRS rules and cannot act as your qualified intermediary.

Timing and what a QI does not do

  • The 45-day identification and 180-day exchange periods run from the day you transfer the old property. See the 45 and 180-day rules.
  • A QI does not give tax advice. Talk to a tax adviser about your own numbers.
  • If the QI causes a timing requirement to fail, the transaction may not qualify as a deferred exchange, and the gain may be taxable in the year of the sale.
  • If a QI defaults because of bankruptcy or receivership, you may be able to report the gain as payments are received under Rev. Proc. 2010-14.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • How are exchange funds held, and who has access to the account?
  • Is the money held in a segregated account in your name or pooled with other clients?
  • What insurance or bonding covers the funds?
  • What is the total fee and what does it include?
  • Who will handle your file, and how do they confirm your 45-day and 180-day dates?
  • Do they support reverse or improvement exchanges, if you may need one?

Check your numbers first

The calculator shows how much of your gain is deferred and whether your replacement property avoids boot. Run it before you talk to a QI so you know the price and debt you need to replace.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a qualified intermediary for a 1031 exchange?

For a delayed exchange, yes in practice. The proceeds must be held by a QI so you do not take possession of them.

Who cannot be a qualified intermediary?

Related parties and your agents, such as your attorney, accountant or broker, cannot serve as a qualified intermediary.

When should I hire a qualified intermediary?

Before the sale of the relinquished property closes, because the exchange documents and the account must be in place at closing.

What happens if the qualified intermediary goes bankrupt?

If a QI defaults due to bankruptcy or receivership, you may be able to report the gain as payments are received under Rev. Proc. 2010-14. Ask a tax adviser how it would apply to you.
