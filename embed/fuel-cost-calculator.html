Formula

How the cost is calculated

Miles and gallons

fuel = distance ÷ MPG
cost = fuel × price per gallon

Kilometres and litres

fuel = distance × (L/100 km) ÷ 100
cost = fuel × price per litre

Your numbers

—

One US gallon is 3.785412 litres and one mile is 1.609344 km. L/100 km = 235.215 ÷ MPG.

Improving accuracy

Why real costs differ

Fuel economy varies with driving style, terrain, load, weather and speed. Highway driving usually beats stop-and-go city driving. For the most accurate estimate use the MPG you have actually measured over a few tanks rather than the manufacturer's rating.

For a long trip with mixed roads, price each leg with its own MPG and add them up.

Worked examples

Illustrative trips, each checked by hand.

450-mile road trip

32 MPG, gas at $3.60 a gallon.

450 ÷ 32 = 14.06 gal
14.06 × 3.60 = $50.63

Same trip, thirstier vehicle

22 MPG, same price.

450 ÷ 22 = 20.45 gal
20.45 × 3.60 = $73.64
that is $23.01 more

Metric trip

500 km, 8.0 L/100 km, $1.80 a litre.

500 × 8.0 ÷ 100 = 40 L
40 × 1.80 = $72.00

Ways to reduce fuel costs

  • Drive smoothly. Hard acceleration and braking and high highway speeds all cut MPG.
  • Keep tires inflated to the recommended pressure and remove roof cargo or excess weight.
  • Combine errands into fewer trips and avoid long idling.
  • Compare prices along the route and fill up where fuel is cheapest.

Frequently asked questions

Does this account for highway vs city driving?expand_more

Use your vehicle's combined MPG for a realistic estimate, or enter the city or highway figure that matches your trip.

How do I find my car's actual MPG?expand_more

Divide the miles driven since your last full fill-up by the gallons it took to refill the tank. Tracking this over a few tanks gives a more reliable average than the rated figure. Our Gas Mileage Calculator does this.

Does this account for fuel price changes during a long trip?expand_more

No. It uses one price. For a long trip, enter the average price you expect to pay along the route.

Does driving style really affect fuel economy that much?expand_more

Yes. Aggressive acceleration and braking, high speeds and idling can reduce fuel economy noticeably compared with smooth, moderate driving, especially in stop-and-go traffic.
