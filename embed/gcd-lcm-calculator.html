    Tiling a floor

    What is the largest square tile that exactly covers a 12 × 18 floor?

    gcd(12, 18) = 6
    Tiles are 6 × 6, and (12 ÷ 6) × (18 ÷ 6) = 6 tiles
    directions_bus

    Bus timetable

    Two routes leave a station every 12 and every 18 minutes. When do they leave together again?

    lcm(12, 18) = 12 · 18 ÷ 6 = 36 minutes

    Gear teeth

    Gears with 19 and 30 teeth are coprime. How long before the same teeth meet again?

    gcd(19, 30) = 1
    lcm = 19 × 30 = 570 teeth — every tooth meets every other tooth, spreading wear evenly

    What are the GCD and LCM?

    The greatest common divisor (GCD, also called highest common factor) of two integers is the largest integer that divides both. The least common multiple (LCM) is the smallest positive integer that both divide. They are used to simplify fractions, to add fractions (the LCM of denominators), and to synchronise repeating cycles.

    Two methods

    • Prime factorisation: write each number as a product of primes. The GCD takes the smallest exponent of each shared prime; the LCM takes the largest exponent of every prime present.
    • Euclid’s algorithm: repeatedly replace (a, b) by (b, a mod b) until the remainder is 0; the last non-zero remainder is the GCD. It is far faster for large numbers, because it never needs to factorise.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does gcd(a, b) · lcm(a, b) = a · b hold for three or more numbers?expand_more

    No. The product identity is true only for two numbers. For a list you compute the GCD by repeatedly taking gcd of the running result with the next number, and the LCM likewise. For 4, 6 and 10: gcd = 2 and lcm = 60, but 2 × 60 = 120 while 4 × 6 × 10 = 240.

    What happens if one or both inputs are zero?expand_more

    gcd(a, 0) = |a| because every number divides 0. gcd(0, 0) is conventionally 0. lcm with 0 is 0 by convention. Negative inputs give the same results as their absolute values.

    Why is Euclid’s algorithm so much faster than factorisation?expand_more

    Each division at least halves the larger number every two steps, so it needs about log₂(min(a, b)) × 2 steps at most. Factorising large numbers has no known comparably fast method, which is why RSA cryptography relies on it being hard.

    What is Bézout’s identity used for?expand_more

    It guarantees integers x and y with ax + by = gcd(a, b). When gcd(a, b) = 1, x is the modular inverse of a modulo b, which is how RSA computes a private key from a public exponent.

    How do I use the GCD to simplify a fraction?expand_more

    Divide the numerator and denominator by their GCD. For 48/180, gcd = 12, so 48/180 = 4/15. If the GCD is 1 the fraction is already in lowest terms.

    What does “coprime” mean?expand_more

    Two integers are coprime (relatively prime) when their only common positive divisor is 1, i.e. gcd = 1. They need not be prime themselves: 8 and 15 are coprime. A set is pairwise coprime if every pair in it is coprime.
