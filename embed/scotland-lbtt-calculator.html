Revenue Scotland ADS 36-Month Reclaim Procedure

Under Schedule 2A of the LBTT (Scotland) Act 2013, if you buy a new main residence before selling your previous one, you pay the 8% Additional Dwelling Supplement at completion and can claim it back once the old home is sold.

Step 1: Settlement Payment

Pay the full 8% supplement with the core LBTT through your solicitor, with the return filed within 30 days of the effective date.

Step 2: 36-Month Sale

Sell your previous main residence within 36 months of the effective date of the new purchase.

Step 3: Reclaim

Submit a repayment claim to Revenue Scotland once the old home is sold — check its guidance for the claim deadline.

Rates this calculator uses

Residential (from 1 April 2021): 0% to £145,000 · 2% to £250,000 · 5% to £325,000 · 10% to £750,000 · 12% above. First-time buyers pay nothing up to £175,000. ADS is 8% of the whole price for additional dwellings of £40,000 or more (from 5 December 2024), added to the band tax. Non-residential freehold and lease premiums: 0% to £150,000 · 1% to £250,000 · 5% above (unchanged by the Scottish Budget 2026-27).

Sources: Revenue Scotland residential rates, Additional Dwelling Supplement, non-residential rates and the Scottish Budget 2026-27. Revenue Scotland, not this calculator, is the authority.

Worked examples

  • Main residence at £250,000: 2% on £105,000 = £2,100. A first-time buyer pays 2% on £75,000 = £1,500.
  • Same £300,000, three buyer types: first-time buyer £4,000 · main residence £4,600 · additional dwelling £28,600 (£4,600 + £24,000 ADS).
  • Additional dwelling at £197,000 (Revenue Scotland example): 2% on £52,000 = £1,040 + ADS 8% × £197,000 = £15,760 → £16,800.
  • Non-residential at £150,000 or below: inside the nil-rate band, so LBTT is £0.
  • Non-residential at £465,000 (Revenue Scotland rates page): £0 on the first £150,000, 1% on the next £100,000 (£1,000) and 5% on the remaining £215,000 (£10,750) = £11,750.
  • Non-residential office at £872,000 (Revenue Scotland LBTT4012): £1,000 + 5% on £622,000 (£31,100) = £32,100. The same price as a main home is £62,990, not £32,100 — the tables are not interchangeable.

Frequently asked questions

Is LBTT the same as stamp duty in England?expand_more

No. Different statute, different bands and a different surcharge. For England and Northern Ireland use the UK Stamp Duty Calculator.

How much is first-time buyer relief in Scotland?expand_more

It raises the nil-rate band from £145,000 to £175,000 — worth up to £600. There is no £500,000 price cap as in England.

What is the Additional Dwelling Supplement?expand_more

8% of the whole price, from 5 December 2024, on additional dwellings of £40,000 or more, added on top of the band tax.

Can I get the ADS back?expand_more

Yes, if the new home replaces your main residence and you sell the previous one within 36 months of buying the new one. You then claim a repayment from Revenue Scotland.

Does this cover a shop or office?expand_more

Yes — choose Non-Residential. Slices are 0% to £150,000, 1% to £250,000 and 5% above; ADS is not added. £150,000 or below is £0. Revenue Scotland's rates-page case of £465,000 is £11,750. The LBTT4012 £872,000 office is £32,100. Lease rent is not calculated.

Is this a Revenue Scotland assessment?expand_more

No. Confirm with your solicitor. This page is a planning estimate from published rates.
