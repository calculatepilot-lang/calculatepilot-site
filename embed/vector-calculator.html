    What is a vector?

    A vector has both magnitude and direction and is written by its components, such as u = (3, 4, 0). Its magnitude is |u| = √(uₓ² + u_y² + u_z²), and dividing by it gives the unit vector û = u ÷ |u|, of length 1.

    Formulas used

    • Dot product: u · v = uₓvₓ + u_yv_y + u_zv_z = |u||v| cos θ.
    • Angle: θ = arccos[(u · v) ÷ (|u||v|)], between 0° and 180°.
    • Cross product: u × v = (u_yv_z − u_zv_y, u_zvₓ − uₓv_z, uₓv_y − u_yvₓ); its length |u||v| sin θ is the area of the parallelogram they span.
    • Projection: projv u = [(u · v) ÷ |v|²] v and compv u = (u · v) ÷ |v|.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why is the dot product a scalar but the cross product a vector?expand_more

    The dot product measures how much two vectors point the same way, which is a single number. The cross product produces a new vector perpendicular to both, whose length is the area of the parallelogram they span. In 2D there is no perpendicular direction within the plane, so the cross product reduces to one number (the z-component).

    What does a zero dot product mean?expand_more

    The vectors are perpendicular (orthogonal), provided neither is the zero vector, because cos 90° = 0. A negative dot product means the angle is obtuse; a positive one means it is acute.

    What does a zero cross product mean?expand_more

    The vectors are parallel or antiparallel (or one is zero), because sin 0° = sin 180° = 0. They span no area.

    How does the right-hand rule fix the direction of the cross product?expand_more

    Point the fingers of your right hand along u, curl them toward v, and your thumb points along u × v. Reversing the order reverses the direction: v × u = −(u × v).

    What is the difference between scalar and vector projection?expand_more

    The scalar projection is the signed length of u’s shadow along v. The vector projection is that length pointing along v’s direction. The difference u − projv u is the part of u perpendicular to v.

    Why does the cross product only exist in 3D?expand_more

    The construction needs a unique direction perpendicular to two given directions, which exists only in three dimensions (and, with a different algebra, in seven). In other dimensions the wedge product generalises it.
