Formulas

How the numbers are worked out.

close

Scaling

Multiply each quantity by the ratio of the servings you want to the original servings.

new amount = old amount × (target servings ÷ original servings)
scale

Volume to weight

Convert to cups, then multiply by grams per cup for that ingredient.

grams = cups × grams per cup
percent

Baker's percentage

Each ingredient weight divided by total flour weight.

percent = ingredient g ÷ flour g × 100

Worked examples

Illustrative figures, each checked by hand. The first is the default setup.

Cookie dough for 10

A recipe for 4 scaled to 10 servings, so 2.5×.

2.5 cups flour × 2.5 = 6.25 → 6 ¼ cups
6.25 × 120 g = 750 g
1.5 sticks butter × 2.5 = 3 ¾ sticks = 426 g

Baker's percentage

750 g flour, 500 g sugar, 426 g butter.

sugar = 500 ÷ 750 = 66.7%
butter = 426 ÷ 750 = 56.8%
flour is always 100%

How to scale a recipe

  • Enter the servings the recipe makes and the servings you want.
  • List each ingredient with its quantity and unit, and choose the ingredient type so weights are right.
  • Read the scaled amounts, grams and total batch weight.
  • Adjust pan size, baking time and seasoning, since these do not scale in a straight line.

Scaling ingredients is simple, but cooking is not always linear. Very large batches, leaveners, spices and baking times may need adjusting, so taste and test as you go.

Frequently asked questions

How do I scale a recipe?expand_more

Divide the servings you want by the servings in the original recipe to get a multiplier, then multiply every ingredient by it. To go from 4 to 10 servings, multiply by 2.5.

Why is weight better than cups for baking?expand_more

A cup of flour can weigh 120 g or more than 150 g depending on how you scoop it. Weighing ingredients removes that variation, which is why bakers use grams.

What is a baker's percentage?expand_more

It expresses each ingredient as a percentage of the flour weight, which is always 100%. A dough with 700 g water to 1,000 g flour has a hydration of 70%.

How many grams are in a cup?expand_more

It depends on the ingredient: about 120 g of all-purpose flour, 200 g of granulated sugar, 227 g of butter and 237 g of water. Use the ingredient type in the tool for the right figure.

Do baking times change when I scale a recipe?expand_more

Often, yes. A larger or deeper batch takes longer, a smaller one less, and pan size changes how it browns. Use time and doneness cues rather than scaling time directly.
