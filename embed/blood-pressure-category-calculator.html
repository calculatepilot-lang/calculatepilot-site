How this calculator works

  • Average: if you enter more than one reading, the systolic and diastolic values are averaged separately, as guidelines recommend basing decisions on 2 or more readings.
  • Category: the higher of the systolic and diastolic categories, using the American Heart Association chart shown above (aligned with the 2025 AHA/ACC high blood pressure guideline).
  • Pulse pressure = systolic − diastolic. Mean arterial pressure ≈ diastolic + (systolic − diastolic) ÷ 3.

A worked example

Readings of 128/82, 132/84 and 126/80 average to 129/82 mm Hg. The systolic of 129 is "elevated", but the diastolic of 82 is in the 80–89 range, so the overall category is stage 1 hypertension. Pulse pressure is 47 mm Hg and mean arterial pressure about 98 mm Hg.

When to act

If a reading is higher than 180/120 mm Hg, the AHA advises waiting a minute and measuring again. If it's still that high and you have no symptoms, it's severe hypertension — call your healthcare professional. If you have symptoms such as chest pain, shortness of breath, back pain, numbness, weakness, vision changes or difficulty speaking, it may be a hypertensive emergency — call 911 (or your local emergency number).

These categories are for adults. Children and teens use age-based ranges, and blood pressure in pregnancy needs individual guidance from your care team. This calculator categorizes readings for awareness only — it is not a diagnosis. Blood pressure varies through the day with stress, caffeine, activity and technique, so a clinician looks at the pattern of readings over time. For home readings, sit quietly for 5 minutes with your back supported, feet flat and arm at heart level, and avoid caffeine, exercise and smoking for 30 minutes beforehand.

Frequently asked questions

Which number matters more, systolic or diastolic?expand_more

Both matter. Your category is set by whichever number falls in the higher band. After about age 50, systolic pressure becomes a stronger predictor of heart disease and stroke risk for most people.

Is one high reading a diagnosis of high blood pressure?expand_more

No. Diagnosis is based on the average of two or more readings taken on two or more occasions. A single high reading is worth rechecking, and repeated high readings are worth discussing with a clinician.

What is a normal blood pressure?expand_more

Under the AHA categories, normal is a systolic below 120 and a diastolic below 80 mm Hg.
