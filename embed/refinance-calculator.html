functionsMathematical Methodology

The quick rule of thumb divides closing costs by the monthly payment saving. This calculator also tracks the loan balances, because a longer new term lowers the payment partly by paying off principal more slowly.

Quick Break-Even (Payment Method)
Break-Even (Months) = Closing Costs ÷ (Old P&I − New P&I)

gavelWhen NOT to Refinance

A lower rate alone doesn't guarantee a good decision. Be cautious when:

  • home_pin
    You'll move before breaking even

    If you sell or refinance again before the break-even month, closing costs exceed your savings.

  • history
    You're deep into your current loan

    Years into a 30-year loan, most of each payment goes to principal. Restarting a new 30-year term puts you back at the interest-heavy start.

  • trending_up
    Switching from a fixed rate to an ARM

    An adjustable rate can reset higher after the intro period, which can erase the savings.

What breakeven point means

The breakeven point is how many months it takes for your monthly savings to cover the closing costs of refinancing. If you plan to stay in the home (or keep the loan) longer than the breakeven period, refinancing is generally worth it purely on the numbers.

If you expect to sell or pay off the loan before reaching breakeven, the closing costs may outweigh the savings — timeline is often the single biggest factor in whether refinancing makes sense.

Beyond the monthly payment

A lower monthly payment doesn't always mean less total interest — resetting to a new 30-year term after already paying down several years of a loan can sometimes increase total interest paid over time, even with a lower rate, because you're extending how long interest accrues.

Comparing total interest between the current loan's remaining term and the new loan's full term (as this calculator does) gives a more complete picture than the monthly payment difference alone. If you're still deciding on your original loan terms, the Mortgage Calculator covers that first step.

Frequently asked questions

Is refinancing worth it if I'm planning to move soon?expand_more

Generally not, if you'd move before reaching the breakeven point — the closing costs may not be recovered through monthly savings in that case.

Can refinancing increase my total interest even with a lower rate?expand_more

Yes — resetting to a longer term (e.g., back to 30 years after already paying several years) extends how long interest accrues, which can offset some or all of the benefit of a lower rate.
