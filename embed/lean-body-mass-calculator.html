How this calculator works

Lean body mass (LBM) includes muscle, bone, organs and water — everything that isn't fat tissue. Each formula estimates it from weight (W, kg) and height (H, cm):

  • Boer (1984): men 0.407W + 0.267H − 19.2 · women 0.252W + 0.473H − 48.3
  • James (1976): men 1.1W − 128(W/H)² · women 1.07W − 148(W/H)²
  • Hume (1966): men 0.32810W + 0.33929H − 29.5336 · women 0.29569W + 0.41813H − 43.2933
  • From body fat %: LBM = W × (1 − body fat ÷ 100) — the most direct option if you have a measured body fat figure.

Fat-free mass index is FFMI = LBM ÷ height (m)². The height-normalized version adds 6.1 × (1.8 − height in m), as proposed by Kouri et al. (1995) to compare people of different heights.

Why lean body mass matters

Lean body mass is a useful figure for setting protein intake targets and for tracking body composition changes during training. Two people with the same total weight can have very different lean body mass, which is part of why weight alone is a limited measure of fitness or health. Clinically, lean mass is also used for dosing some medications.

A worked example

A man weighing 80 kg at 180 cm. Boer: 0.407 × 80 + 0.267 × 180 − 19.2 = 32.6 + 48.1 − 19.2 = 61.4 kg lean mass, leaving 18.6 kg fat mass (about 23% body fat). James gives 62.7 kg and Hume 57.8 kg for the same inputs.

His FFMI is 61.4 ÷ 1.8² = 19.0; at exactly 1.8 m, the height-normalized FFMI is the same.

Limitations

Formula estimates become less precise at the extremes — very high muscle mass or very high body fat. The James formula in particular starts to fall as weight rises at high BMI, so it is unreliable above a BMI of about 35. Kouri et al. reported that FFMI values above about 25 were rare among drug-free male athletes, but FFMI calculated from a formula-based LBM carries the formula's error. DEXA scans and hydrostatic weighing measure body composition directly and are more accurate for people outside typical ranges.

Frequently asked questions

How is lean body mass different from muscle mass?expand_more

Lean body mass includes muscle, but also bone, organs, and water — it's a broader measure than muscle mass alone, which is why LBM is typically higher than an estimate of muscle tissue by itself.

Why does the formula use height as well as weight?expand_more

Height helps account for overall body frame size, improving the accuracy of the estimate compared to using weight alone.

Which lean body mass formula is most accurate?expand_more

No single formula is best for everyone. Boer is widely used and generally reasonable for typical builds; if you have a measured body fat percentage (for example from DEXA), calculating lean mass directly from it is more accurate than any height-and-weight formula.

What is FFMI?expand_more

Fat-free mass index is lean mass divided by height in metres squared — like BMI, but counting only lean tissue. It helps compare muscularity between people of different heights.
