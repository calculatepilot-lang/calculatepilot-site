Sleep stages and waking up

A night is a run of cycles of about 90 minutes, each moving through light sleep, deep sleep and REM. Waking at the end of a cycle, from light sleep, usually feels better than waking from deep sleep.

StageShare of a nightWhat happensIf you wake in it
N1 light sleepabout 5%Drifting off, muscles relax, heart rate slows.Easy to wake, feel alert
N2 core sleep45–55%Body temperature falls, memory processing.Mild grogginess
N3 deep sleep15–25%Physical recovery, growth hormone release. Mostly early in the night.Heaviest grogginess
REM sleep20–25%Vivid dreams, memory and mood processing. Longer near morning.Usually easy to wake

Formulas

How the numbers are worked out.

bedtime

Bedtime for a wake time

Count back whole cycles from the time you want to wake, then allow for falling asleep.

bedtime = wake − (cycles × 90 min) − fall-asleep time
alarm

Wake time from a bedtime

Add the time to fall asleep to your bedtime, then count forward whole cycles.

wake = bedtime + fall-asleep time + (cycles × 90 min)
hotel

Sleep you get

Whole cycles times 90 minutes. Time in bed is that plus the time to fall asleep.

sleep = cycles × 90 min

How to use the sleep calculator

  • Choose whether you know your wake time, your bedtime, or want a nap.
  • Enter the time and how long you usually take to fall asleep.
  • Pick the age group so the result is compared with the recommended hours.
  • Choose the option that fits your schedule. Five cycles (7.5 hours) suits most adults.

This is a planning tool, not medical advice. If you regularly sleep badly, snore heavily, or feel exhausted after a full night, speak to a doctor.

Frequently asked questions

How long is a sleep cycle?expand_more

About 90 minutes on average, moving through light sleep, deep sleep and REM. Individual cycles vary from about 70 to 120 minutes, and the mix shifts through the night.

How many hours of sleep do I need?expand_more

The National Sleep Foundation recommends 7 to 9 hours for adults aged 18 to 64, 8 to 10 for teens, and 7 to 8 for adults 65 and over.

Why do I wake up tired after 8 hours?expand_more

Waking during deep sleep causes sleep inertia, a heavy groggy feeling that can last for a while. Shifting the time by 15 to 30 minutes can change how you feel.

What is the best length for a nap?expand_more

A 10 to 20 minute nap gives an alertness boost with little grogginess. A 90-minute nap is a full cycle. Around 30 to 60 minutes often means waking from deep sleep.

Why include time to fall asleep?expand_more

Cycles only start once you are asleep. If you take 15 minutes to drift off, you need to be in bed 15 minutes earlier to get the same amount of sleep.
