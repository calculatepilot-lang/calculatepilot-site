    Key formulas

    • Multiplication: (a+bi)(c+di) = (ac−bd) + (ad+bc)i.
    • Division: multiply by the conjugate: z₁/z₂ = z₁z̄₂ / |z₂|².
    • Modulus: |z| = √(a²+b²). Argument: θ = atan2(b, a), in (−π, π].
    • De Moivre's theorem: [r(cos θ + i sin θ)]ⁿ = rⁿ(cos nθ + i sin nθ).
    • nth roots: ⁿ√z has n values, at angles (θ+2πk)/n for k = 0, …, n−1, each with modulus |z|^(1/n).

    Frequently asked questions

    Why does dividing complex numbers use the conjugate?expand_more

    Multiplying the denominator by its conjugate z̄ turns it into the real number |z|², eliminating i from the denominator — the complex equivalent of rationalizing a square root.

    What is the geometric meaning of multiplying complex numbers?expand_more

    Multiplication multiplies the moduli and adds the arguments — geometrically, it scales and rotates. This is exactly what De Moivre's theorem generalizes to powers and roots.

    Why does a complex number have n different nth roots?expand_more

    Because angles that differ by a full turn (2π) describe the same point, but raising to the nth power multiplies the angle by n — so n different starting angles, spaced 2π/n apart, all land on the same result.

    What is the complex conjugate used for?expand_more

    Besides division, z·z̄ = |z|² always gives a real number, which is essential for finding magnitudes, normalizing vectors, and in signal processing for computing power from a complex amplitude.

    How is Euler's formula related to polar form?expand_more

    Euler's formula e^(iθ) = cos θ + i sin θ lets polar form r(cos θ + i sin θ) be written compactly as r·e^(iθ) — the same number, and the form that makes multiplication, powers and roots most natural.
