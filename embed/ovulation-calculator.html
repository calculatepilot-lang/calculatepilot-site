How this calculator works

  • Ovulation ≈ next period − luteal phase. The luteal phase (after ovulation) is usually about 14 days, so ovulation ≈ cycle day (cycle length − 14). If you know your luteal phase from tracking, enter it.
  • Fertile window = the 5 days before ovulation plus ovulation day. Sperm can survive up to about 5 days, while the egg survives about a day. In Wilcox et al.'s study (NEJM, 1995), pregnancies only occurred from intercourse within this 6-day window, with the highest chances on the 2 days before and the day of ovulation.
  • Varying cycles: the classic calendar rule (Ogino-Knaus) marks days from (shortest cycle − 18) to (longest cycle − 11) as potentially fertile.

A worked example

A last period starting 1 September with a 28-day cycle: next period ≈ 29 September, so ovulation ≈ 15 September (cycle day 15), and the fertile window runs 10–15 September. With cycles varying from 26 to 32 days, the wider calendar window is cycle days 8–21 (8–21 September).

How reliable is this estimate?

Calendar estimates work best for regular cycles. Even then, ovulation timing varies: a follow-up study (Wilcox et al., BMJ 2000) found only about 30% of women had their fertile window entirely within days 10–17 of the cycle. Ovulation predictor kits (which detect the LH surge), basal body temperature and cervical mucus tracking give more precise, cycle-by-cycle signals.

Not for contraception on its own. Calendar-based estimates are much less reliable than other birth control methods. If you want to use fertility awareness to avoid pregnancy, learn a full method with a healthcare professional.

Frequently asked questions

How many days is the fertile window?expand_more

About six days: the five days before ovulation and the day of ovulation. The chance of conception is highest on the two days before ovulation and on ovulation day itself.

Does ovulation always happen on day 14?expand_more

No. Day 14 applies only to a 28-day cycle with a 14-day luteal phase. With a 32-day cycle, ovulation is more likely around day 18, and timing can shift from cycle to cycle.

How can I confirm ovulation?expand_more

Ovulation predictor kits detect the hormone surge 1–2 days before ovulation, and a sustained rise in basal body temperature confirms it has happened. Tracking over several cycles also shows your personal pattern.
