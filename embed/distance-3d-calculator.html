    What is 3D distance?

    The straight-line distance between (x₁, y₁, z₁) and (x₂, y₂, z₂) is d = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)² + (z₂ − z₁)²]. It follows from applying the Pythagorean theorem twice: first across the floor (Δx, Δy), then up the height (Δz).

    Point-to-plane distance

    For a plane Ax + By + Cz + D = 0 and a point (x₀, y₀, z₀), the shortest distance is |Ax₀ + By₀ + Cz₀ + D| ÷ √(A² + B² + C²). The foot of the perpendicular is the point minus the signed distance times the unit normal.

    Frequently asked questions

    How does 3D distance differ from 2D distance?expand_more

    In 2D you use √(Δx² + Δy²). In 3D a third term Δz² is added under the root. Geometrically, the 2D distance becomes one leg of a right triangle whose other leg is the height difference.

    What is the difference between Euclidean and Manhattan distance?expand_more

    Euclidean distance is the straight-line “as the crow flies” length. Manhattan distance sums the absolute coordinate differences, the length of a path that moves along the axes only, like streets in a grid. Manhattan is never shorter than Euclidean.

    What are direction cosines?expand_more

    They are the cosines of the angles the displacement makes with the x, y and z axes: cos α = Δx ÷ d, cos β = Δy ÷ d, cos γ = Δz ÷ d. They always satisfy cos²α + cos²β + cos²γ = 1 and give the unit vector along the line.

    What are the spherical coordinates of a displacement?expand_more

    The distance r = d, the azimuth θ = atan2(Δy, Δx) measured in the xy-plane, and the polar angle φ = arccos(Δz ÷ d) measured from the +z axis.

    What if the plane equation is entered as ax + by + cz = k?expand_more

    Move k to the left: enter D = −k. For example 2x − y + 2z = 4 becomes A = 2, B = −1, C = 2, D = −4.

    Does the unit matter?expand_more

    No, as long as all coordinates use the same unit. The unit box is only a label copied into the results; the arithmetic is unit-free.
