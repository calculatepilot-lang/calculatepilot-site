How this calculator works

Protein and carbs provide 4 calories per gram, while fat provides 9 calories per gram — this calculator converts your percentage split into actual gram targets.

Grams = (Calories × percent ÷ 100) ÷ calories per gram — protein & carbs = 4 kcal/g · fat = 9 kcal/g

The guideline check uses the Acceptable Macronutrient Distribution Ranges (AMDR) for adults from the Institute of Medicine's Dietary Reference Intakes, also used by the 2020–2025 Dietary Guidelines for Americans: protein 10–35%, fat 20–35% and carbohydrate 45–65% of calories. Splits outside these ranges — such as ketogenic diets — are used by some people, but are outside general population guidance.

Adjusting macro splits for different goals

A 30/30/40 split works well as a general default, but different goals often call for different ratios. Higher protein (35-40%) is common for muscle-building or fat-loss phases to help preserve lean mass. Lower carb, higher fat splits are used in specific approaches like ketogenic diets.

Protein needs are the most consistent recommendation across goals — most guidance suggests prioritizing adequate protein intake first, then adjusting the fat-to-carb ratio based on personal preference and how your body responds. For people who exercise regularly, the International Society of Sports Nutrition suggests about 1.4–2.0 g of protein per kg of body weight per day; the RDA for adults in general is 0.8 g/kg.

A worked example

At 2,400 calories/day with a 35/25/40 split (higher protein): protein target is (2400 times 0.35) divided by 4, or 210g; fat is (2400 times 0.25) divided by 9, about 67g; and carbs are (2400 times 0.40) divided by 4, or 240g.

Compare that to a standard 30/30/40 split at the same calorie level: protein drops to 180g while fat rises to 80g — the same total calories, distributed differently based on which macro is prioritized. Note that 40% carbohydrate is just below the 45% lower end of the AMDR.

Adjusting macros for different goals

Higher protein (35-40%) tends to support muscle retention during fat loss, while endurance athletes often benefit from a higher carbohydrate percentage to fuel training. Protein needs are also often better set as a direct gram target based on body weight (see our Protein Calculator) rather than a pure percentage, since protein needs don't scale purely with total calories the way fat and carbs more flexibly do.

Frequently asked questions

What macro split should I use?expand_more

A 30/30/40 (protein/fat/carb) split is a common general-purpose starting point, but ideal splits vary by goal — higher protein for muscle building, higher fat for some low-carb approaches.

What if my percentages don't add up to 100%?expand_more

Adjust the values so protein, fat, and carb percentages sum to 100% for the gram targets to match your total calorie goal exactly.

Which macro is most important to prioritize?expand_more

Most nutrition guidance prioritizes protein intake first for muscle maintenance and satiety, then allows more flexibility in balancing fat and carbohydrate based on personal preference and performance.

Do I need to hit my macros exactly every day?expand_more

Being consistently close over time matters more than hitting exact numbers daily — small day-to-day variation is normal and generally doesn't meaningfully affect results.

Should my macro percentages change if I'm trying to lose fat?expand_more

Many people increase protein percentage during fat loss to help preserve muscle mass, often to 35-40% of total calories, adjusting fat and carbs accordingly.

What are the recommended macro ranges for adults?expand_more

The Acceptable Macronutrient Distribution Ranges for adults are 10–35% of calories from protein, 20–35% from fat and 45–65% from carbohydrate.
