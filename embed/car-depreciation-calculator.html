Tax note

Market value vs tax depreciation

This tool estimates resale value. The tax depreciation used for business vehicles follows different schedules, such as the IRS MACRS tables and vehicle limits in IRS Publication 946, and is not the same thing.

Registration, sales tax, parking and repairs beyond routine maintenance are not included.

How does car depreciation work?

  • A new car loses value fastest in the first year or two, often 15% to 30%, then more slowly.
  • Depreciation depends on the make and model, mileage, condition, and demand in the used market.
  • Total cost of ownership adds loan interest, insurance, maintenance and fuel to the value you lose.
  • A slightly used car often costs less to own because the first owner has already absorbed the steepest loss.

Treat every figure here as an estimate. For a car you already own, check its current value in a used-car guide or with dealer offers.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a car depreciate in five years?expand_more

A common rule of thumb is around 40% to 60% for a new car, depending on the model. This calculator's typical curve loses about 50% over five years for a midsize SUV, but individual vehicles vary widely.

Which cars hold their value best?expand_more

Trucks and some popular SUVs tend to keep more value, while luxury sedans and many electric vehicles have lost more. Reliability, fuel prices, incentives and new model launches all move the market.

Is it cheaper to buy used?expand_more

Often, because the first owner pays for the steepest depreciation. Use the condition setting to see the effect, but include the cost of any extra maintenance an older car needs.

Does mileage affect depreciation?expand_more

Yes. Higher mileage lowers resale value and can accelerate wear. This tool uses your mileage to work out cost per mile; for a precise resale value, compare listings with similar mileage.

What is total cost of ownership?expand_more

It is everything that leaving the car in your driveway costs over the time you own it: the value it loses, loan interest, insurance, maintenance, fuel and other running costs, less nothing you get back except the resale value.
